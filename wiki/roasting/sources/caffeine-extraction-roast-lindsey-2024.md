# Caffeine Content in Filter Coffee Brews as a Function of Degree of Roast and Extraction Yield

**Source**: Lindsey, Z.R., Williams, J.R., Burgess, J.S., Moore, N.T., Splichal, P.M. *Scientific Reports* 14 (2024). PMC11586412. DOI: 10.1038/s41598-024-80385-3. Nature / Springer.
**Funding**: Summer Undergraduate Research Fellowship and Berry College Office of Undergraduate Research. No competing interests declared.
**Credibility**: **High** — PMC-indexed; peer-reviewed in Scientific Reports (Nature portfolio); HPLC caffeine quantification + refractometry (TDS/EY) + SEM porosity; water chemistry controlled (40 ppm CaCO₃); grind distribution controlled (sieved 700–900µm); 10-day degassing period; triplicate brews for each of 30 combinations. Primary limitation: 2 Ethiopian coffees only (1 natural, 1 washed); AeroPress brew only; no sensory/flavor panel.

**Note — wiki relevance**: The paper's primary research question is caffeine content (a health/consumer interest topic). The most valuable contributions to *this* wiki are the secondary findings: (1) mass loss as a practical roast degree measurement with specific thresholds; (2) CGA destruction rate by roast degree (closes the fermentation-CGA-roasting chain); (3) first sourced Aillio Bullet charge temperature data; (4) natural vs. washed Ethiopian different roast behavior under same post-FC time.

## Summary

The first paper to quantitatively compare caffeine content, extraction yield, and porosity across five degrees of roast for two Ethiopian coffees (natural and washed) on an Aillio Bullet v2 drum roaster. The central finding: under identical brewing conditions, darker roasts produce lower caffeine concentrations primarily because they have lower extraction yields (less total dissolved solids extracted). However, at identical extraction yields, darker roasts show higher caffeine concentrations — caffeine is more stable than most other extractable compounds and becomes a larger fraction of what remains. CGA concentrations drop dramatically with roast degree. Porosity increases monotonically with roast degree but cannot compensate for the mass lost to volatilization and decomposition in dark roasts.

## Key Claims

### Mass loss as a roast degree proxy

- **Extraction yield** showed maximum values for roast batches with ~12–14% roasting mass loss (typically light to medium roast), decreasing for all darker batches
- **Caffeine concentration in 10-min brews** was maximum at ~14–16% mass loss, then decreased for darker roasts
- This suggests that at >12–14% mass loss, decomposition/volatilization of extractable compounds begins to outpace the porosity increase that drives extraction
- The threshold ~12–14% mass loss (declining EY begins) and ~14–16% (declining caffeine begins) provide practical batch quality checkpoints

### Natural vs. washed different roast behavior

- Under identical recipe (same RoR design, same post-FC designation), natural Ethiopian consistently achieved **darker effective roast levels** than washed Ethiopian: higher mass loss, lower Agtron color readings, lower drop temperatures for the same R-designation
- Washed Ethiopian R0 and R1 would be characterized as "underdeveloped" by specialty coffee standards (mass losses 8.9% and 11.6% — below the ~12% threshold for full development)
- Natural Ethiopian showed a wider distribution of extraction yields (~15–21%) vs. washed (~16–20%), attributed to the wider range of effective roast levels achieved
- **Implication**: when roasting natural and washed coffees on the same roasting profile (same CT, same RoR recipe), naturals will produce darker effective roasts. Adjust CT or DT targets per coffee type.

### CGA (chlorogenic acid) — dramatic decrease with roast degree

- CGA concentrations (4-CQA and 5-CQA isomers, measured by HPLC at 333nm) **decreased dramatically and monotonically** with increasing roast degree for both coffees
- Degree of roast was the dominant variable determining CGA content, far outweighing brew time or extraction yield
- CGA continued to decrease for darker roasts even as extraction yield decreased — i.e., CGA destruction during roasting is more severe than general compound loss
- This confirms that dark roasting effectively eliminates the CGA that light roasting would preserve

### Caffeine content — the paradox

- Under identical brew conditions: caffeine is **lower in dark roasts** due to lower extraction yields
- At identical extraction yield: caffeine is **higher in dark roasts** — caffeine (as xanthine) is more thermally stable than most other extractable compounds; it makes up a larger fraction of remaining extractable material after dark roasting
- The net result is that optimal caffeine concentration in the cup is achieved at light-medium roast (mass loss ~14–16%), where extraction yield is still high and caffeine loss to sublimation is minimal
- Caffeine sublimation only becomes significant at drop temperatures >400–420°F (~205–215°C) — which is roughly medium-dark to dark roast territory for the Aillio Bullet

### Porosity mechanism

- Porosity increased from 13.6% (green) → 45.8% (darkest R7 roast) — measured by SEM elliptical mapping
- Initial increases in caffeine extraction with roast degree were attributed to higher porosity creating more extraction pathways
- At darker levels, porosity increase could not compensate for compound loss — extraction yield declined
- R0 and R1 washed Ethiopian showed lower EY than R3 — attributed to insufficient porosity (underdeveloped) inhibiting extraction kinetics

## Contradictions / Nuances vs. Existing Wiki

- **Confirms the light-medium roast bias in the decision table**: declining EY at >12-14% mass loss supports the preference for lighter roasts in the roasting overview. Darker roasts require adjusted brew parameters to achieve similar extraction.
- **Partially answers an open question in fermentation-flavor-compounds.md**: "Does light roast vs. medium roast differentially preserve or transform fermentation-derived compounds?" CGA is dramatically reduced by dark roasting — so light roast is required to preserve the CGA benefits documented in Moncayo 2025. Other fermentation compounds are not covered by this paper.
- **Brew method caveat**: All extraction data is from AeroPress (full immersion, 100°C, 15:1 ratio). V60 pour-over and espresso may show different EY vs. roast degree relationships. The qualitative patterns (darker = lower EY due to compound loss) likely hold directionally.
