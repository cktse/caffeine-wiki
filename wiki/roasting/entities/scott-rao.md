# Scott Rao

## What / Who

Scott Rao is an American specialty coffee consultant, author, and educator. He is widely recognized as the most influential practitioner authority in specialty coffee roasting. He has consulted for approximately 600 roasters worldwide.

## Relevance

Rao invented the **Development Time Ratio (DTR)** concept, introduced in "The Coffee Roaster's Companion" (2014). DTR is now one of the most widely used roasting QC tools in specialty coffee. His frameworks for smooth RoR, baked roast identification, and roast profiling underpin the conventional wisdom of the specialty roasting community. He is cited by Lindsey et al. 2024 ([[sources/caffeine-extraction-roast-lindsey-2024]]) for "insightful conversations related to roasting."

## Key Facts

- **Invented DTR** (Development Time Ratio): introduced in "The Coffee Roaster's Companion" (2014) as a normalization tool for comparing development across different batch sizes, roasters, and profiles. (source: [[sources/coffee-roasting-fundamentals-rao-2020]])
- **Books**: "The Coffee Roaster's Companion" (2014); "Coffee Roasting: Best Practices"; "The Professional Barista's Handbook"; "Water for Coffee" (with Maxwell Colonna-Dashwood)
- **~600 roasting clients consulted** worldwide; Rao claims 99% reported better results after achieving smoother RoR; fewer than 10% reached what he calls "mastery" of smooth RoR (source: [[sources/coffee-roasting-fundamentals-rao-2020]])
- **Aillio Bullet IBTS comment (2020)**: Rao stated in 2020 that the single-point IR sensor on the Bullet v2 is less accurate than the BT probe; noted that Aillio had "added a 128-point IR sensor to their new machine" — this is exactly the R2 Pro's IBTS array (source: [[sources/coffee-roasting-fundamentals-rao-2020]])
- **Personal preference**: Rao personally consumes very light roasts but explicitly states this is not a universal recommendation — roasters should only go as light as their skill allows, and under-development is a real quality risk
