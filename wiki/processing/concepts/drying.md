# Drying

## Definition

The stage after depulping/fermentation (or directly after harvest for natural) where moisture is reduced to ~10–11% for stable export. Duration and method significantly affect cup quality and shelf life.

## Why It Matters

Slow, even drying preserves fermentation-derived flavor compounds and prevents mold. Too fast = uneven drying, grassy notes. Too slow / humid = over-fermentation, fungal defects.

## Methods
- **Raised beds (African beds)** — best airflow, slow and even; gold standard for washed and honey
- **Patio drying** — concrete or asphalt; faster but less even; common in Central America
- **Mechanical dryers** — drums or conveyors; used when weather unreliable; faster, controllable
- **Shade drying** — slower; used to slow fermentation continuation in naturals

## Typical Duration
| Method | Duration |
|---|---|
| Washed | 1–3 weeks |
| Honey | 2–4 weeks |
| Natural | 3–6 weeks |
