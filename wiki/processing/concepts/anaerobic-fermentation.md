# Anaerobic Fermentation

## Definition

Fermentation conducted in an oxygen-excluded environment. In coffee, oxygen exclusion is achieved by either:
1. **Self-induced CO₂ purge (SIAF)** — sealing the container and allowing microbial and fruit metabolism to consume residual oxygen and generate CO₂; anaerobiosis builds gradually over hours
2. **CO₂ injection / modified atmosphere** — externally introducing CO₂ before fermentation begins to actively displace air; anaerobiosis is immediate

Both achieve oxygen exclusion, but via different routes with different timing and different starting microenvironments. The CO₂ injection approach provides faster and more precise oxygen displacement; the SIAF approach is simpler and more accessible to producers but has a microaerobic transition window at the start. Both have sourced academic evidence of significant quality improvement.

## Why It Matters

Borrowed from winemaking. Produces distinctive flavor compounds (liquor-like aromatics, silky body, fruity notes) not achievable in aerobic fermentation. Particularly impactful for natural (dry-processed) coffees where fermentation conditions are otherwise harder to standardize than in washed processing. Peer-reviewed evidence shows CO₂ modified atmosphere raises SCA scores by ~2 points vs. aerobic and ~4.5 points vs. no fermentation on the same variety.

## Variants

| Variant | Mechanism | CO₂ source | Duration evidence | Cup result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SIAF — Natural** | Whole cherry sealed in container; microbes + fruit metabolism consume O₂ and generate CO₂ | Self-generated | 3–7 days (Brazil, 625–1,150m) | Fruity, sweet, creamy, clean — caramel/peach/tangerine range |
| **SIAF — Washed cherry** | Density-selected washed cherries sealed; faster pH drop and microbial succession | Self-generated | 3–4 days to peak | Same profile as SIAF Natural but reached faster; narrower over-fermentation window |
| **CO₂ Modified Atmosphere** | CO₂ injected externally to displace air before sealing; immediate anaerobiosis | External CO₂ (NaHCO₃ + acetic acid) | 24–60h (Colombia, 1,740m) | Aromatic, liquor-like, silky body — wine/whisky range |
| **Anaerobic Washed** | Depulped bean in sealed tank; CO₂ builds from yeast activity | Self-generated | Varies | Cleaner vs. SIAF natural; complex, floral |
| **Carbonic Maceration** | Whole cherry in CO₂ atmosphere; intracellular fermentation within the cherry | External CO₂ | Varies | Juicy, wine-grape character, aromatic |

**On carbonic maceration vs. CO₂ injection in coffee**: In winemaking, carbonic maceration specifically triggers intracellular fermentation inside the intact fruit. In coffee, the cherry structure limits intracellular fermentation differently. "CO₂ modified atmosphere" in coffee applies the same oxygen-exclusion principle. Sensory overlap (liquor-like, aromatic, silky body) suggests they are related techniques, but they have not been formally equated in peer-reviewed coffee literature indexed here.

## SIAF: Sourced Evidence

Braga et al. (2023) conducted three SIAF processes on two Brazilian farms: 20L sealed plastic containers with one-way airlocks; no added water, inoculants, or CO₂; spontaneous fermentation of whole cherries for up to 8 days.

| Process | Variety | Altitude | Cherry prep | Best SCA score (day) | Control | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FP1 | Red Icatú | 1,150m | Unwashed | 86.08 (day 7) | 82.83 | +3.25 |
| FP2 | Red Icatú | 1,150m | Washed | **86.75 (day 3)** | 82.92 | +3.83 |
| FP3 | Red Catuaí | 625m | Washed | 84.17 (day 4) | 81.67 | +2.50 |

**Sensory profile at peak**: fruity, sweet, creamy, clean — caramel, peach, tangerine, honey, brown sugar candy, almonds, velvety body, bright acidity. This profile is distinct from CO₂ injection (see below).

**Washing effect**: pre-washing cherries before SIAF accelerates fermentation significantly — washed lots peaked 3–4 days earlier. But the window past peak is narrower: from day 6, washed lots developed "fermented" and "olive" off-flavors. Unwashed lots are more time-tolerant, peaking later with a shallower decline past peak.

**Altitude effect**: 1,150m farm was more time-tolerant and achieved higher peak scores than 625m farm. Higher altitude = smaller bacterial diversity, slower microbial succession, longer optimal window.

**SIAF forgiving vs. wet fermentation**: extended SIAF did not ruin the coffee. Even the most over-fermented samples maintained total scores above the control. Traditional wet fermentation (hours) offers no such margin.

**Mycotoxin risk**: *Fusarium* sp. was present in significant amounts (up to 32.6% of fungal sequences at FP3) throughout the SIAF process. *Fusarium* produces trichothecenes and fumonisins — both detected in commercial roasted coffee, confirming they survive roasting. While anaerobic conditions reduce *Fusarium* activity, *F. oxysporum* can survive and produce metabolites in microaerobic and anaerobic environments. The transition period at the start of SIAF (before anaerobiosis is fully established) is the highest-risk window.

**Natural vs. Pulped in SIAF (Silva 2024)**: A controlled comparison on Catiguá MG2 (Brazil, 1,048m) shows natural SIAF substantially outperforms pulped SIAF — not as a marginal difference but a structural one:

| Condition | Score range | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Natural solid-state ≥48h | 85.75–87.50 | Red/yellow fruits → rum/wine/grape at 96h |
| Natural submerged 48h | 85.30 | Banana candy, cassis, fruity |
| Pulped submerged ≥72h | 84.35–84.95 | Chocolate, rum, malt, molasses |
| Control (no fermentation) | 83.30–83.60 | Chestnut, caramel, honey |
| Pulped solid-state 24h | **78.95** | **Below specialty grade** |

The chemical explanation: natural coffee has ~2× the fermentable sugars available (the intact cherry's mucilage and pulp). More substrate → more lactic acid, ethanol, and glycerol → more complex profile and higher scores. Pulped coffee, having had much of the mucilage removed, starts with half the substrate.

**Duration decision rules (ML-derived)**: For natural solid-state SIAF, duration >36h produces specialty-grade results 93% of the time; <36h is predominantly low-scoring. For natural submerged, quality peaks at 48h then declines past 60h. For pulped, commit to ≥60h submerged or skip SIAF entirely — 24h solid-state on pulped scored below the unfermented control.

**Sourcing implication**: spontaneous SIAF without starter cultures carries mycotoxin risk that CO₂ injection does not. When sourcing SIAF lots, ask about:
- Cherry preparation (washed = faster peak, tighter window; unwashed = slower, more forgiving)
- Altitude of the farm (higher = safer, longer window, higher ceiling)
- Starter culture use (selected cultures suppress *Fusarium* and other spoilage fungi)
- Mycotoxin testing (currently not standard practice)

## CO₂ Modified Atmosphere: Sourced Evidence

Moncayo-Palacios et al. (2025) tested CO₂ modified atmosphere on natural coffee. Applied to *Coffea arabica* cv. 'Colombia' at 1,740m in El Águila, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. CO₂ generated from NaHCO₃ + 5% acetic acid; 20L CO₂ purged sealed 30L containers via one-way valve.

| Treatment | Final SCA | Sensory Descriptors |
|---|---|---|
| P0 (no fermentation) | 82.15 | Tea leaves, raw cane sugar, medium acidity |
| P1 (aerobic 24h) | 84.70 | Hazelnut, juicy, balanced |
| P2 (aerobic 48h) | 85.10 | Blueberry, intense aftertaste, spicy/citrus |
| **P3 (CO₂ 24h)** | **86.90** | Aromatic, liquor-like, silky body — highest balance |
| P4 (CO₂ 60h) | 86.50 | Silky body, whisky, liquor-like — highest fragrance/aroma |

P3 and P4 statistically significantly outperform P1 and P2 for balance; all outperform P0 for final score. Uniformity, clean cup, sweetness: 10/10/10 for all treatments.

**Mechanism**: CO₂ inhibits sucrose consumption by microorganisms → favors alcoholic and lactic fermentation pathways → preserves sucrose, chlorogenic acid, and linoleic acid; reduces elaidic acid below 10%.

**Diminishing returns past 24h**: P3 (24h) and P4 (60h) not statistically different on most attributes. 24h captures the main quality benefit.

**Safety advantage of CO₂ injection vs. SIAF**: immediate oxygen displacement before fermentation begins eliminates the microaerobic transition window where *Fusarium* and other aerobic spoilage fungi establish. This is a meaningful safety difference from spontaneous SIAF.

## SIAF vs. CO₂ Injection: Comparison

Both methods achieve significant quality gains on the same starting-point coffees. Comparing the two bodies of evidence:

| Dimension | SIAF (Braga 2023) | CO₂ Injection (Moncayo 2025) |
|---|---|---|
| SCA score gain | +2.5 to +3.8 points | +4.75 points |
| Peak scores | 84–87 | 86–87 |
| Sensory signature | Fruity, sweet, creamy, clean | Aromatic, liquor-like, silky body |
| Duration to peak | 3–7 days | 24–60 hours |
| Mycotoxin risk | Present (Fusarium survives transition) | Lower (immediate anaerobiosis) |
| Producer accessibility | High — sealed bucket only | Medium — CO₂ source required |
| Timing precision | More forgiving (especially unwashed) | Tighter window (24h is optimal) |

**Caveat**: these are different varieties, altitudes, and countries. The sensory profile difference may reflect method, terroir, variety, or altitude — a controlled head-to-head study using the same coffee has not been conducted.

## Open Questions

- Is carbonic maceration in coffee a true subcategory of anaerobic fermentation, or a distinct process with different intracellular mechanics? **Partial answer**: CO₂ injection produces similar sensory descriptors to carbonic maceration (liquor-like, aromatic, silky body) — a direct comparison study in coffee is not yet indexed in this wiki.
- How does altitude affect CO₂ injection fermentation? The Moncayo 2025 study is at 1,740m. Braga 2023 suggests altitude affects SIAF speed and ceiling (1,150m > 625m), but the comparison across injection vs. self-induced remains indirect.
- Does extended CO₂ fermentation beyond 60h generate defects? P4 (60h) shows no defects, but the upper bound was not tested.
- **Partially answered**: How do self-induced CO₂ (SIAF) and externally injected CO₂ compare? Both produce ~3–4 SCA point gains, but sensory signatures differ — SIAF produces fruity/sweet/creamy; CO₂ injection produces liquor-like/silky. Whether this is a method difference or variety/altitude/origin difference requires a controlled head-to-head study.
- What is the mycotoxin incidence rate in finished SIAF lots (post-roasting) under real commercial conditions? The Braga 2023 study identifies the presence of *Fusarium* during fermentation, but does not measure mycotoxin levels in the final roasted coffee.
