# K72 Washed

## Definition

**K72 washed** is a **washed** process with an extended, controlled fermentation stage — the "72" denotes an approximately **72-hour** fermentation (often described as staged, e.g. successive ~24-hour cycles, sometimes sealed/low-oxygen) applied to depulped coffee before the mucilage is washed off and the parchment is dried. The aim is to build aromatic complexity and floral/sweet intensity *while keeping the clean cup and reduced ferment-artifact risk of a washed coffee* — i.e. more aromatic lift than a standard 12–36 h washed ferment, but cleaner and less "boozy" than a natural or whole-cherry anaerobic.

It is associated with Colombian producers ("K"-series protocols) and has been adopted in **Yunnan, China** ([[../varietals/entities/china-yunnan]]).

## Why It Matters (in this domain)

- **Position on the ferment spectrum:** sits between conventional washed and sealed-tank/anaerobic methods — longer, more deliberate fermentation than classic washed, but still washed (mucilage removed, clean parchment dry). Relate to [[concepts/fermentation]] and [[concepts/anaerobic-fermentation]].
- **Sensory expectation:** elevated florals, sweetness, and aromatic complexity over baseline washed, without the heavy fermented/winey character of naturals or whole-cherry anaerobics. This makes it a **good fit for CK's "amplify sweetness, avoid winey/fermentation artifacts" preference** — the aromatic-sweetness pathway ([[../shared/sweetness-perception]]) is engaged without the ferment-artifact downside.
- **Roasting/brewing implication:** treat as a clean washed with elevated aromatics — light-to-light-medium roast to preserve florals; bright/clarity-forward brew with full extraction for sweetness. Worked example: [[../brewing/sources/yunnan-purple-caturra-k72-label]].

## Terminology caution

"K72" is exactly the kind of **producer-specific, non-standardized label** that Kornman warns about ([[concepts/processing-terminology]]): the number implies a duration but the actual protocol (staged vs. continuous, sealed vs. open, inoculated vs. spontaneous, temperature) varies by producer and is rarely disclosed. Kornman's guidance applies — **ask what equipment, what coffee state, and what the fermentation actually was**, rather than trusting the name. All fermentation is technically anaerobic, so "K72" tells you a target duration, not a mechanism.
