# Mucilage

## Definition

The sticky, pectin-rich layer surrounding the parchment of the coffee seed, located between the seed and the fruit skin. Also called the "pulp" in some contexts (though technically distinct from the outer skin/pulp).

## Why It Matters

The amount of mucilage left on the bean during drying defines the honey process spectrum (white → yellow → red → black honey). Mucilage contains fermentable sugars that directly influence sweetness and body in the final cup.

## Structure
```
[Outer Skin] → [Fruit Flesh / Pulp] → [Mucilage] → [Parchment] → [Silver Skin] → [Seed]
```

## Honey Level Reference
| Honey Level | Mucilage Retained | Drying Color | Cup Character |
|---|---|---|---|
| White | ~20% | Light | Clean, mild sweetness |
| Yellow | ~50% | Golden | Balanced |
| Red | ~75% | Amber-red | Rich sweetness |
| Black | ~100% | Dark brown | Full sweetness, fruit-forward |
