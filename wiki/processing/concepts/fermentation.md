# Fermentation

## Definition

The microbial breakdown of sugars and organic compounds in the coffee cherry or mucilage. Produces acids, esters, and alcohols that become precursors to roasted flavor compounds. Fermentation occurs across all processing methods — in natural processing it happens during or before the drying phase; in washed processing it occurs in water tanks after depulping; in honey processing it occurs during drying with mucilage retained.

## Why It Matters

Fermentation is the stage where most defects can occur — and also where most flavor differentiation happens. Controlled fermentation (particularly CO₂ modified atmosphere) has been shown to lift final SCA scores by 4+ points vs. no fermentation on the same variety at the same altitude. The oxygen environment is the primary lever.

## Aerobic vs. CO₂ Modified Atmosphere

A peer-reviewed study (Moncayo-Palacios et al. 2025, PMC12767571) applied five treatments to natural *C. arabica* cv. 'Colombia' at 1,740m in Valle del Cauca, Colombia:

| Treatment | O₂ access | Duration | Final SCA Score |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 (control) | Ambient | None | 82.15 |
| P1 (aerobic) | Open basket | 24h | 84.70 |
| P2 (aerobic) | Open basket | 48h | 85.10 |
| P3 (CO₂ atmosphere) | Sealed, CO₂ purged | 24h | **86.90** |
| P4 (CO₂ atmosphere) | Sealed, CO₂ purged | 60h | 86.50 |

All scored ≥80 SCA (specialty grade). No defects — uniformity, clean cup, sweetness all 10/10 across all five treatments.

## Biochemical Mechanism

**Aerobic fermentation** (O₂ present):
- Sugars break down rapidly via oxidative microbial activity
- Risk pathway: sucrose → butyric and propionic acids (off-flavors at high concentration)
- Chlorogenic acid degrades via thermal isomerization and oxidation during prolonged air exposure
- Elaidic acid tends to remain high (>10%) — a negative quality marker

**CO₂ modified atmosphere** (O₂ excluded):
- CO₂ inhibits sucrose consumption by microorganisms → residual sucrose preserved → sweetness maintained
- CO₂ protects chlorogenic acid from oxidative degradation; may promote it via CO₂-favored yeast activity (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* associated with CGA increase)
- CO₂ selectively suppresses lactic and acetic acid bacteria → better acidity control
- Favors alcoholic and lactic fermentation pathways → complex aromatics (liquor-like, fruity, aromatic)
- Elaidic acid suppressed to <10%

## Key Quality Compounds in Green Coffee

| Compound | Role | Direction | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sucrose** | Primary fermentation substrate; residual = sweetness | Higher residual = better | CO₂ slows consumption |
| **Chlorogenic acid (CGA)** | Major phenolic antioxidant; becomes CGA-derivative precursors during roasting | Higher = better (in green) | Degraded by aerobic exposure; preserved by CO₂ |
| **Linoleic acid** | Unsaturated FA; ~40–45% of total FA in arabica; positively correlated with SCA score | Higher = better | Highest in P2, P3, P4 |
| **Elaidic acid** | Trans isomer of oleic acid; reduces perceived acidity; negatively correlated with SCA | Lower = better (target <10%) | P0 and P1 >10%; CO₂ reduces below 10% |
| **Stearic + arachidic acids** | Saturated FAs; negatively correlated with sensory quality | Lower = better | No significant treatment difference |

**Roasting implication**: Chlorogenic acid degrades substantially during roasting — lighter roasts retain more. Higher pre-roast CGA from CO₂ fermentation means more survives into the cup. This links fermentation choice to roasting strategy.

## Key Variables

- **Oxygen access** — the primary lever; CO₂/anaerobic environment is substantially better for natural coffee than aerobic
- **Duration** — CO₂ 24h (P3) vs. 60h (P4): diminishing returns after 24h; P3 higher on balance/acidity; P4 higher on fragrance/aroma. Aerobic 48h (P2) develops blueberry/spicy profile vs. 24h hazelnut/juicy — time matters even aerobically.
- **Temperature** — warmer = faster fermentation; study conducted at 22–29°C ambient
- **Substrate** — whole cherry (natural) vs. depulped (washed/honey) vs. mucilage-only — changes which compounds are available to microorganisms
- **Cherry preparation (washing)** — pre-washing cherries before sealed anaerobic fermentation accelerates microbial succession and pH drop, causing peak sensory quality to be reached significantly earlier (3–4 days washed vs. 7 days unwashed in SIAF) and over-fermentation window to be narrower. Source: [[sources/siaf-braga-2023]]
- **Altitude** — higher altitude correlates with smaller bacterial diversity, slower microbial succession, longer optimal fermentation window, and higher ceiling scores. 1,150m vs. 625m showed meaningful differences in SIAF timing and peak scores. Source: [[sources/siaf-braga-2023]]
- **Cherry integrity (natural vs. pulped)** — keeping the cherry intact (natural) preserves ~2× more fermentable sugars vs. pulped (fructose: 82 vs. 37 mg/g; sucrose: 60 vs. 29 mg/g). In SIAF, this translates to substantially more lactic acid (~80 vs. ~60 mg/g at 96h) and dramatically more ethanol (62 vs. ~10 mg/g) in the final green bean — driving more complex sensory profiles and higher SCA scores. Source: [[sources/siaf-catigua-mg2-silva-2024]]
- **Solid-state vs. submerged** — in SIAF, fermenting without added water (solid-state) produces 2× more ethanol than the same fermentation with 30% water added (submerged), because water acts as a solvent that dilutes ethanol in the medium. Solid-state is optimal for natural coffee. Submerged has different dynamics — best results for pulped coffee at ≥60h. Source: [[sources/siaf-catigua-mg2-silva-2024]]

## Sensory Descriptors by Fermentation Method (Natural Processing)

| Treatment | Primary Descriptors |
|---|---|
| No fermentation (P0) | Tea leaves, raw cane sugar, medium acidity |
| Aerobic 24h (P1) | Hazelnut, juicy, balanced |
| Aerobic 48h (P2) | Blueberry, intense aftertaste, spicy/citrus |
| CO₂ 24h (P3) | Aromatic, liquor-like, silky body, complex/fruity |
| CO₂ 60h (P4) | Silky body, whisky, liquor-like |

## Scope Note

CO₂ modified atmosphere findings (Moncayo 2025) apply to natural processing on one introgressed Colombian variety at 1,740m. SIAF findings (Braga 2023; Silva 2024) are from Brazilian introgressed varieties at 625–1,150m. Silva 2024 adds a direct natural vs. pulped SIAF comparison: natural SIAF produces substantially higher scores and more complex profiles than pulped SIAF, with the sugar substrate difference (2×) as the chemical explanation. Washed fermentation (depulped beans in water tanks) involves a different substrate and duration convention — cross-apply these natural-processing findings to washed cautiously. Cross-applying from low-altitude Brazil to high-altitude Ethiopia or Colombia also requires caution.

## Open Questions

- What yeasts are dominant in washed fermentation vs. natural? (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae* linked to CGA increase in CO₂ environments; full microbial community not characterized in [[sources/fermentation-natural-coffee-moncayo-2025]])
- At what fermentation duration / temperature does natural processing cross from complexity into defect ("over-fermented")?
- Do CO₂ atmosphere findings generalize from cv. 'Colombia' (introgressed Catimor-adjacent) to Ethiopian landraces, Typica, or Bourbon varietals?
- How does altitude affect CO₂ fermentation speed and flavor output? The study is at 1,740m — would results differ at 1,200m or 2,000m?
- In washed processing, does CO₂ modified atmosphere during tank fermentation produce analogous benefits to the natural coffee findings here?
