# Impact of Coffee Cherry Fermentation Methods on the Quality Attributes of Dry‐Processed Coffee

**Authors**: Moncayo-Palacios MF, Muñoz-Carvajal VH, Largo-Avila E, Suárez-Rodríguez CH, Garzón-García AM
**Published**: The Scientific World Journal, 2025 Dec 29 (PMCID: PMC12767571)
**DOI**: 10.1155/tswj/4278424
**Credibility**: Highest tier — peer-reviewed, PubMed-indexed; UHPLC + GC + HPLC chemical analysis; SCA sensory protocol with 3 certified Q-graders; controlled experimental design on-farm.

## Summary

Compares five pre-drying fermentation treatments applied to natural (dry-processed) *Coffea arabica* cv. 'Colombia' cherries at 1,740m in El Águila, Valle del Cauca, Colombia. CO₂ modified atmosphere fermentation (24h and 60h) outperformed aerobic fermentation (24h and 48h) and the unfermented control on all sensory metrics. CO₂ inhibits sucrose consumption, preserves chlorogenic acid, reduces elaidic acid (a quality-reducing fatty acid), and promotes linoleic acid retention — all correlated with higher SCA scores. P3 (CO₂ 24h) scored 86.90; P4 (CO₂ 60h) scored 86.50 — both in the upper specialty range.

## Five Treatments

| Code | Method | Duration |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Control — no fermentation | — |
| P1 | Aerobic (baskets open to air) | 24h |
| P2 | Aerobic (baskets open to air) | 48h |
| P3 | CO₂ modified atmosphere (sealed containers, CO₂ injected) | 24h |
| P4 | CO₂ modified atmosphere (sealed containers, CO₂ injected) | 60h |

CO₂ generated in situ from NaHCO₃ + acetic acid reaction (20L CO₂); displaced air via one-way valve at container top. All treatments followed by solar drying to 10–12% moisture wb (213h total, 24–42°C, RH 30–55%).

## Key Claims

- CO₂ inhibits microbial sucrose consumption → more residual sugar → sweetness preserved
- CO₂ preserves/promotes chlorogenic acid; aerobic exposure (P1/P2) degrades it via thermal and oxidative processes
- Elaidic acid (trans isomer of oleic acid) >10% in P0 and P1; CO₂ treatments reduce to <10% — high elaidic acid negatively correlates with perceived acidity and final score
- Linoleic acid highest in P2, P3, P4; positively correlated with sensory quality; normally ~40–45% of total fatty acids in arabica
- All five treatments scored ≥80 SCA (specialty grade); uniformity, clean cup, sweetness all 10/10 — no defect generation in any treatment
- CO₂ 24h vs. 60h: P3 marginally higher balance (8.20 vs. 8.15); P4 higher fragrance/aroma (8.30 vs. 8.15); not significantly different from each other in most attributes
- Aerobic 48h (P2) develops blueberry, intense aftertaste, spicy/citrus character — highest linoleic acid among aerobic treatments; interesting profile but lower overall balance

## Sensory Descriptors

| Treatment | Primary Descriptors |
|---|---|
| P0 (no fermentation) | Tea leaves, raw cane sugar, medium acidity |
| P1 (aerobic 24h) | Hazelnut, juicy, balanced |
| P2 (aerobic 48h) | Blueberry, intense aftertaste, spicy/citrus, pleasant bitter |
| P3 (CO₂ 24h) | Aromatic, liquor-like, silky body, complex/fruity |
| P4 (CO₂ 60h) | Silky body, whisky, liquor-like (more bitter tendency) |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Study uses only cv. 'Colombia' (an introgressed Catimor-adjacent variety); whether results generalize to Ethiopian landraces, Typica, Bourbon, or Kenyan SL varieties is unconfirmed
- CO₂ was generated externally (NaHCO₃ + acid reaction); differs from commercial-scale CO₂ injection tanks or "self-induced anaerobic" where CO₂ accumulates from yeast activity — results may not transfer directly to different-scale operations
- The study does not characterize specific microbial populations; which organisms drive the CO₂ advantage is not established by this study alone
- Comparison with washed fermentation not included — this study is dry-processing only
- Single growing season and single farm; variability across seasons or farms not assessed
