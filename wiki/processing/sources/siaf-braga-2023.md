# Study on Coffee Quality Improvement by Self-Induced Anaerobic Fermentation

**Authors**: Ana Valéria Ulhano Braga, Márcio André Miranda, Hiroshi Aoyama, Flávio Luís Schmidt (University of Campinas / UNICAMP, Brazil)
**Journal**: Food Research International, Vol. 165, 2023, Article 112528
**DOI**: 10.1016/j.foodres.2023.112528
**Published**: 2023-02-03
**Funded by**: FAPESP (São Paulo Research Foundation); farms: Daterra Coffee and Fazenda Tozan do Brasil
**Credibility**: **High** — peer-reviewed in a well-established Elsevier food science journal; 3 certified Q-graders; high-throughput sequencing (16S rRNA + ITS); enzyme activity assays; rigorous statistical analysis. Real on-farm conditions. Primary limitation: only two Brazilian farms, two arabica varieties (Red Icatú and Red Catuaí), at lower-than-ideal specialty altitudes (625m and 1,150m) — generalizability to high-altitude Ethiopian or Central American coffees unclear.

## Summary

Three SIAF (Self-Induced Anaerobic Fermentation) processes were evaluated on Brazilian farms for up to 8 days, using 20L sealed containers with one-way airlocks. Sensory improvement was consistent across all three processes (+2.5 to +3.83 SCA points). Pre-washing cherries before fermentation accelerated microbial succession and reduced the time to peak sensory score, but also narrowed the over-fermentation window. A mycotoxin risk from *Fusarium* sp. was identified. Microbial community and fungal diversity were strongly influenced by farm location (terroir).

## What is SIAF

SIAF is the self-induced variant of anaerobic fermentation: coffee cherries are sealed in a container and **no CO₂ is added externally**. Residual oxygen is consumed by microorganisms and fruit metabolism; CO₂ builds up from their activity, creating anaerobic conditions spontaneously. This contrasts with CO₂ Modified Atmosphere fermentation (Moncayo 2025), where CO₂ is externally injected before sealing to achieve immediate anaerobiosis. See [[concepts/anaerobic-fermentation]].

## Key Findings

### Sensory Score Improvement

| Process | Variety | Farm altitude | Washing | Best score (day) | Control score | Gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FP1 | Red Icatú | 1,150m | No | 86.08 (day 7) | 82.83 | +3.25 |
| FP2 | Red Icatú | 1,150m | Yes (density) | **86.75 (day 3)** | 82.92 | +3.83 |
| FP3 | Red Catuaí | 625m | Yes (density) | 84.17 (day 4) | 81.67 | +2.50 |

All three lots started in specialty range (≥80 SCA). SIAF significantly lifted all three.

### Sensory Profile at Peak (what SIAF actually tastes like)

**FP1 day 7 (86.08)**: caramel, peach, orange, very soft, buttery, fruity (apple), brown sugar candy, tangerine, hibiscus, mango, clean, long and sweet aftertaste, citric/malic acidity

**FP2 day 3 (86.75)**: honey, very soft, delicate acidity, creamy, sweet, almonds, tangerine, juicy, brown sugar candy, velvety body, clean and pleasant finish, nuts, hazelnut, grapefruit, bright acidity, lemon, caramel

Pattern: **fruity, sweet, creamy, clean**. This is notably different from CO₂ modified atmosphere injection (Moncayo 2025, [[sources/fermentation-natural-coffee-moncayo-2025]]), which produces "aromatic, liquor-like, silky body" descriptors. Both methods achieve similar score gains (~3–4 points over control), but with distinct sensory signatures.

### Washing Before SIAF: Faster Fermentation, Tighter Window

Pre-washing cherries before placing in the fermentation container significantly accelerates the process:
- Washed lots (FP2, FP3) achieved best sensory scores at days 3–4 vs. day 7 for unwashed (FP1)
- Past the peak (from day 6 onward), washed lots developed "fermented" and "olive" off-flavors indicating over-fermentation
- Unwashed lot (FP1) was more time-tolerant — less steep decline past peak

**Practical implication for sourcing**: a producer using washed cherries in SIAF needs more precise timing control. A producer using unwashed cherries has a longer window but a slower, later peak. Ask producers about their cherry preparation protocol when sourcing SIAF lots.

### Altitude Interaction

- 1,150m farm (FP1, FP2): slower fermentation, more time-tolerant, higher peak scores (86+)
- 625m farm (FP3): faster fermentation, earlier peak, lower peak score (84.17), lower starting score (81.67)
- Higher altitude → smaller bacterial diversity, slower microbial succession, longer optimal fermentation window
- Higher altitude also correlated with higher starting and peak scores in this study

**Sourcing implication**: SIAF coffees from higher altitudes should be expected to have longer fermentation windows and potentially higher ceiling scores. Low-altitude SIAF may peak earlier and lower.

### Over-Fermentation: Risk and Tolerance

SIAF is significantly more forgiving than traditional wet fermentation:
- Extended fermentation (past peak) did not ruin the coffee — all samples maintained total scores above control even at day 8
- Traditional wet fermentation "lasts just a few hours, which can rapidly compromise the quality, if not well conducted"
- SIAF's anaerobic environment prevents aerobic spoilage; time errors produce off-flavors rather than defective lots

This forgiving window is a major practical advantage for producers.

### Mycotoxin Risk (Safety Concern)

*Fusarium* sp. was identified in significant amounts throughout the process, particularly in FP3:
- FP3_0 (control, pre-fermentation): 32.6% of fungal sequences
- FP3_4 (day 4, best sensory day): 20.8% of fungal sequences

*Fusarium* sp. produces **trichothecenes and fumonisins** — both have been detected in commercial roasted coffee, confirming they are **not fully degraded by roasting**. While the anaerobic environment limits mycotoxin production (most Fusarium strains require aerobic conditions), *Fusarium oxysporum* can develop under microaerobic or anaerobic conditions and produce metabolites accordingly.

**Sourcing implication**: Spontaneous SIAF (no starter cultures) carries a documented mycotoxin risk that external CO₂ fermentation does not. This risk appears higher at lower altitudes and in environments with higher ambient Fusarium load. When sourcing SIAF lots, ask about:
- Whether starter cultures were used (selected starters suppress Fusarium)
- Farm altitude and environment (higher altitude = lower ambient Fusarium)
- Whether mycotoxin testing was performed

The CO₂ injection method (Moncayo 2025) achieves immediate anaerobiosis before fermentation begins, eliminating the microaerobic window where Fusarium develops — a potential safety advantage over SIAF.

### Terroir Influence

Fungal community composition was more strongly influenced by farm location than by the washing protocol. Each farm's ambient microbiota creates a distinct fungal signature that contributes to the final cup profile — consistent with the terroir concept ([[varietals/concepts/terroir]]). This provides a mechanistic basis for why the same fermentation method produces different flavor profiles at different origins.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- This study uses unwashed whole cherries (SIAF natural) and washed then sealed (SIAF washed-but-whole-cherry). These are distinct from anaerobic washed (depulped + sealed). The Moncayo 2025 paper uses whole cherries with injected CO₂. These methods aren't directly comparable in a controlled way — the sensory profile differences could be due to O₂ exclusion speed, cherry state, variety, or altitude.
- Both SIAF (this study) and CO₂ injection (Moncayo 2025) achieve ~3–4 SCA point gains over control, but the sensory signatures differ. Whether this is a method-level difference or a variety/origin/altitude difference cannot be determined without a controlled head-to-head study.
- Study varieties (Red Icatú, Red Catuaí) are Brazilian commercial varieties; neither is a high-end specialty variety. Whether SIAF applied to Ethiopian landraces or Bourbon varietals would produce the same or different results is unknown.
