# Enhancing Sensory Quality of Coffee: The Impact of Fermentation Techniques on Coffea arabica cv. Catiguá MG2

**Authors**: Lívia C F Silva, Paulo V R Pereira, Marcelo A D da Cruz, et al. (8 authors)
**Journal**: *Foods* (MDPI), Special Issue: Coffee and Coffee Byproducts Vol. II
**Published**: 2024-02-21
**PMC**: PMC10931400
**DOI**: 10.3390/foods13050653
**Credibility**: High — peer-reviewed, PMC-indexed; 5 certified Q-Graders; HPLC biochemical analysis (12 compounds: 7 acids, 2 alcohols, 3 sugars); multi-agency Brazilian research funding (FAPESP + FAPEMIG + CNPq + CAPES). *Foods* (MDPI) is a lower-impact venue than *Food Research International* (Elsevier), but experimental rigor is comparable to Braga 2023. Multi-agency funding is a positive quality signal.

## Summary

Controlled SIAF experiment on Catiguá MG2 — an introgressed CLR-resistant Brazilian variety (Timor Hybrid UFV 440-10 × Catuaí Amarelo IAC 86; 2021 Coffee of the Year champion, natural category) at Santiago Farm, Alto Paranaíba, Minas Gerais, Brazil (altitude 1,048m). Four conditions × five timepoints (0, 24, 48, 72, 96h) plus controls: HPLC measured sugars, organic acids, and alcohols; sensory evaluation by 5 certified Q-Graders per SCA protocol; all roasted to medium (Agtron; 187–195°C, ~10 min).

Primary contributions to this wiki: (1) first direct natural vs. pulped SIAF comparison; (2) compound-to-flavor mapping with HPLC evidence; (3) ML-derived duration decision rules; (4) confirms SIAF quality gains on a different variety and location than Braga 2023.

## Key Claims

|---|---|---|

| Natural submerged 24h | 83.85 | Honey, caramel, molasses, chocolate, chestnut, alcoholic |
| Natural submerged 96h | 83.75 | Brown sugar, chocolate, chestnut, caramel, red and yellow fruits, floral and alcoholic |
| Pulped solid-state 72h | 83.70 | Chocolate, chestnut, peanut, caramel, malt, fruity |
| **Control pulped** | **83.60** | **Caramel, chestnut, honey, almond, brown sugar** |
| Pulped submerged 96h | 83.50 | Cereal, honey, chestnut, caramel, orange, black tea, raspberry, berry, malic acidity |
| Natural solid-state 24h | 83.35 | Chestnut, caramel, honey, wine, fruity |
| **Control natural** | **83.30** | **Chestnut, cocoa, molasses, brown sugar candy, peach, almond, caramel, ripe fruits** |

All treatments except pulped solid-state 24h qualified as specialty (≥80 SCA). Natural SIAF consistently outscores pulped SIAF.

### Decision Rules (ML decision trees, 82–86% accuracy)

**Natural coffee** (threshold ≥84.75 = "high"):
- Duration **<36h** → 84.6% probability of low score
- Duration **>36h + solid-state** → 93% probability of score ≥84.75
- Duration **>36h + submerged + ≤60h** → high; **>60h submerged** → score declines

**Pulped coffee** (threshold ≥83.25 = "high"):
- Duration **<60h** → mostly low scores
- Duration **>60h + submerged** → 80% probability of high score
- Duration **>60h + solid-state + >84h** → high score; shorter solid-state → low

### Biochemical Compound → Flavor Mapping

| Compound | Peak amount (natural solid-state 96h) | Flavor contribution |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactic acid** | ~80 mg/g | Smooth, rounded, milky/creamy/yogurt-like; coconut at high concentration; most abundant acid in all treatments |
| **Ethanol** | 62 mg/g (natural SS) — 6× more than pulped | Alcoholic notes; body/viscosity enhancer; solvent for volatile aromatics; drives "rum/wine" character at 96h |
| **Glycerol** | Elevated in natural only | Mouthfeel, creaminess; stabilizes aroma retention; participates in Maillard during roasting |
| **Malic acid** | Present at t=0; consumed by ~48h | Apple, green fruit, tartaric acidity — characteristic of early-fermentation and short treatments |
| **Citric acid** | Produced in early aerobic phase (0–24h Krebs cycle) | Citrus, lemon/orange — part of the aerobic-to-anaerobic transition |
| **Acetic acid** | 6.18 mg/g (natural) vs. 2.62 mg/g (pulped) at 96h | Fruity acidity when moderate; vinegary off-note if excessive — higher in natural at late stages |
| **Succinic acid** | Small quantities | Contributes pleasant tartness and complexity |
| **Propionic / butyric acid** | **Not detected** | Would indicate spoilage; their absence is a quality indicator |

**Why natural >> pulped**: natural coffee starts with ~2× the sugars available for fermentation (fructose: 82.3 vs. 37.03 mg/g; sucrose: 59.75 vs. 29.40 mg/g). More substrate → more microbial activity → dramatically more lactic acid and ethanol → more complex sensory profile and higher scores.

**Why solid-state >> submerged (natural)**: water addition in submerged acts as solvent, diluting ethanol in the fermentation medium (natural submerged = 31.3 mg/g ethanol vs. solid-state 62.05 mg/g — exactly 2×). Water's high heat capacity also causes larger temperature fluctuations (day/night swings), altering microbial dynamics.

### Flavor Profile Evolution Over Time (natural solid-state)

| Phase | Duration | Sensory character |
|---|---|---|
| Early | 0–24h | Chestnut, caramel, honey — close to unfermented baseline |
| Mid | 48–72h | Red/yellow fruits, chocolate, blueberry, spice, floral |
| Late | 96h | Rum, grape, wine, red fruits, dried fruits — most complex but most alcoholic |

### Variety and Altitude Context

- **Catiguá MG2**: introgressed (Timor Hybrid UFV 440-10 × Catuaí Amarelo IAC 86); CLR-resistant; EPAMIG breeding program, Minas Gerais; 2021 Coffee of the Year champion in natural category
- **Altitude**: 1,048m — lower than Braga 2023 (625–1,150m) and Moncayo 2025 (1,740m). Scores still highly competitive.
- **SIAF peak score** (87.50) consistent with Braga 2023 peak (86.75 at 1,150m), suggesting SIAF quality ceiling is relatively robust across varieties and altitudes

### Roasting Note

All samples roasted to **medium** (Agtron; ~187–195°C, 10 min). Results are medium-roast specific. Light roast behavior of these fermentation compounds was not tested.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Natural 96h has the highest score (87.50) but the most alcoholic profile (rum, wine, dried fruits). The 48h profile (fruit/chocolate/floral) may be more commercially versatile even at a slightly lower score. Duration choice involves a tradeoff between peak score and cup style.
- Pulped solid-state 24h fell below specialty grade — the only treatment to do so. Short SIAF on pulped may be worse than no fermentation, suggesting a go/no-go threshold for producers: if doing SIAF on pulped, commit to ≥60h submerged or don't bother.
- Results from single variety (introgressed; CLR-resistant), single altitude (1,048m), single farm, single season. Cross-apply to Ethiopian landraces, Bourbon varieties, or high-altitude origins with caution — variety and altitude both affect microbial community and fermentation dynamics.
