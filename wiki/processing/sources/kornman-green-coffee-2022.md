# Green Coffee: A Guide for Roasters and Buyers — Excerpt on Processing

**Author**: Chris Kornman, quality specialist; Roast Magazine
**ISBN**: 978-0-9987717-4-8
**Publisher**: Roast Magazine
**Published**: 2022-03-15
**Credibility**: High-Medium — respected specialty coffee trade publication; author has direct field experience at origin (Brazil, Ethiopia, etc.) and is an industry quality specialist. Practitioner handbook tier: above commercial blog, below peer-reviewed. No original chemical analysis; authority comes from field experience and industry expertise.

## Summary

A processing overview chapter from the leading green coffee buyer/roaster reference. Primary contribution to this wiki: a clear-eyed critique of processing terminology — arguing that "natural," "anaerobic," "carbonic maceration" and related terms are imprecise and often applied inconsistently across the industry. Kornman and Evan Gilman produced **Kornman's Processing Flowchart** — a color-coded diagram showing which processing steps are "almost always," "almost never," or ambiguously present in each method. Also provides processing history context: Colombian natural ban and 2016 FNC resolution; Brazilian natural evolution; Ethiopian naturals reaching Grade 1.

## Key Claims

### Terminology: What's Imprecise and Why

The most significant contribution. Kornman argues that processing language evolved ad hoc and lacks industry-wide standardization:

| Term | Problem | Kornman's preferred alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Natural" | FDA defines "natural food" differently; opaque about the process itself | "Dried in the cherry" or "dried in the fruit" |
| "Sun-dried" | Too inclusive — many non-natural coffees also sun-dry | Avoid as a synonym for natural |
| "Dry process" | Accurate, but could be confused with "dry milling" (hulling, sorting) | Acceptable; use with caution |
| "Wet process" | Applied to both washed and honey/pulped natural; not synonymous with "washed" | Use "washed" for fully-washed coffees specifically |
| "Honey / pulped natural / semi-washed" | Three names for essentially the same process; "semi-washed" fading; "honey" winning | "Honey" has become the de facto standard |
| "Anaerobic" | Technically ALL fermentation is anaerobic; in coffee used loosely for any sealed low-oxygen environment | "Sealed-tank fermentation or maceration" |
| "Carbonic maceration" | In wine, a specific intracellular fermentation process; in coffee, almost exclusively means whole cherry in sealed tank | Be specific: "whole cherry sealed-tank maceration" |
| "Lactic" | Used to indicate dominance of lactic acid bacteria in anaerobic conditions; not a standalone processing description | Note: a microbial tendency in sealed-tank processes, not a method name |
| "Fully washed" | Historically distinguished washed from semi-washed/honey | Now rarely needed; context usually sufficient |
| "Double washed" | Post-fermentation soak in clean water (common in Kenya, Ethiopia) | Useful as a precision term |
| "Triple washed" | Pre-fermentation flotation + fermentation + post-fermentation soak | Useful as a precision term |

**Kornman's recommendation**: rather than relying on imprecise terms, specify the equipment used and the state of the coffee during fermentation (whole cherry? depulped parchment? submerged in water?). This matters for understanding flavor potential in green bean selection.

### Kornman's Processing Flowchart

Kornman and Evan Gilman created a flowchart (Figure 1, "Coffee Processing Types") showing which steps are part of each processing method:
- **Green (🟩 Always)** = almost always present in this method
- **Brown (🟫 Rarely)** = almost never present
- **Tan (🟨 Sometimes)** = ambiguous / varies by producer
- plus 🟦 Usually and 🏁 Partially for a few cells

10 process steps (cherry flotation → drying state) × 9 methods (fully/double/triple washed, natural, pulped natural/honey, wet hulled, anaerobic, carbonic maceration, eco-pulped). The most precise summary in the wiki of what steps actually constitute each method, and it operationalizes the terminology ambiguity Kornman critiques — the "Sometimes" cells are where producer usage diverges.

### Processing Method History

**Natural / Dry Process**
- World's oldest post-harvest method; minimal infrastructure required
- Fell out of favor when coffee spread to humid climates where sun-drying was impractical
- Currently in a "mid-renaissance" as specialty market demands differentiated flavors
- **Ethiopian naturals**: previously considered inferior to washed; modern properly-sorted Ethiopian naturals have achieved Grade 1 status (formerly washed-only) with strawberry, peach, jasmine cup notes
- **Brazilian naturals**: only method available in Brazil until early 1990s; industrial-scale mechanized harvest limited quality; "new Brazilian natural" profile emerged from 2000s onward from smallholder microlot producers
- **Colombian naturals**: *effectively banned from export* until the FNC introduced a new less strictly defined export grade in 2016 (resolution allowing natural exports)

**Honey / Pulped Natural**
- First appeared Brazil, early 1990s — motivated by reducing water use while achieving washed-like flavor
- Invented/documented at Pinhalense equipment factory, Espírito Santo do Pinhal, São Paulo, 1991 (source notes this is unverified anecdote)
- Portuguese term: *cereza descascado* = "de-husked cherry" (skin removed, flesh retained)
- English: "pulped natural" (depulped but dried like a natural)
- Spanish: *semi-lavado* ("semi-washed"); slight variation in water use during pulping
- "Honey" color labels (black/red/yellow/white) = vague indication of mucilage retained; not standardized

**Anaerobic / Sealed-Tank**
- Increasing prevalence in third decade of 21st century
- Intent: restrict oxygen → limit fermentation microbiota to more beneficial types; develop new flavor compounds
- "Anaerobic" and "carbonic" are terms that "convolute and misconstrue both their role and the role of more traditional fermentation" — Kornman
- No settled industry language yet for the full spectrum of these techniques

### Other Processing Details

- **Ecopulpers / demucilaginators**: remove all or nearly all mucilage mechanically without fermentation step; sometimes labeled "washed" due to sensory similarity; not truly washed
- **Cherry flotation**: removes rocks and underripe cherries; often precedes multiple processing styles, not just washed; rarely indicated on packaging despite its quality importance
- **Bag / table maceration**: whole cherry partial/full sealing; "winey" flavors; high defect risk; sometimes labeled "anaerobic"
- **Temperature-controlled fermentation**: sealed tanks in air-conditioned environments or underground; extends maceration timeline
- All processing: "40% of quality determined in field, 40% in primary processing, 20% in secondary processing and storage" (consistent with Girma 2022 review)

### Ferment Defect
- Field observation: discarded cherry/skin piles smelled of "spoiled wine and black olives" — this is the "ferment" sensory defect in cupping
- All coffee undergoes some fermentation — the question is whether it's controlled and beneficial, or uncontrolled and defective
