# Wet Hulling (Giling Basah)

## Definition

A processing method unique to Indonesia in which coffee parchment is removed while the bean is still at high moisture content (20–40%), rather than after full drying. The Indonesian name is *Giling Basah* ("wet grinding/hulling").

## Why It Developed

Indonesia's humid tropical climate makes it difficult to dry coffee using traditional methods — extended drying at high humidity risks mold and uneven drying. Wet Hulling evolved as a practical solution: hulling the parchment earlier shortens the critical drying phase and reduces the moisture-related risk window.

## The Four Steps

| Step | Detail |
|---|---|
| **1. Pulping** | Outer skin and pulp removed, as in washed processing |
| **2. Partial drying** | Beans (in parchment) dried only to ~20–40% moisture — far shorter than a standard drying cycle |
| **3. Hull while wet** | Parchment removed at high moisture; beans are soft, exposed, and physically vulnerable |
| **4. Final drying** | Naked beans dried to ~10–12% moisture for export |

The key difference from washed processing is step 3: in washed, hulling happens after the bean is fully dry. Here, the bean goes through its most vulnerable development stage exposed to the environment.

## Effect on Cup Quality

The high-moisture hulling step is the mechanism behind the Sumatran flavor signature:

| Characteristic | Direction | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Acidity | **Lower** | Extended contact with high-moisture environment reduces perceived acidity |
| Body | **Heavier** | Bean structure changes during wet hulling; more permeable to environment during drying |
| Flavor notes | **Earthy, woody, herbal, spicy** | Environmental exposure during naked drying; characteristic "Sumatran earthiness" |
| Sweetness | Chocolate, brown sugar undertones | Medium-dark roast development; inherent bean character |

Wet-hulled coffees are distinctly different from washed or natural coffees at equivalent altitudes — the processing method dominates the cup character more than varietal genetics.

## Roasting Implications

Medium to medium-dark roast is generally recommended for Wet Hulled coffees:
- Softens sharper herbal/woody edges that read as harsh at lighter roasts
- Develops caramel and dark chocolate sweetness
- Heavy body holds up well through medium-dark development without becoming bitter
- Light roasting a Wet Hulled coffee tends to emphasize earthy/herbal notes uncomfortably and lacks the sweetness development that makes the cup work

## Where It's Used

Primarily Indonesia:
- **Sumatra**: Aceh/Gayo, Lintong, Mandheling — the most widely used; defines the "Sumatran" specialty profile
- **Java, Sulawesi, Papua**: Arabica from these islands may also use Wet Hulling (details not yet in wiki)

## Trade-offs vs. Other Processing Methods

| vs. Washed | vs. Natural |
|---|---|
| Less clarity and brightness | Less fruit intensity and sweetness |
| More body | Similar or more body |
| Earth/spice replaces floral/citrus notes | Earth/herb replaces berry/jam notes |
| Higher risk of physical bean damage during hulling | — |

## Cold Brew Note

A controlled UC Davis study (Batali et al. 2022) found that Sumatra wet-hulled coffee brewed hot produced notably more **"smoky"** character than when brewed cold. Cold brewing suppresses the earthy/smoky signature that defines the Sumatran profile. If preserving the characteristic Sumatran cup is the goal, hot brewing is the better approach. Cold brewing produces a different coffee — less earthy, less smoky — which could be an intentional choice but is not the representative expression of the processing method. ## Open Questions

- Does altitude interact with Wet Hulling in a measurable way? High-altitude Gayo (1,700m) processed via Giling Basah vs. lower-altitude Lintong: does altitude's usual quality contribution survive the processing method?
- Are there "cleaner" wet-hulled coffees that approach washed clarity, or is the earthy/heavy profile inherent to the method regardless of control?
- How does fermentation time in step 2 (partial drying phase) affect final cup quality? Some producers now experiment with controlled fermentation before the wet hull step.
