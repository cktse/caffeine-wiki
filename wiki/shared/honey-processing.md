# Honey Processing

## Definition

A processing method in which the coffee cherry skin and pulp are removed (as in washed processing), but some or all of the mucilage layer is intentionally left on the parchment before drying. The amount of mucilage retained — and the drying and fermentation conditions — determines the flavor outcome and the color label applied.

The name comes from the honey-like stickiness of the mucilage-coated parchment during drying, not from any honey ingredient.

## Costa Rica Association

Honey processing is **strongly associated with Costa Rica**, which has been central to developing and standardizing the color-label system. Some sources attribute the origin of honey processing to Costa Rican producers; this source (1Zpresso) says it "developed later" than washed and natural but stops short of formal attribution. A more authoritative processing history source is needed to confirm or deny the origin claim.

Regardless of origin, Costa Rica remains the reference country for honey processing — its eight regions apply honey variants systematically, and it has been the primary driver of standardizing what Black / Red / Yellow / White honey means in international trade.

## The Color Label System

Color labels indicate how much mucilage was retained before drying. **There is no single universal standard** — percentages and practices vary by region and mill. Treat as a general reference, not a precise specification.

| Color | Mucilage retained | Cup character |
|---|---|---|
| **Black honey** | ~75–100% | Deeper sweetness; richer fruit character; softest acidity; closest to natural processing in style |
| **Red honey** | ~50% | Pronounced sweetness; fuller mouthfeel; gentler acidity |
| **Yellow honey** | ~25% | Balanced sweetness and floral-fruity aromatics; livelier acidity; cleaner, lighter feel |
| **White honey** | ~10% | Brighter acidity; softer sweetness; lighter fermentation character; freshest profile |

**Direction of travel**: More mucilage → more sweetness, more body, softer acidity, more fermentation character. Less mucilage → more clarity, brighter acidity, lighter body, cleaner finish.

## Non-Standard Honey Variants

Two experimental/producer variants found in Costa Rica:

**Raisin Honey** — a producer term, not an industry standard:
- First drying + fermentation: whole cherry dried until it shrivels into a raisin-like state
- Second drying + fermentation: skin and part of pulp removed; mucilage retained; second drying/fermentation cycle until ready to hull
- Cup: vivid fruit aroma, pronounced sweetness, softer acidity, noticeable fermentation-driven character, heavier body

**Extended Fermentation Honey** — informal designation:
- More mucilage retained; fermentation time extended beyond standard honey process
- Cup: sweeter, more intensely tropical, sometimes passion-fruit aromatics; not a formal industry category

## Comparison with Washed and Natural

| | Washed | Honey | Natural |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mucilage | Removed | Partially retained | Fully retained (dried inside whole cherry) |
| Acidity | Highest | Medium (varies by color) | Lowest |
| Body | Lightest | Medium | Heaviest |
| Sweetness | From variety/terroir | Enhanced by mucilage contact | Intense fruit sweetness |
| Clarity | Highest | Medium | Lowest (most complex/opaque) |
| Fermentation character | Minimal | Variable (more with Black/Red) | High |

## Relation to Mucilage

The mucilage layer is the pectin-rich substance between the fruit pulp and the parchment. In honey processing, controlled mucilage retention is the key variable. See [[processing/concepts/mucilage]] for the full concept page on mucilage composition and role.

## Brewing Implications

- Honey-processed coffees generally benefit from recipes that balance sweetness with clarity — the 4:6 method's first-phase pour ratio is a useful dial (higher first pour = more sweetness, which honey coffees can lean into, or lower first pour for brightness)
- Black/Red honey coffees can handle slightly coarser grind and lower temperature (closer to medium/medium-dark roast parameters) — the inherent sweetness doesn't require maximum extraction clarity
- Yellow/White honey coffees sit closer to washed in extraction behavior — treat more like washed for temperature and grind
