# Fermentation Compounds and Flavor

## Why This Page Exists

Fermentation during coffee processing produces a predictable suite of organic acids and alcohols. These compounds — either directly or as precursors to roasting-derived aroma molecules — shape the sensory attributes of the finished cup. This is the chemical bridge between a processing decision (fermentation method and duration) and a sensory outcome (cup profile and SCA score).

This page is the cross-domain link between [[processing/concepts/fermentation]] and cup quality.

## The Core Pathway

```
Cherry/mucilage sugars (sucrose, fructose, glucose)
    → [microbial fermentation: bacteria + yeasts]
    → Organic acids + alcohols + CO₂
    → [thermal transformation during roasting: Maillard, Strecker degradation]
    → Aroma and flavor compounds in the cup
```

The fermentation step is the only stage in the chain that is fully controlled post-harvest.

## Compound → Flavor Mapping

| Compound | Sensory contribution | Range concern |
|---|---|---|
| **Lactic acid** | Smooth, rounded, milky/creamy/yogurt-like; at high concentration: coconut notes | Universally desirable at expected SIAF concentrations (~60–80 mg/g); most abundant acid in all controlled SIAF conditions |
| **Ethanol** | Alcoholic notes (rum, wine); enhances body/viscosity; acts as solvent for volatile aromatics; indirect Maillard precursor | At very high concentration (natural solid-state 96h) → dominant wine/rum character; may not suit all buyers |
| **Glycerol** | Mouthfeel, creaminess, tactile body; stabilizes volatile aroma retention; participates in Maillard during roasting | Increases only in natural SIAF (fruit skin present); explains body difference between natural and pulped SIAF |
| **Malic acid** | Apple, green fruit, tartaric acidity character | Consumed by ~48h in natural fermentation — short treatments show more malic character; largely absent by late stages |
| **Citric acid** | Citrus, lemon/orange acidity | Produced during aerobic-to-anaerobic transition (first 24h, Krebs cycle); useful acidity at moderate concentration |
| **Acetic acid** | Fruity/bright acidity when moderate; vinegary/sour off-note if excessive | Higher in natural SIAF (6.18 mg/g) vs. pulped (2.62 mg/g) at 96h — monitor in long natural fermentations; well below off-flavor threshold in the studied conditions |
| **Succinic acid** | Pleasant tartness, complexity | Present in small quantities; generally positive |
| **Propionic acid** | Rancid, off-note | **Not detected** in properly controlled SIAF — its absence is a quality indicator |
| **Butyric acid** | Rancid, vomit-like off-note | **Not detected** in properly controlled SIAF — absence signals no spoilage bacteria |
| **Sucrose (residual)** | May contribute directly to sweetness when present at low concentrations (below threshold) | Rapidly consumed in natural fermentation; slower in pulped; CO₂ modified atmosphere slows sucrose consumption — see [[processing/concepts/fermentation]] |

## How Method and Duration Shape the Profile

### Natural solid-state SIAF — flavor evolution

| Phase | Duration | Dominant metabolites | Cup character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pre-fermentation | 0h | Malic acid; high sugars | Chestnut, cocoa, caramel (unfermented baseline) |
| Early | 24h | Sugar consumption begins; citric acid from Krebs cycle; ethanol starting | Caramel, honey, mild wine — close to control |
| Mid | 48–72h | Lactic acid building; malic consumed; ethanol rising | Red/yellow fruits, chocolate, blueberry, spice, floral notes |
| Late | 96h | Lactic dominant (~80 mg/g); high ethanol (62 mg/g); glycerol elevated | Rum, grape, wine, red fruits — most complex, most alcoholic |

### By method at 96h fermentation

| Method | Ethanol (mg/g) | Glycerol | Lactic acid | Cup character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Natural solid-state | 62 | Elevated | ~80 mg/g | Wine, rum, red fruits, highly complex |
| Natural submerged | 31 (2× lower) | Elevated | ~80 mg/g | Fruity, banana/cassis, less alcoholic |
| Pulped solid-state | ~10 (6× lower) | Unchanged | ~60 mg/g | Chocolate, caramel, chestnut — restrained |
| Pulped submerged | ~10 | Unchanged | ~60 mg/g | Chocolate, molasses, malt — restrained |

**Why natural >> pulped in SIAF**: natural coffee starts with ~2× the fermentable sugars (fructose: 82.3 vs. 37.0 mg/g; sucrose: 59.75 vs. 29.40 mg/g). More substrate drives more lactic acid, ethanol, and glycerol production.

**Why solid-state >> submerged (for natural)**: water addition in submerged acts as a solvent, diluting ethanol concentration. Water's high heat capacity also causes larger temperature oscillations, altering microbial dynamics. Result: half the ethanol in the final coffee.

## Compound Behavior During Roasting

| Compound | Behavior during roasting | Cup implication |
|---|---|---|
| Lactic acid, succinic acid | Relatively stable | Contribute directly to cup acidity |
| Acetic acid | Volatile; partially evaporates | Green bean levels moderate down to cup; moderate green level → pleasant acidity |
| Ethanol | Fully evaporates | No ethanol in the cup — but contributes to ester formation (fruity aromatics) via Maillard and Strecker reactions |
| Glycerol | Stable; participates in Maillard | Contributes to body and mouthfeel; Maillard-derived aroma precursor |
| Chlorogenic acid (CGA) | **Dramatically and monotonically destroyed by roasting**; partially degraded by aerobic fermentation; protected by CO₂ atmosphere | Light-medium roast is required to preserve CGA in the cup. CGA decreases with every increment of roast degree and is essentially eliminated by dark roasting (Lindsey 2024, HPLC). CO₂/anaerobic fermentation protects CGA in the green bean (Moncayo 2025) — but this benefit is realized *only* at light-medium roast. Dark roasting destroys the CGA advantage regardless of processing. **Cross-domain chain: CO₂ fermentation (processing) → CGA preserved in green bean → light-medium roast (roasting) → CGA survives to cup.** |

## Connection to Perceived Sweetness

The aromatic compounds produced by fermentation — fruity/wine-like esters derived from ethanol, and creamy notes from lactic acid — fall in the sweetness-enhancing aromatic category. See [[shared/sweetness-perception]].

This is the mechanism behind why CO₂-fermented and SIAF naturals score higher on perceived sweetness: the fermentation builds the exact aromatic profile (fruity, fermented, wine-like) that the aromatic modulation pathway uses to enhance sweetness perception — even though sugar content itself is largely depleted.

## Sourcing Decision Rules

1. **Natural SIAF >> pulped SIAF** for sensory quality — the sugar substrate difference is structural
2. **Short SIAF on pulped (24h solid-state) may be worse than no fermentation** — only one study, but 78.95 SCA vs. 83.60 unfermented control is a notable risk
3. **Duration ≥48h for natural solid-state** = evidence-based minimum for meaningful quality improvement; <36h is predominantly low-scoring
4. **Natural solid-state 96h** = highest scores but most alcoholic profile (rum/wine dominates) — a style choice, not just a quality ceiling
5. **Absence of propionic and butyric acids** is a quality signal — their presence indicates spoilage
6. **CO₂ injection** achieves faster anaerobiosis (24–60h vs. 3–7 days for SIAF), lower mycotoxin risk, and a different sensory signature (liquor-like/silky vs. fruity/sweet/creamy for SIAF) — see [[processing/concepts/anaerobic-fermentation]]

## Diacetyl — Two Upstream Pathways to the Same Sweetness Compound

**Diacetyl (2,3-butanedione)** appears in the cup via two entirely different upstream processes:

1. **Fermentation pathway**: Produced by lactic acid bacteria during extended post-harvest fermentation. Documented in Chiroso extended fermentation (60h multi-stage; [[varietals/sources/chiroso-sensory-pazmino-2022]]) alongside acetoin (3-hydroxy-2-butanone, a related compound). These fermentation-derived diketones contribute the buttery/milky/dairy notes characteristic of extended fermentation.

2. **Roasting pathway**: Produced via Maillard/Strecker degradation of sugar fragments during the development phase ([[roasting/sources/dtr-sensory-alstrup-2020]]). Peaks at fast DT (90s post-1C vs. 390s); degrades monotonically with extended development time at same Agtron color.

Both pathways produce the same compound contributing to the same sensory outcome (buttery, caramel, sweetness-enhancing). A coffee with extended fermentation AND a fast DT roast may have additive diacetyl contributions from both stages — though this cross-pathway interaction is not yet directly measured in this wiki's sources.

**Implications for roasting decisions based on upstream processing**:
- **Extended-fermentation naturals** (SIAF 96h solid-state, CO₂ fermented): already carry fermentation-derived diacetyl and acetoin into the green bean. A fast DT roast adds Maillard-derived diacetyl on top. Sweetness potential may be maximized with fast DT.
- **Washed or short-fermentation coffees**: less fermentation-derived diacetyl. Roasting-derived diacetyl (fast DT) is the primary pathway. Sweetness from diacetyl depends more heavily on DT control.

## Open Questions

- How do these compound profiles shift at higher altitudes (1,500–2,200m), where the most distinctive Ethiopian and Colombian naturals grow? All SIAF data is from Brazil at 625–1,150m.
- Does variety (introgressed CLR-resistant vs. Ethiopian landrace vs. Bourbon) significantly alter the microbial community and thus the compound profile in SIAF? Catiguá MG2 has Timor Hybrid genetics — its natural microbiota may differ substantially from landrace varieties.
- Does light roast vs. medium roast differentially preserve or transform the fermentation-derived compounds? **Partially answered for CGA**: Lindsey 2024 confirms CGA decreases dramatically with every increment of roast degree, so lighter roast clearly preserves more CGA. For other fermentation compounds (lactic acid, ethanol, glycerol, acetic acid) across the light-to-medium roast spectrum: not yet sourced in this wiki.
- What is the practical threshold at which acetic acid crosses from "pleasant acidity" to "vinegary off-note" in a roasted coffee? Not established in the wiki sources.
