# Jonathan Gagné

## What / Who

Jonathan Gagné is a physicist (astrophysicist by training) and specialty coffee researcher known for applying rigorous physics and fluid dynamics thinking to filter coffee brewing. He is the author of **"The Physics of Filter Coffee"** (2021, published by Scott Rao), which is the most technically rigorous treatment of filter coffee in the specialty coffee literature.

He runs the blog **Coffee ad Astra** (coffeeadastra.com), where his primary research and experimental findings are published.

## Relevance

Gagné's most significant contribution to this wiki is his 2022 filtration model for coffee astringency: astringency is a **filtration problem**, not an over-extraction or flow-evenness problem. Astringent compounds are nearly insoluble at typical brew temperatures — they float as suspended particles in the slurry and are physically filtered out by the coffee bed. This reframes the understanding of why channeling, fine grinds, and high pressure all produce astringency.

His model directly explains and predicts:
- Why channeling causes astringency (short-circuits filtration, not over-extraction)
- Why grinding finer hits an unavoidable astringency ceiling (more broken cells = more particles liberated)
- Why higher pressure increases astringency (faster microscopic flow drags particles through)
- Why even a fully saturated long-steep slurry produces astringency during uneven drawdown
- Why decaf beans appear nearly immune to astringency
- Why a thick, flat, undisturbed bed with slow flow reduces astringency

He is also the first in this wiki to note that wine tannins (the primary astringency compounds in wine) were searched for in coffee and not found, and that Samo Smrke's colleagues had likely identified the actual compound (identity withheld in 2022 pending peer review).

## Key Facts

- Background: astrophysicist (Ph.D.)
- Author: *The Physics of Filter Coffee* (2021, published by Scott Rao)
- Blog: Coffee ad Astra (coffeeadastra.com)
- 2022 astringency model: developed after conversation with Samo Smrke (food scientist, ZHAW) at the Specialty Coffee Expo, Boston
- Empirically tested the 70°C low-astringency strategy; confirmed EY 27% without astringency but with significantly duller cup profile; Rao confirmed the same finding independently years earlier
- SOL dripper experiment (Canadian Roasting Society, with Thibaut Paggen): near-espresso grind + even flow + paper filter + thick bed still produced unavoidable astringency, establishing the grind-size ceiling concept
- Scott Rao published Gagné's book and participated in the 70°C experiment discussion; Rao subsequently cited Gagné's suspended-particle theory in his 2025 Decent Espresso masterclass
- 2023 PSD analysis of the Kaffeemacher/ZHAW 24-grinder dataset: defined **unimodality** (few fines) vs **uniformity** (few boulders); "dialling in espresso = dialling in the fines fraction"; over-shooting effect (source: [[brewing/sources/gagne-grinder-psd-analysis-2023]])
- 2025 bed-depth framework: dripper **diameter = batch size**, bed **depth = a fundamental flavor parameter**; deeper → coarser + longer + more forgiving; extended his surface-adsorption filtration model of astringency (source: [[brewing/sources/gagne-bed-depth-2025]])
