# UC Davis Coffee Center

## What / Who

A university research center at the University of California, Davis, producing peer-reviewed coffee science on brewing, extraction, and sensory/consumer analysis. Led by chemical engineer **William D. Ristenpart** (physics/engineering of brewing; co-author of *The Design of Coffee*) and sensory scientist **Jean-Xavier Guinard** (descriptive + consumer sensory science). Graduate researchers on the wiki's cited work include **Mackenzie Batali, Scott Frost, and Andrew Cotter**.

## Relevance

The center is the source of two of this wiki's brewing pillars and the rigorous extraction math underneath them:

- The **Brewing Control Chart** synthesis ([[../sources/brewing-control-chart-guinard-2023]]) — the TDS × PE × brew-ratio sensory/consumer framework.
- The **cold-brew vs. iced** sensory study ([[../sources/cold-brew-vs-iced-batali-2022]]) — Batali et al.
- The **brew-temperature** finding (Batali et al. 2020): at fixed TDS/PE, temperature has little sensory impact — a recurring caveat across the brewing pages.
- The **extraction/absorption math**: R_abs ≈ 2.1 liquid-retention ratio and the equilibrium-desorption model (Ristenpart & Kuhl; Liang et al. 2021) that underpin [[../concepts/brewing-control-chart]] and [[../concepts/bypass]].

Their work is a **high-credibility anchor** for brewing claims in this wiki — controlled designs, trained panels, and consumer preference mapping rather than practitioner assertion.

## Key Facts
- Ristenpart = engineering/physics of brewing; Guinard = sensory/consumer science (source: [[../sources/brewing-control-chart-guinard-2023]]).
- Three underlying BCC studies: Frost et al. 2020 (TDS×PE×roast), Batali et al. 2020 (TDS×PE×temperature), Cotter et al. 2021 (consumer liking; two preference clusters).
- Consumer panels drew on young Northern-California black-coffee drinkers — a stated generalizability limit.
