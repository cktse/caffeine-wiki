# Decent Espresso

## What / Who

Decent Espresso is a specialty espresso machine company co-founded by John Buckman. The Decent Espresso machine (DE1 and subsequent models) is a software-controlled espresso machine capable of independently varying pressure, flow rate, and temperature throughout a shot — enabling brewing profiles impossible on conventional machines.

## Relevance

Decent Espresso enables three brewing techniques that are specific to the machine's unique capabilities:
- **Blooming Espresso** — espresso with a ~30-second pressure-held pause (impossible on standard machines)
- **Allongé** — high-flow, high-volume long espresso optimized for light roasts
- **Filter3** — converts the Decent into a no-bypass filter brewer by dispensing water at zero pressure

Scott Rao ([[roasting/entities/scott-rao]]) developed Blooming Espresso on a Decent and has demonstrated it at masterclasses. Rao is credited with early testing and development of these profiles.

## Key Specifications / Capabilities

| Capability | Description |
|---|---|
| Flow control | Can set precise ml/sec flow rate at any point in a shot |
| Pressure control | Can hold pressure without flow (enables Blooming pause) |
| No-pressure dispense | Can dispense water at 0 bar (enables Filter3) |
| Temperature control | Independent water temperature throughout shot |
| Bluetooth scale integration | Stop-by-weight feature for precise yield control |
| Shower screen | Dimples/Freckles models: intentionally uneven water distribution to compensate for radial extraction differences (outer ring of puck under-extracts) |

## Blooming Espresso

**Decent-specific** (3-way valve on standard machines would release pressure on flow-stop). Core profile:
1. Pressurized preinfusion at maximum flow rate (fills puck in ~2 seconds, bottom to top)
2. **~30-second pause** with pressure maintained (visible passive pressure drop); material dissolves into standing liquid
3. High-flow percolation phase — pushes dissolved material out

EY up to 29–30% possible with very light roasts (Kenyans, Ethiopians). Requires fine grind; pressure flow limiter prevents excessive back-pressure; headspace recommended (+~1% EY). "Challenging to dial in" (Rao). (Source: [[brewing/sources/rao-blooming-filter3-quakers-2025]])

## Allongé

Long espresso with high flow rate and high solvent volume. Suited to light roasts:
- **Flow rate**: 4.5 ml/sec (sourced: Rao 2025); ratio 4:1–5:1; **EY** 26–27% typical
- Large water volume → high EY via solvent quantity
- High flow rate → fruit acid emphasis; "highlights acidity and fruitiness in a way filter coffee can't"
- Rao: ~2.5× more concentrated than Filter3
- JB's personal preference for light roasts ("vibrant, balanced, beautiful")
- Channeling risk at long ratios: deep bed + careful puck prep + Decent flow profiling (backs off pressure if channel forms → prevents channel widening)
- Commercial validated: Rao served 150/day at his Montreal café (2008–2012) with dedicated grinder and puck prep routine

## Turbo Shot

High-flow short shot — Allongé alternative for setups with higher channeling risk (non-Decent machines, imperfect puck prep). Achieves fruit acid emphasis via high flow without the long water volume that amplifies channeling. Turbo Turbo variant: ~3.5 ml/sec, stopped earlier than standard Allongé.

## Filter3

No-bypass filter brewing within a Decent:
- 22g coffee; ~8.5cm deep basket; paper filter below; water at 0 bar
- Extremley coarse grind; ~4 minutes
- Check liquid level at 100 seconds (should be halfway up basket) to confirm grind
- Use Bluetooth scale to stop 20g before target weight
- If bed dries out late: increase flow rate in final 30 seconds
- Rarely astringent; clean, floral cup; ~2.5× more dilute than Allongé
- Works especially well for decaff (decaf has 90%+ astringent compounds removed)

## Key Facts

- Predecessor models: DE1 (original), various model iterations
- JB background: philosophy background; began with software/product design; found that fast/maximum pre-infusion rate (not slow 4ml/sec) dramatically reduced channeling — had previously set 4ml/sec as "the rate the puck can absorb water," which was wrong
- Radial extraction insight: outer ring of puck consistently under-extracted; Dimples/Freckles shower screens add more water to outer ring to compensate
