# Specialty Coffee Association (SCA)

## What / Who

The global trade association representing the specialty coffee industry. Formed in 2017 from the merger of the Specialty Coffee Association of America (SCAA, est. 1982) and the Specialty Coffee Association of Europe (SCAE). Headquartered in the United States with global membership.

## Relevance

The SCA sets the dominant standards for coffee quality evaluation worldwide:
- **SCA Cupping Protocol** (2004; currently under revision) — the scoring system used in Cup of Excellence, Q-grader certification, and most specialty coffee buying decisions
- **Coffee Value Assessment** — the replacement/successor to the 2004 protocol; emphasizes intensity scales over binary presence scoring, including sweetness
- **Q-grader certification** — the professional quality credential for coffee buyers and evaluators; issued under the Coffee Quality Institute (CQI), which operates under SCA framework
- **Coffee Science Foundation** — the SCA's research funding arm; commissioned the Ohio State University sweetness research

## Key Facts

- Merged 2017 (SCAA + SCAE)
- SCA Cupping Protocol (2004): 100-point scale; specialty grade ≥80; evaluates fragrance/aroma, flavor, aftertaste, acidity, body, balance, uniformity, clean cup, sweetness, overall
- In the 2004 protocol, sweetness is scored as presence/absence (10 points each; 10 = sweet, 0 = not sweet) — a known weakness identified by 95% of experts surveyed in 2021
- The 2021 survey of ~50 experts found strong consensus that sweetness deserves an intensity scale, not just a presence marker
- Q-grader certification: issued by Coffee Quality Institute (CQI); requires passing 22 exams
