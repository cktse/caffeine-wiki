# Tetsu Kasuya

## What / Who

Japanese barista and 2016 World Brewers Cup Champion. Known in specialty coffee for developing structured, repeatable pour-over recipes with a strong emphasis on controlling extraction by dividing the brew into discrete pours.

## Relevance

Popularized the "devil Switch technique" — an early recipe framework for the Hario Switch that influenced later recipe developers including [[entities/asser-christensen]].

## Key Facts

- 2016 World Brewers Cup Champion; won with the 4:6 Method on V60 (Fact: [[sources/kasuya-46-method-philocoffea]])
- Founder of Philocoffea — coffee brand and shop in Funabashi City, Chiba, Japan
- **4:6 Method (V60):** 1:15 ratio; 40% phase controls flavor (pour ratio), 60% phase controls strength (pour count); medium-coarse to coarse grind; temp 93/88/83°C for light/medium/dark roast (Fact: [[sources/kasuya-46-method-philocoffea]])
- "Devil Switch technique" — his Hario Switch recipe; lower extraction than Christensen's adaptation (Fact: [[sources/hario-switch-review-coffee-chronicler]])
- Devil recipe parameters: 20g / 280ml / 1:14 / 90°C → 70°C temperature drop / Medium-fine grind / 3:00 total (Fact: [[sources/tetsu-kasuya-devil-recipe-hario-switch]])
- Design philosophy: maximize extraction early (open/hot), slow it in later stages (closed/cool) (Fact: [[sources/tetsu-kasuya-devil-recipe-hario-switch]])
- 2025 "New Hybrid" recipe: 20g / 300ml / 1:15 / starts switch CLOSED (immersion bloom) / 90°C → 70–80°C / 3:30 total; described as producing "a very sweet cup" (Fact: [[sources/kasuya-2025-new-hybrid-hario-switch]])
- Structural evolution devil → 2025: added immersion bloom phase at start; ratio shifted from 1:14 to 1:15; total time extended 30 sec
- **Flash-chill iced (2026):** two Switch recipes — 4:6-applied (halved water, sharp acidity) vs a 2-pour immersion hybrid (smoother, sweeter); preferred the hybrid, choose by bean (Fact: [[sources/kasuya-iced-46-vs-hybrid-philocoffea]]); see [[concepts/flash-chill-iced-coffee]]
