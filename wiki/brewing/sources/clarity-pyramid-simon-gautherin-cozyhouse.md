# Simon Gautherin's Clarity Pyramid & Selective Extraction — 2026 WBrC 2nd (Cozy House analysis)

## Summary

The same SCA educator (暖窩 Cozy House Coffee, 黃明志) analyses the 2026 World Brewers Cup **runner-up**, Australia's **Simon Gautherin**. His routine is built on **subtraction, not maximisation**: discard the first ~10g of the most concentrated flow, and cut the tail early, keeping peach/citrus/white-flower and suppressing the cocoa/bitter tail. He frames it as a **"Clarity Pyramid"** — *green coffee creates → roasting unlocks → brewing selects → water highlights.* Credibility: **high-medium (reputable practitioner/educator)**; well-reasoned, transparent about data ambiguity.

> **Flags:** (1) **commercial bias** — course-seller blog. (2) Recipe is **secondhand** competition transcription; the "~140g" cut is ambiguous (total collected vs final serving weight) — sources agree only on the two core *actions* (front-discard + early tail cut). Filed **as-claimed**.

## The Clarity Pyramid

*Green coffee **creates**. Roasting **unlocks**. Brewing **selects**. Water **highlights**.*

| Stage | Role |
|---|---|
| **Create** (green) | variety, climate, ripeness, processing set the *possible* flavors |
| **Unlock** (roast) | heat opens the bean's existing sweetness/acidity/aroma — it doesn't invent them |
| **Select** (brew) | grind, temp, time, flow, **cut point** decide what enters the cup and what's suppressed |
| **Highlight** (water) | minerals are seasoning — shift which acidity/fruit/texture you notice first; not flavor from nothing |

**Simon's "clarity":** the cups people most easily understand and love tend to be **high legibility, lower extraction yield, lower concentration, higher minerality**. (Cozy House's caveat: clarity ≠ "the weaker the better" — a thin cup may just be under-extracted; real clarity is when sweetness/acidity/aroma/body are all present but *locatable*.)

## Key Claims

- **Selective extraction = subtraction:** front ~10g discarded (the first valve flow is the most concentrated/intense and would mask the fruit + silky texture); tail cut early (~140g of 200g) before cocoa/bitterness rises. Not "extract more" — "decide what's worth keeping."
- **Not universal rules:** on a V60 with normal percolation the first drops are a different condition than Pulsar's steep-then-release; front liquid can hold lovely aroma/acidity. Judge by the *blended* cup, not a memorised rule.
- **Two-Geisha blend by complementarity:** 50% Colombia Peñas Blancas (warmer site → ripe stone-fruit sweetness) + 50% Panama Finca Gales (cooler → delicate florals); both natural, ~30-day slow cold dry. Blend to fill what the cup lacks, like espresso blending — not "pile good beans together." (Final Panama swapped in on finals morning — recipe choices go to the last minute.)
- **Dual-DTR roast + long rest:** Colombia DTR ~10% (open sweetness/nuts), Panama ~6.7% (preserve fine florals); roasted separately, mixed before brewing; ~6-week rest to reduce green/herbal notes. Numbers are bean/roaster-specific, not universal.
- **NextLevel Pulsar — no-bypass valve dripper:** flat bed + top dispersion cap + bottom flow valve; no-bypass forces water through the bed → repeatable ratio/extraction; valve enables steep-then-percolate. Equipment gives *control*, not a correction-free answer.
- **~80ppm custom water:** MgSO₄ : KCl : SiO₂ ≈ 3:2:1 — Mg sulphate emphasises **citrus**, KCl makes **peach/stone-fruit** more perceptible, silica → **round/silky** texture. Caveat: ppm alone ≠ full water spec (alkalinity, Ca/Mg, ions matter). Simon co-founded water brand **APAX LAB**.
- **Multimodal sensory:** paired a cup with an intentionally thicker rim to amplify perceived silkiness via touch — rim thickness, temperature, and sip flow all shape the description.
- **Meta:** learn the *reasoning* (define the target cup, then arrange technique), not the 10g. Simon's own takeaway: "trust your intuition."

## Final recipe (as-claimed)

14g (Colombia 7g + Panama 7g, both Geisha) · 200g · 89 °C · ~80ppm · very coarse, uniform, low-fines grind · **NextLevel Pulsar** · valve closed, 100g steep to **1:20** → 100g, open valve → **discard first ~10g** → **cut ~140g** before the cocoa tail.
