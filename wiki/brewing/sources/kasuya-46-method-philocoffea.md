# How to Make Coffee Using the 4:6 Brewing Method

## Summary

Authoritative description of the 4:6 Method from Kasuya's own brand, Philocoffea. The method won him the 2016 World Brewers Cup. It divides total water into a 40% phase (controls flavor balance) and a 60% phase (controls strength/concentration), each adjustable independently. Designed for V60 with medium-coarse to coarse grind; intended to be repeatable without relying on technique or experience.

## Key Claims

- The 4:6 split is a **two-axis control system**: first axis = flavor (sweetness/acidity), second axis = strength (concentration)
- Flavor axis: controlled by the ratio between the two pours within the 40% phase
  - First pour < second pour → sweeter
  - First pour > second pour → brighter/more acidic
- Strength axis: controlled by how many pours the 60% phase is divided into
  - 1 pour → lighter; 2 pours → stronger; 3 pours → even stronger
- Designed for medium-coarse to coarse grind; Kasuya recommends coarse
- Uses a 1:15 ratio (20g coffee : 300g water)
- Water: soft purified water, 30–50 mg/L hardness recommended
- Avoid freshly boiled water; temperature varies by roast (see below)
- Each pour should wait until the previous has almost completely drained before the next

## Core Recipe (standard / 5-pour)

**Formula:** Each pour = dose × 3 g. Five equal pours = dose × 15 g total.

For 20g coffee / 300g water:

| Time | Pour | Cumulative | Phase |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | 60 g | 60 g | 40% — pour 1 of 2 |
| 0:45 | 60 g | 120 g | 40% — pour 2 of 2 |
| 1:30 | 60 g | 180 g | 60% — pour 1 of 3 |
| 2:15 | 60 g | 240 g | 60% — pour 2 of 3 |
| 2:45 | 60 g | 300 g | 60% — pour 3 of 3 |
| 3:30 | Remove dripper | | |

## Flavor and Strength Variations

| Profile | 40% phase | 60% phase | Pour sequence (20g/300g) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Standard (balanced, lighter) | 60+60 | 180 (1 pour) | 60+60+180 |
| Sweeter + medium strength | 50+70 | 90+90 (2 pours) | 50+70+90+90 |
| Brighter + stronger | 70+50 | 60+60+60 (3 pours) | 70+50+60+60+60 |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No contradictions with existing wiki. This is the first V60-specific recipe source.
- The standard recipe uses 5 equal pours (60g each for 20g dose). The article also says "40% in 2 pours, 60% in 1 pour" as "standard" in the examples — which produces only 3 pours total (60+60+180). Slight ambiguity: the "standard" illustrated example differs from the "basic formula" equal-pour example. Likely the 5-pour equal version is the teaching default; the 3-pour is a lighter-body shortcut.
- No mention of bloom/pre-infusion step. Most V60 recipes bloom first; 4:6 treats the first pour as the bloom implicitly.
- Grind advice says "medium-coarse to coarse" but also notes the special Kasuya Model V60 dripper (ribs removed at bottom) slows flow to compensate for coarse grind. Standard V60 may need grind adjustment.
