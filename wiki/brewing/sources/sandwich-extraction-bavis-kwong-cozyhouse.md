# Bavis Kwong's Sandwich Extraction — 2026 WBrC 3rd (Cozy House analysis)

## Summary

A Taiwanese SCA educator (暖窩 Cozy House Coffee) analyses Hong Kong competitor **Bavis Kwong**'s 3rd-place 2026 World Brewers Cup routine — **"sandwich extraction" (三明治萃取)**: deliberate **particle-size stratification** in one dripper, coarse grounds on top (percolation → florals/acidity) and a thin layer of fines on the bottom, isolated between two fast filter papers (delayed, low-agitation, near-immersion → sweetness/body). The value isn't "two papers"; it's the reframing of **fines from a defect into a structural tool**, and the explicit **co-design of roast and extraction**. Credibility: **high-medium (reputable practitioner/educator)** — well-reasoned and transparent about its own data gaps.

> **Flags:** (1) **commercial bias** — a course-seller's blog (links the Contour Drip retailer, sells SCA courses). (2) The **recipe is secondhand** competition transcription with acknowledged discrepancies (215g vs 250g water; 94 vs 95 °C) — filed as-claimed, not asserted as canonical. (3) The "Kasuya re-roasted the beans on-site" story is explicitly **unconfirmed** by the author.

## Key Claims

- **Sandwich structure = 3 functions, not a shape:** `1st paper → 2g fines → 2nd paper → 14g coarse`. Water serves the top coarse first, then passes the mid paper and bottom fines. Top (14g coarse) → florals/white-grape/peach/bright acidity; bottom (2g fines, ~12.5%) → honey sweetness, medium body, roundness; mid paper → keeps the two size fractions from remixing.
- **Fines get a job, not the bin:** separated with a **900μm sieve**, a fixed 2g is pinned to the bottom — controllable in weight, position, and start time — rather than migrating through the bed and choking flow / turning bitter before the coarse extracts.
- **Bottom is near-immersion, not sealed:** water still passes; it's lower-agitation, paper-isolated, and delayed, so it behaves more like gentle immersion than the top.
- **Dry bloom delays the bottom (its real purpose):** 15g bloom onto 14g top coarse (~1:1) is sized to **stay in the top layer and not reach the fines** — because fines start extracting the instant they're wet, an early soak = ~40s extra on the bottom (~20% of a 3:30 brew) → sweetness tips to bitterness. Highest-failure step.
- **Dripper as a thermal gradient device:** Contour Drip's **ceramic top** (heat-retentive → drives coarse acidity) + **silicone base** (less retentive → protects bottom fines from prolonged heat). Extends "water temp" from a kettle number to a position-dependent environment — but home brewers shouldn't attribute all of it to material (preheat, room temp, pour speed, bed height, wait times all matter).
- **Roast↔brew are co-designed:** roast is extreme short development (first crack ~8:30, **DTR ~10% → ~57s development**), maximising acid complexity + volatile florals but leaving Maillard sweetness (caramel/nut/cocoa) undeveloped — a "bet on acidity." The **bottom-fines immersion is the structure that adds the sweetness back.**
- **Low-mineral water for direct acidity:** ~**35ppm, pH 6** natural mineral water (deliberately below the SCA 50–175ppm range, target ~68ppm) to keep Geisha acidity sharp/clear — a targeted choice, not a universal setting; and ppm/pH alone don't capture extraction (alkalinity, Ca/Mg, ion composition matter).
- **"White to Red":** hot = peach/white grape/jasmine; cooling → red grape/apricot/honey/citrus. Scoring rewards **consistency between stated and delivered** flavor, so the structure is built to keep sweetness/acidity/texture intact as it cools.
- **Competition ≠ operational:** ~90s–2min prep per cup; a 0.3g miss on the 2g bottom changes its extraction. A comp technique, not a café one.

## Final recipe (as-claimed; 215g version = internally consistent)

16g total (2g fines / 14g coarse, 900μm) · two **Sibarist FAST** papers · 94–95 °C · ~35ppm/pH6 water · dry bloom **15g ~40s** (top only) · **4 × 50g** every 40s (0:40/1:20/2:00/2:40) · done ~3:25–3:30 → **1:13.4**. (A 250g record floats around → 1:15.6, thin — likely transcription drift; author recommends starting from 215g + bypass.)
