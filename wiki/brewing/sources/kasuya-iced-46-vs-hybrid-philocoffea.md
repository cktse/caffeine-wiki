# Iced Coffee: 4:6 vs Hybrid Flash-Chill (Tetsu Kasuya / Philocoffea)

## Summary

Tetsu Kasuya (2016 World Brewers Cup champion) compares two **flash-chill iced** recipes (急冷 *kyūrei* — brew hot, drop directly onto ice), both on the **Hario Switch**, same bean/dose/grind/ice, changing only the pour method: **Recipe A** applies the standard **[[brewing/concepts/46-method|4:6]]** with the water halved; **Recipe B** is a **hybrid** using the Switch's immersion in just two pours. His finding: even at matched strength the two taste **clearly different** — A keeps **sharp acidity**, B is **smoother with rounded, standout sweetness**. Kasuya markedly preferred B ("might change all my shop recipes"). Credibility: **high (reputable practitioner)**; sensory read is his expert tasting (n=1, subjective).

> **Source note:** verbatim YouTube transcript (Japanese) + 4 on-screen recipe slides; typos corrected and Japanese rendered to English on ingest (original terms kept in parentheses for provenance). Bean used: Philocoffea house **"No. 12"** (chosen for year-round availability).

## Method framing — flash-chill (急冷式)

Brew **hot and strong** to extract fully, then **rapidly cool** by pouring directly onto ice — locking in aromatics. Distinct from [[brewing/concepts/cold-brew|cold brew]] (long cold immersion). Both recipes: **20 g coffee · 150 g hot water** (half the 4:6 standard 300 g) · **80 g ice** in the server → ~**1:7.5 hot ratio**, then ice-melt dilutes to serving. Kasuya's prior assumption was that at matched concentration iced results wouldn't differ much by method — the video disproves that.

## Recipe A — 4:6 applied to flash-chill (4:6応用の急冷)

Take the standard [[brewing/concepts/46-method|4:6]] and **halve the water** (300 g → 150 g): replace 60 g × 5 with **30 g × 5**, poured **directly onto 80 g ice** in the server.

| Param | Value |
|---|---|
| Dose | 20 g |
| Hot water | 150 g (30 g → 30 → 30 → 30 → 30) |
| Temp | 90–93 °C |
| Grind | 28 clicks |
| Ice (in server) | 80 g |

- Pour intervals follow the 4:6 cadence (wait for near-drain between pours).
- Kasuya: raise temp to extract fully, then chill hard; add **fresh ice** after brewing to serve; "pouring onto ice is best."

## Recipe B — Hybrid flash-chill (ハイブリッド急冷)

Uses the Switch's **immersion** in only **two pours**.

| Time | Switch | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Open** | Pour 50 g; swirl gently to wet evenly |
| 0:40 | **Closed** | Pour up to 150 g; immersion steep |
| 1:30 | **Open** | Swirl and drain fully (落とし切り) |

Same 20 g / 80 g ice / 28 clicks. **Do not lower the water temp** — with only two pours, cooling the water makes the cup **too weak/thin** (下げすぎると薄くなりやすい). The long closed steep is essential because there are only two pours.

## Key Claims

- **Method changes the cup even in iced, at matched strength.** Kasuya expected little difference; found a clear one.
- **A (4:6 applied):** acidity clearly felt (酸がしっかり), sharp/defined outline (輪郭がはっきり).
- **B (hybrid):** smooth (滑らか), flavors come together (味のまとまり), **sweetness stands out** (甘さが際立つ) — "sweet even though it's iced."
- **Choose by bean** (there is no single "correct" one):
  - Draw out **clean acidity** → **4:6 flash-chill** (good for **Kenya / Ethiopia** — bright, sharp キレ).
  - **Flashy / sweetness-forward beans** → **hybrid flash-chill**.
- **Both are just pour-method changes on the same Hario Switch** — trivial to A/B at home.
- Hybrid-specific: keep temp up (only 2 pours); the closed immersion phase does the work.
