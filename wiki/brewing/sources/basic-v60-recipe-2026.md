# Basic HARIO V60 Recipe — Updated for 2026

## Summary

A 2026 take on the V60 that inverts the conventional grind wisdom. Uses a fine grind (closer to table salt, finer than traditional V60) combined with a fast-flowing filter (Hario V60 Meteor or Sibarist) and 98°C water, enabled by the Hario V60 Drip Assist accessory which distributes water evenly without a gooseneck kettle. The result is a faster brew (2:00–2:30 total) with higher extraction, more clarity, and more pronounced acidity compared to traditional V60 technique.

## Key Claims

- Traditionally, fine grind on V60 caused clogging and over-extraction → fast-flowing filters remove this constraint
- Fast filters (Meteor, Sibarist) allow finer grind without stalling: more solubles extracted, more sweetness and clarity
- 98°C water (near boiling) accelerates extraction to match the shorter contact time
- Hario V60 Drip Assist: sits atop V60, distributes water through small holes → eliminates need for gooseneck kettle or spiral pour technique
- Total brew time 2:00–2:30 — significantly faster than Kasuya's 4:6 (~3:30) despite same dose and ratio
- Philosophy: "removing variables instead of adding complexity"

## The Recipe

| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Dose | 20 g |
| Water | 300 g |
| Ratio | 1:15 |
| Grind | Fine — "finer than traditional V60, closer to table salt" |
| Temperature | 98°C |
| Total time | 2:00–2:30 |
| Required gear | V60 (01 or 02) + Drip Assist + fast filter (Meteor or Sibarist) |

**Pour sequence:**

| Time | Action | Cumulative |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Bloom — 50g | 50g |
| 0:40 | Pour to 100g | 100g |
| 1:00 | Pour to 150g | 150g |
| 1:20 | Pour to 225g | 225g |
| 1:40 | Final pour to 300g | 300g |
| 2:00–2:30 | Draw down complete | |

Rinse filter before brewing. Add Drip Assist on top of V60 after adding coffee. Pour through the Drip Assist.

## Why the System Works

The three components are interdependent:
1. **Fast filter** → removes flow restriction → allows finer grind
2. **Fine grind** → more surface area → higher extraction efficiency
3. **98°C** → accelerates extraction to match faster flow

With standard filters, step 1 is a bottleneck that forces coarser grinds and longer contact time. The Drip Assist handles pour technique so even extraction doesn't require a gooseneck.

These are not contradictory — they are different systems with different equipment assumptions and different flavor targets.

## Cup Character

- Higher extraction than traditional V60
- Cleaner finish; more clarity
- More pronounced acidity; better flavor separation
- Slightly fuller body than ultra-fast brews

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No grind click count given for "fine (table salt)". From [[concepts/comandante-grind-size]]: table salt ≈ medium-fine = ~20 clicks on C40, but this recipe says "finer than traditional V60" which is typically 20–30 clicks. Suggests ~15–20 clicks — needs confirmation.
- 98°C is above the upper range of Kasuya's light-roast recommendation (93°C) and the general V60 guidance in this wiki (previously 88–96°C). Not a contradiction — different filter/grind system changes what temperature is appropriate. Updated V60 concept page to reflect this.
- No guidance on what this recipe does with darker or more developed roasts — 98°C may over-extract medium or dark roast coffees without grind adjustment.
