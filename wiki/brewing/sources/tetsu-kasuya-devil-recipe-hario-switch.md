# Tetsu Kasuya "Devil" Recipe for Hario Switch

## Summary

Step-by-step transcription of Tetsu Kasuya's "devil" Hario Switch recipe. The recipe's defining feature is a deliberate mid-brew temperature drop: hot water (90°C) is used during the open/percolation phase to maximize early extraction, then water is cooled to 70°C before the immersion phase to slow extraction and prevent bitterness. The recipe was developed by Kasuya, 2016 World Brewers Cup champion.

## Key Claims

- Design philosophy: "maximizing extraction early in the brewing process and slowing it down in the later stages"
- The temperature drop is intentional, not a timing artefact — the brewer actively cools the kettle between the second and third pours
- The "devil" naming is unexplained in this source but likely refers to the unusual technique (see Kasuya's original YouTube video for full context)

## The Recipe

**Parameters:**
| Variable | Value |
|---|---|
| Dose | 20 g |
| Water | 280 ml |
| Ratio | 1:14 |
| Temp (phase 1) | 90°C |
| Temp (phase 2) | 70°C |
| Grind | Medium-fine |
| Total time | 3:00 |

**Preparation:** Insert and rinse filter. Add ground coffee. Open switch. Prepare a bottle of cold water (for temperature adjustment).

**Pour sequence:**

| Time | Action | Cumulative water | Switch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Add 60 g hot water | 60 g | Open |
| 0:10 | Wait | — | Open |
| 0:30 | Add to 120 g total (60 g more) | 120 g | Open |
| 0:40 | Add cold water to kettle → cool to 70°C | — | Open |
| 1:15 | Close switch; add to 280 g total (160 g at 70°C) | 280 g | **Closed** |
| 1:30 | Wait | — | Closed |
| 1:45 | Open switch; wait for full drawdown | — | **Open** |
| 3:00 | Done | | |

## Comparison with Christensen Recipe

See [[concepts/neo-switch]] for side-by-side. Key differences:
- Kasuya uses a richer ratio (1:14 vs. 1:15–1:17) but cooler immersion temp (70°C vs. consistent high temp)
- Immersion window: Kasuya 30 sec @ 70°C vs. Christensen 75 sec @ full temp
- Christensen explicitly noted his recipe extracts more than Kasuya's — the temperature mechanics explain why
