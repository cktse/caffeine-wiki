# Pour-Over Variables: Blooming, Pouring & Extraction (Hedrick 2024)

## Summary

Lance Hedrick runs a **controlled bloom-time experiment** (30 s / 1 min / 2 min) on two coffees, measuring TDS, EY, drawdown time, and blind sensory, then reasons about **number of pours** and **pour turbulence** as extraction variables. Headline: longer bloom **monotonically raises extraction** (TDS + EY) and drawdown, but the **taste-optimal bloom is coffee-specific** (gas-dependent) — "more extraction ≠ better." Credibility: **high-medium (reputable practitioner + real data)**; note n = 3 per condition, single session, 2 coffees — illustrative, not definitive.

## The bloom-time data (15 g : 250 g · 50 g bloom then single 200 g pour · no swirl)

**Peru washed** — SO Coffee Roasters, medium-light:

| Bloom | TDS (avg) | EY (avg) | Drawdown | Sensory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 1.18 % | **17.2 %** | 2:03 | **best** (body/sweetness/balance high) |
| 1 min | 1.22 % | 18.0 % | 2:32 | 2nd |
| 2 min | 1.26 % | **18.5 %** | 3:41 | **worst** — flat body/sweetness despite highest EY |

**Kenya washed** — Leuchtfeuer, Nordic light:

| Bloom | TDS (avg) | EY (avg) | Drawdown | Sensory |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 s | 1.40 % | 20.4 % | 2:33 | worst |
| 1 min | 1.44 % | 20.8 % | 3:04 | mid |
| 2 min | 1.47 % | **21.2 %** | 4:08 | **best** — most acidity, balance, sweetness |

## Key Claims

- **Bloom time is an EY lever.** 30 s → 2 min raised EY ~**+1.2 pp** (Peru) / **+0.8 pp** (Kenya), TDS ~+0.07–0.08 %, and drawdown ~**+1:35–1:40**. Consistent across both coffees.
- **Taste-optimal bloom is coffee-specific, not "less/more is better."** Peru best at **30 s** (its lowest EY); Kenya best at **2 min** (its highest EY). The gassier/lighter/fresher the coffee, the longer the bloom it wants.
- **"High and dry" mechanism.** Too-short a bloom on a gassy coffee leaves grounds under-saturated, floating and clinging to the wall — they don't give up solubles *and* don't join the bed's filtration. Proper bloom → CO₂ off-gassed → grounds sink → even extraction + clean cup.
- **Counterintuitive acidity.** Kenya's 2 min pull had the **highest EY *and* the most acidity** — against the "lower EY = brighter" assumption. Proper degassing → even extraction → clarity that *reads* as vibrant acidity; the under-bloomed 30 s was muddy.
- **Off-gassing, not "degassing."** Bloom's job is to expel CO₂ so **diffusion** can proceed; trapped CO₂ inhibits extraction and drives **upward channels** on later pours. Bed "swelling" during bloom is CO₂ release + interstitial water spacing the particles, **not** particle swelling (cites **Samo Smrke**, [[brewing/entities/zhaw-coffee-excellence-center|ZHAW]]).
- **Fewer pours = cleaner (reasoned, no data).** Each pour disturbs the bed → ejects more fines through the filter (muddier cup), clogs filter pores → longer drawdown → more astringency/channeling. Pragmatic advice: **minimize pours, dial the bloom instead.** The **coffee bed itself is a filter** — its efficacy is the number-one clarity tool in pour-over and espresso.
- **Pour turbulence — three types:** **laminar** (pointed, shallow agitation), **turbulent** (pour just below the stream's break-up point → deepest full-bed agitation, most effective), **droplet/osmotic** (broken-up stream, minimal agitation → for dark roasts / deliberate under-extraction).
- **Grinder-agnostic recipe philosophy.** Recipes that specify a grind size fail across grinders because **fines fraction varies** ([[brewing/concepts/particle-size-distribution|PSD]]) → different drawdowns/cups. Better to fix dose/water and manipulate **bloom + pours + timing**.
- **Special cases.** *S-bloom* (cold bloom to preserve VOCs) degasses less efficiently → needs a longer bloom. *Decaf* has little CO₂ (short bloom) but lots of fines (laminar pour, avoid clogging, <3 min drawdown). *Aging* coffee off-gasses → grind finer over time.
- **Flat-bottom note.** Kalita can clog (unless extra holes / open base like SS/Orea); flat pucks (e.g. Pulsar) have a near-static bottom-centre with side bypass → coarser grind + low water column. Hedrick still prefers **V60** for its striated extraction. Corroborates [[brewing/concepts/flat-bottom-drippers]].

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- n = 3, single session, 2 coffees — directional, not conclusive; the two coffees also differ in roaster/roast/age, so "why Peru≠Kenya" is confounded (Hedrick says as much).
- "Fewer pours = cleaner" is reasoned from bed-filtration mechanics, **no data** here (user-flagged).
- Bloom→acidity result (higher EY, more acidity) is one data point against convention — worth its own test.
