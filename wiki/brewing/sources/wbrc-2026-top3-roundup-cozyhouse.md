# 2026 WBrC Top 3: Resistance, Selection, Stratification (Cozy House roundup)

## Summary

Cozy House's own synthesis (黃明志) placing the 2026 WBrC top three side by side: three different answers to the same question, with one shared lesson — **good coffee isn't "extract the most," it's "make the right choices."** Consumer-facing companion to the three deep-dives already filed. Credibility: **high-medium (practitioner/educator)**; derivative of the three primary analyses.

> **Flag:** commercial bias (course-seller). Derived synthesis — cross-references the three source articles rather than adding primary data.

## Key Claims

- **Three philosophies, ~10 pts apart, all Panama Geisha:**
  - 🥇 **Nas Jaafar — Resistance:** make water pass *evenly* through the bed → [[brewing/concepts/flow-resistance]].
  - 🥈 **Simon Gautherin — Clarity Pyramid / Selection:** keep the clean part, discard the too-strong/muddy part → [[brewing/concepts/clarity-pyramid]].
  - 🥉 **Bavis Kwong — Sandwich / Stratification:** separate coarse & fine, each with a job → [[brewing/concepts/sandwich-extraction]].
- **What the contest actually tests:** bean selection, roast understanding, grind, water, brew control — *and* describing the flavor to judges in 15 min. Judges score the **consistency between what's said and what's in the cup** ("say it specifically, hit it exactly → high score").
- **Three trends** (same as the champion piece): (1) **hybrid extraction mainstream** (Switch/Pulsar/Tricolate valve brewers, to sequence aroma→acid→sweet→body); (2) **fines management on stage** (low-RPM grind, level bed, sieving — home instability is often a *fines* problem); (3) **bean narrative changed** (altitude+fermentation+brew as one logic; "why," not just "how good").
- **The common thread:** all three first decided *what they wanted to taste*, then arranged every action backward — the wiki's own sensory-first, variable-chain principle.
- **Green homogenization caveat:** all six finalists used Panama Geisha → when the green variable is equalized, competition drifts toward *budget/resources*; Bavis's "solve with structure, not with a more expensive bean" is valuable against that.

## Home practice (shared across all three)

Same bean, brew three times: (1) baseline; (2) finer / slower pour → longer stay (feel "selection" + "resistance"); (3) same grind but +10s bloom & more back-pours → longer contact (feel how sweetness is "grown"). Compare aroma/acid/sweet/body/finish side by side.
