# Scott Rao Masterclass on Blooming Espresso, Filter 3 & what about Quakers?

**Source**: Scott Rao (SR) and John Buckman (JB). Decent Espresso masterclass. YouTube transcript. 2025-08-30.
**Credibility**: **High-Medium (practitioner)** — Scott Rao is the established practitioner authority in specialty coffee (see [[roasting/entities/scott-rao]]); John Buckman is co-founder of Decent Espresso (hardware authority on Decent-specific techniques). Masterclass/video transcript format — not peer-reviewed; some claims from personal experience (nylon filter experiment, quaker sorting percentages). Jonathan Gagné's astringency mechanism is cited second-hand. No COI concern (Rao is not selling Decent Espresso; JB's preference for Allongé is personal taste).

**Coffee served**: Ruby Chiroso (Colombian Chiroso by Wilder Lasso; notable: dried on beds of lemon grass → carries lemon grass aroma, not infused — an unusual microprocessing choice connecting to [[varietals/concepts/chiroso]])

## Summary

A Decent Espresso masterclass covering three light-roast espresso/filter strategies (Blooming, Allongé, Filter3), a detailed discussion of quakers (sources, sorting, cup impact), and a precise account of the three sources of astringency in coffee. The most transferable content is the astringency framework (channeling, quakers, underdevelopment/CGA), which applies across brew methods regardless of machine. The quaker content is relevant at the green bean purchase stage. The Blooming/Filter3/Allongé techniques are specific to the Decent Espresso machine.

## Key Claims

### Three Sources of Astringency (Rao)

Rao identifies three sources in order of prevalence:

**1. Channeling during percolation** — most common by far; applies to espresso and filter.

Mechanism (via Jonathan Gagné's theory): Astringent particles are *suspended* (not dissolved) and are larger than other extractable compounds. They only exit the coffee bed in large numbers when there is an easy route — a channel, a wider path. Evidence: nylon filters removed 75–80% of astringency → suspended particles, not dissolved. High-extraction can be achieved without astringency as long as there is no channeling.

This reframes the "over-extraction causes astringency" belief: it is channel-extracted suspended particles, not dissolved compounds from high EY.

**2. Quakers** (immature beans) — applies in any brewing method; even one quaker in a cupping bowl without channeling disrupts the cup. See [[shared/quakers]] for full concept page.

**3. Severe underdevelopment during roasting** — excess chlorogenic acid (CGA) remains unbroken-down.

"Chlorogenic acid, which is the most prevalent acid in coffee, is somewhat astringent, and during roasting it breaks down, and if you underdevelop, you end up with too much chlorogenic acid and then you notice the astringency from it." (Rao)

**Cross-domain connection to roasting**: CGA is our established cross-domain chain compound. Underdevelopment (too-light roast, or specifically very short DT or low DCT) is the risk. This is consistent with the Alstrup 2020 finding that fast DT at constant Agtron color gives higher CGA — though Alstrup's Fast profile at Agtron 76 is not underdeveloped in the same sense (it holds same color). True underdevelopment = dropping below minimum viable roast threshold, likely below Agtron 80+ where roasting-phase CGA destruction hasn't yet occurred adequately. See [[roasting/concepts/roasting-parameters]] for CGA section.

**Decaffeination**: The decaffeination process removes 90%+ of astringent compounds — this is why decaff can be pushed to extreme extraction methods (Filter2: 24–26% EY) without astringency risk even with percolation approaches that would normally cause channeling.

### Quakers — Full Account

Full content filed at [[shared/quakers]]. Key claims summary:
- Immature seeds from underripe cherries; lighter color, smoother texture post-roast
- Smell test: smash on table — fruity = not quaker; nutty = quaker
- Cup impact: truncated aftertaste (dry sensation stops the oily smooth finish); even one quaker in a cupping bowl (no channeling) causes this
- Washed < Naturals for quaker prevalence (float test in washed channel removes floaters/quakers before drying)
- Cause: inadequate soil nutrition from insufficient rainfall preventing fertilization
- Rao sorts ~20% of his green by weight; typical producers remove <3%
- Optical sorter: camera detects color threshold on falling beans, compressed air jet ejects rejects individually; also used in rice/grain industry
- Risk of over-sorting: borderline beans that aren't quakers may be the most intensely fruity → some quaker sorting is optimal; cost-benefit requires 5-bowl blind comparison

### Blooming Espresso (Decent Espresso specific)

**Origin**: Rao's adaptation of pour-over pre-wet/bloom technique to espresso, made possible by Decent's ability to stop flow while maintaining pressure.

**Cannot do on standard machines**: A standard machine's 3-way valve opens on flow-stop, releasing pressure and sucking slurry back through the group head. Decent maintains pressure without flow.

**Structure**:
1. Pressurized preinfusion — fills puck with water rapidly (water reaches bottom of puck in ~2 seconds)
2. **30-second pause** — pressure held but flow stopped (passive pressure drop visible); puck sits saturated; material dissolves into the standing liquid
3. High-flow percolation phase — pushes the dissolved material out

**Why it works for light roasts**:
- Long preinfusion gives maximum contact time → high extraction
- High-flow percolation phase aligns with Rao's principle that lighter roasts taste better at higher flow rates (more fruitiness)
- Combines high-EY via contact time AND flavor from high flow — two strategies in one

**Results**: EY up to 29–30% with very light roasts (Kenyans, Ethiopians); "sometimes incredibly delicious"

**Practical notes**:
- Requires fine grind (risk of overpressure → use flow limiter)
- Headspace recommended: ~1% higher EY vs. no headspace; allows puck to swell (echoes V60 bloom swelling)
- "Challenging to dial in" — Rao's own assessment
- Fine grind limiter (pressure-based flow control) has made Blooming more practical in recent machine versions

### Allongé (Light-Roast Long Espresso)

A high-flow, high-solvent long espresso that works well with light roasts.

**Why it works**: For light roasts (denser structure, more resistant to giving up solubles quickly), pushing a large volume of water through the puck increases total solvent — high EY through volume rather than time. High flow rate also brings out fruitiness in light roasts.

**Characteristics**: ~2.5× more concentrated than Filter3; vibrant, some astringency risk (100g through 20g puck = challenging without some channeling); JB's preference: "beautiful balance, top notes and warming notes"; audience: "a little muddier [than Filter3] but more interesting on the nose, apricot kernels"

### Filter3 (Decent Espresso specific)

**What it is**: Decent Espresso configured as a no-bypass filter brewer. Water dispensed at no pressure (Decent-specific capability).

**Setup**: 22g coffee in ~8.5cm deep basket; paper filter below; water drizzled in at no pressure; no bypass path → every drop passes through the coffee bed

**Grind**: Extremely coarse — coarsest setting on most grinders. Counter-intuitive for espresso operators.

**Brew time**: ~4 minutes regardless of grind setting (within the acceptable range)

**Sensory character**: Clean, floral, "very floral on the nose"; rarely astringent; lower concentration (~2.5× more dilute than Allongé); JB: "muted, a little dusty, middle missing"; audience member: "I started with something really dark and hit back and go, is to make Filter3. I love it." Use case: best for very light roasts where clarity > concentration is desired.

**No-bypass advantage**: V60 allows 20–30% bypass (water exiting without passing through grounds). Filter3 eliminates this → more even extraction; allows coarser grind; lower astringency risk.

**Practical tips**:
- Check liquid level at 100 seconds (should be halfway up basket); adjust grind based on this
- Use Bluetooth scale: stop by weight 20g before target; set recipe to overshoot (Decent calculates flow inaccurately at no pressure, especially on first 1-2 brews)
- If bed dries out near end of brew: increase flow rate in last 30 seconds

**Application**: Very useful for decaff (no astringency risk + very high EY 24–26%)

### Pre-infusion Mechanics (General Principle)

**Slow preinfusion is not better**: A slow pre-infusion rate means the top of the puck contacts water long before the bottom — the top over-extracts while the bottom is just starting. Maximally uneven extraction along the puck depth.

**Fast/maximum flow preinfusion is better**: Pressurized fast preinfusion fills the puck in ~2 seconds (water visible on underside within 2 seconds in Blooming). Entire puck contacts solvent simultaneously → more even extraction across puck depth.

JB's mistake: original Decent profiles ran 4 ml/sec preinfusion (the rate at which the puck can absorb water). This led to the Decent's reputation as a "channeling monster." Fix: maximum flow rate for pre-infusion dramatically reduced channeling and improved taste.

**Headspace**: ~1% higher EY with headspace during long pre-infusion (allows puck to swell; prevents air pockets being compressed into channels). Standard espresso practice of "dry pucks" (no headspace) may not be optimal for light-roast blooming.

### Shower Screen Design (Advanced / Decent-Specific)

Stéphane's radial extraction research: outer ring of the puck receives the least water and has the lowest extraction (~17% in a puck averaging 20% EY). Decent Espresso's Dimples/Freckles shower screens intentionally deliver more water to the outer ring to compensate — "even on the puck, not even on the shower screen."

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Gagné's astringency theory (suspended particles via channels) has not been verified in peer-reviewed literature as far as this wiki knows. Nylon filter experiment is compelling but unpublished personal experience. File as high-medium credibility.
- "Light roasts taste better with higher flow rates" — Rao states this as a general principle; mechanism is intuited (high flow brings out fruitiness) but not specifically sourced in this session.
- Is the underdevelopment-CGA-astringency link specifically about Agtron threshold, DCT, or DT? Rao's statement is qualitative. This wiki's sourced CGA data (Lindsey 2024, Alstrup 2020) addresses roast degree and DT but not the minimum development threshold for astringency avoidance specifically.
