# Sensory Analysis of Full Immersion Coffee: Cold Brew is More Floral, and Less Bitter, Sour, and Rubbery than Hot Brew

**Authors**: Batali ME, Lim LX, Liang J, Yeager SE, Thompson AN, Han J, Ristenpart WD, Guinard J-X
**Published**: *Foods* 2022 (doi: 10.3390/foods11162440) — open access
**Summarized in**: SCA "25" Magazine, Issue 19, 2023-05-02 (Mary Basco summary, lead author commentary by Batali)
**Institution**: UC Davis Coffee Center; postdoctoral research Jan–Dec 2021
**Funders**: Coffee Science Foundation, Toddy LLC
**Credibility**: High — peer-reviewed paper in *Foods* (MDPI open access); UC Davis Coffee Center; rigorous full factorial design; 14 trained sensory panelists × 27 samples × 3 evaluations = 1,134 evaluations; descriptive analysis (not preference); wiki entry summarizes from SCA article but references the full paper.

## Summary

A controlled, systematic study comparing full-immersion brewing at three water temperatures (4°C, 22°C, 92°C) across three origins (El Salvador honey, Ethiopia washed, Sumatra wet-hulled) and three roast levels (light/medium/dark), all diluted to a fixed 2% TDS and served chilled. Cold brew is confirmed more floral and less bitter/sour/rubbery than hot brew. Cold brew is confirmed less acidic (higher pH). **Cold brew is NOT confirmed sweeter** — sweetness was not among the significant differences found. Roast level and origin effects were larger than brewing temperature effects.

## Study Design

- 3 green coffees: El Salvador Las Ranas honey (ELS); Ethiopia Guji washed organic (ETH); Sumatra Fair Trade Organic wet-hulled (SUM)
- 3 roast levels: Light (Agtron Gourmet 58), Medium (Agtron 48), Dark (Agtron 38)
- 3 brew temperatures: Refrigerated 4°C, Ambient 22°C, Hot 92°C
- Total: 27 samples
- Brew method: full immersion (Toddy Cupping Kits, paper filters), 5:1 water:coffee ratio
- Brewed to equilibrium TDS (not fixed time): 36h @ 4°C, 12h @ 22°C, 1h @ 92°C
- All samples diluted to 2% TDS and chilled before serving
- 14 trained panelists, descriptive analysis (0–100 intensity scale, 27 attributes, remote evaluation during COVID-19)

## Key Claims

### Temperature effects (all coffees, all roasts)
- Cold brew more **floral**; less **bitter**, less **sour**, less **rubbery** than hot brew
- Cold brew higher pH (less acidic) — confirmed both chemically and sensorially
- **Sweetness: not addressed directly.** The article identifies four key temperature-driven differences (bitter/sour/rubbery/floral). Sweetness is absent from this list — but the article never explicitly states sweetness was measured and found non-different. The full 27-attribute descriptive ballot is not disclosed. Additionally, the SCA sweetness intensity scale was only added to cupping forms in September 2023; this study was conducted January–December 2021. Whether sweetness was a scored attribute, and at what granularity, is unknown. **Do not infer "cold brew is not sweeter."**
- Separately: reduced bitterness (confirmed) can produce a *perceived* sweetness increase via sensory modulation, even without any change in sweet-tasting compounds — see [[shared/sweetness-perception]]
- Refrigerated (4°C) and ambient (22°C) cold brew: **no perceptible sensory difference** — panelists couldn't tell them apart

### Equilibrium TDS findings
- Hot (92°C): TDS 4.27%, extraction yield (EY) 22.28%
- Ambient (22°C): TDS 3.98%, EY 20.74%
- Refrigerated (4°C): TDS 3.76%, EY 19.55%
- Even at equilibrium, cold water extracts slightly less than hot — some compounds do not extract at low temperatures regardless of time
- Light roast reached slightly higher TDS/EY than medium/dark at all temperatures

### Roast × temperature interactions (beyond baseline cold/hot differences above)
| Roast | Additional cold vs. hot difference |
|---|---|
| **Light** (Agtron 58) | None beyond baseline — cleanest interaction |
| **Medium** (Agtron 48) | Hot brew adds "burnt" and "whiskey" flavors |
| **Dark** (Agtron 38) | Cold brew adds "woody" flavor |

### Origin × temperature interactions
| Origin | Hot brew character | Cold brew character |
|---|---|---|
| Ethiopia washed (ETH) | More "burnt" | More **"fruity"** |
| El Salvador honey (ELS) | — | More "woody" |
| Sumatra wet-hulled (SUM) | More **"smoky"** | Less smoky/earthy |

### Hierarchy of effect sizes
Origin and roast level produced larger sensory differences than brew temperature. The study's key conclusion: **the coffee you choose matters more than whether you cold brew it**.

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- "Cold brew is sweeter" is one of the most common consumer claims about cold brew — this study found no such sweetness difference when TDS and extraction are controlled. The perceived sweetness of commercial cold brew products likely reflects: (a) added sugar/sweeteners, (b) dark roast where bitterness suppression reads as "sweetness," or (c) fruity-origin selection (e.g., Ethiopian) that enhances perceived sweetness via aromatic pathway per [[shared/sweetness-perception]]
- The study could not fully equalize equilibrium TDS across temperatures — "differences perceived may relate to extraction yield rather than brewing temperature." Further research planned.
- Consumer panelists not tested — trained panelists may perceive differently from general public
- Toddy paper filter used throughout; different filtration (metal vs. paper) may change results, especially for body/texture attributes
- Only one example of each processing type tested; generalizing honey process or washed findings across all origins requires caution
