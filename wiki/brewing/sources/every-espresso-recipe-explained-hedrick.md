# Every Espresso Recipe Explained (Lance Hedrick)

## Summary

A reputable-educator walkthrough (YouTube) of **six espresso "styles"** pullable on any **stock 9-bar machine** — ristretto, normale, lungo, allongé, turbo, and sprover (café crème) — holding **dose, temperature, coffee, and pressure constant** and changing only **yield (ratio)** and **grind**. Each shot is measured (grind, dose, yield, TDS, EY), giving a concrete concentration/extraction map across the spectrum. Credibility: **high-medium (reputable practitioner/educator)**; the numbers are single illustrative pulls (n=1 each), not a controlled dataset — treat the *pattern* as the takeaway, not the exact decimals.

> **Note on the raw file:** the transcript was pasted from a YouTube auto-caption and carries expected typos (ristretto/"restredo", astringency/"aringency", lungo/"Luno", portafilter/"poura filter", etc.); corrected here. ## Key Claims

- **One machine, six drinks:** all six are the *same* 9-bar shot with only **ratio and grind** changed (same dose/temp/coffee). Ristretto → Normale → Lungo share **one grind (1)** and differ only by where you stop; Allongé/Turbo/Sprover progressively **coarsen**.
- **Grind sets the extraction ceiling, not just flow:** because coarser grounds cap extraction at a given contact time, **Turbo** (coarse 1.5, 1:2.75) extracts *less* (EY 18.2%) than **Lungo** (fine 1, 1:3, EY 22.1%) despite a similar ratio; **Allongé** (coarse 1.3, huge 1:5.6) only *matches* Lungo (~22%) because the coarse grind offsets the large solvent volume. EY is therefore **not** monotonic with yield.
- **Ristretto** (stop early, ~1:1.5): higher concentration, bigger body, longer-lasting coat; can extract as evenly as normale (his 28.3 g pull read as efficient as the 38.9 g normale).
- **Lungo** (1:3–1:4, same grind, run longer): more extraction, smoother, more diluted, thinner crema ratio; runs **hotter** (less puck buffer); astringency can creep in. Hedrick prefers **diluting a ristretto with water** over pushing more water through the puck.
- **Allongé** (attrib. **Scott Rao**, Montreal ~20 yrs ago; for light roasts): ~1:5–1:6, 30–40 s, coarser. Even on a fixed 9-bar machine it self-profiles — pressure **rose to ~8 bar then cascaded down** as bed resistance fell. TDS lands **between filter and espresso**; bright, fruit-forward. Recommend **cooler temp (80–85 °C)** for light roasts to curb astringency.
- **Turbo** (attrib. **Christopher H. Hendon & Michael Cameron** paper): 7–15 s, originally 16 g dose, ~1:3, coarse grind → more even extraction; EY rivals normal espresso at lower TDS. **Debunks the "needs a 6-bar OPV" myth:** a 7–15 s shot physically can't build 6 bar (this hit **5 bar**); any machine — even a 20-bar pump — gives turbo because inadequate flow can't build pressure. Hedrick's **favorite** ("best shot of the day"); most fruity/aromatic, biggest acid presentation, preserves VOCs.
- **Sprover / café crème / "Texas filter"** (attrib. **Matt Perger**, 2013 WBC): ~1:8–1:12 (here 1:11.4), very coarse (2.5), espresso machine used as a **no-bypass filter brewer**. Near-complete/even extraction; foam is mocha-pot-like, **not true crema**. Recommend cooler temp; clean up with a **bottom paper filter** or by pouring through a V60.
- **Temperature × yield:** the bigger the yield, the hotter the cup; for big-yield styles (allongé, sprover) go **cooler** — "a lot of what we love comes out at lower temps (~85 °C)"; high temp + big yield → astringency/bitterness.
- **Puck-prep scales with grind:** the coarser you go, the **less puck prep matters** (fine = critical; coarse = forgiving). Use a spouted portafilter for messy high-yield styles.
- **EY dogma challenged:** Hedrick often drinks **15–18% EY**, not the 18–22% convention; **ratio is the biggest dictator** of EY (with grind/temp/coffee).
- **Summary TDS bands:** Normale 9–12% · Ristretto 11–15% · Lungo 7–8% · Allongé 5–8% · Turbo 6.5–8% · Sprover 1.5–2.5%.
- **Roast pairing (implied):** darker roasts suit **normale/ristretto** (body, puck integrity, crema); lighter roasts suit **turbo/allongé/sprover** (bright/fruity, but harder to extract — need a capable grinder).

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Allongé EY vs. Rao:** [[brewing/concepts/espresso-fundamentals]] records Rao's allongé at **26–27% EY** (Decent, 4.5 ml/s flow control); Hedrick measured **22.25%** at 1:5.6 on a **stock 9-bar** machine with a coarser grind. Not a true contradiction — different equipment/grind/coffee — but a reminder that "allongé" spans a wide EY depending on flow control and grind.
- **Sprover vs. Filter3:** the sprover (coarse 9-bar, no bypass) is conceptually the stock-machine cousin of Rao's **Filter3** (0-bar Decent no-bypass brewer). Same "espresso machine as filter" idea; different pressure regime.
- Single-pull measurements (n=1) — the decimals (e.g. 19.99% vs 22.06%) shouldn't be over-read.
