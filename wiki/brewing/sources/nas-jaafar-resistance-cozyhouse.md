# Nas Jaafar's Resistance — 2026 WBrC Champion (Cozy House analysis)

## Summary

Cozy House Coffee (暖窩, 黃明志) breaks down the 2026 World Brewers Cup **champion**, Malaysia's **Nas Jaafar** (the first Malaysian to win). His entire routine hangs on one word — **Resistance**: flow rate is set by the *structure* the water passes through, not by how much you pour, so the job is to make the bed's resistance **even**, not minimal. Credibility: **high-medium (reputable practitioner/educator)**; recipe as-claimed. Especially useful because the classroom "resistance" lens explains most home-brew failures.

> **Flag:** commercial bias (course-seller blog). Recipe/figures are secondhand competition transcription — as-claimed.

## Podium & context

2026 WBrC, Brussels, Jun 25–27. Final: **1 Nas Jaafar (Malaysia) · 2 Simon Gautherin (Australia) · 3 Bavis Kwong (Hong Kong)** · 4 Jackie Tran (Czechia) · 5 Ethan Junseong Park (Korea) · 6 Angie Molina (France). Same week: Andy Philein (China) won Coffee in Good Spirits; Benjamin Brassart (Belgium) won World Roasting. Top three within ~10 pts; all six finalists used Panama Geisha.

## Key Claims

- **"Resistance" opening demo:** three beakers of different materials/particle sizes, same water poured → wildly different flow. **Flow rate is decided by the bed the water crosses, not the volume poured.**
- **Most home brewers train the wrong end:** they perfect the *input* (steady stream, pretty circles, hitting the time) but taste is set by the *channel* — how evenly water passes through the bed. A pretty pour over a clogged/uneven bed is still bitter.
- **The triad:** **flow rate · resistance · contact time.** Illustrated by three classroom failure cases:
  - **Right time, but sour-front / bitter-tail / hollow-middle** → *uneven resistance*: a fines-packed zone under-extracts (sharp sour) while a low-resistance channel over-extracts (bitter) — both in one cup; perfect total time can't save it. Tell from the spent bed (uneven height, crater, muddy surface). Fix = make resistance **even** (WDT, low-RPM grind), not minimal. **Fix order: level bed → distribute pour → stop over-circling → grind *last*.**
  - **Great aroma, watery/thin** → flow too fast, contact time too short; aromatics elute but sweetness/body compounds don't dissolve in time. Champion's closed-valve steep fixes it; without a Switch, slow the later pours / pulse / add 5–10s bloom.
  - **Copied the valve-close, got muddy/astringent** → contact time is double-edged; his 1-min immersion works *because* of low fines + level bed + 92 °C. **Copy the logic, not the parameters** — start with a 20–30s close and calibrate by taste.
- **Terroir framed as resistance:** high-altitude cold → slow cherry maturation (natural resistance); sealed low-oxygen anaerobic ferment (designed resistance) → flavor density. He strings altitude → fermentation → brewing into one logic rather than piling adjectives.
- **Three trends (Cozy House):** (1) **hybrid extraction is going mainstream** — Switch / Pulsar / Tricolate valve-control brewers, to *sequence* aroma→acid→sweet→body; (2) **fines management moved to the front** — low-RPM grinding + WDT (ex-espresso details) now in brewing comp; home inconsistency is often a *fines* problem, not technique; (3) **the way of talking about beans changed** — "why," not just "how good."

## Recipe (as-claimed)

15g / 200g / **1:13** / **92 °C** · **UFO V3** dripper on a **Hario Switch** base · WDT · grind ~**700μm**, low grinder RPM to reduce fines.

| Time | Action | Mode |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | pour 1st 100g in circles | percolation (valve open) |
| 0:58 | close Switch valve | — |
| 1:00 | pour 2nd 100g | immersion (steep) |
| 2:00 | open valve, drain | — |
| 2:10 | done (total 2:10) | — |

Front fast flow → aroma + bright acidity; back immersion → sweetness + body. Two modes sequenced in one cup: **clean → sweet → structure.**
