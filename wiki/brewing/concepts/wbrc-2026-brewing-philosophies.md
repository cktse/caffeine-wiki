# 2026 WBrC: Three Brewing Philosophies & Trends

## Definition

A synthesis hub for the 2026 World Brewers Cup (Brussels) top three — three different answers to one question, within ~10 points, **all on Panama Geisha**. The shared lesson: **good coffee isn't "extract the most," it's "make the right choices"** — decide the target cup first, then arrange every action backward (the wiki's own sensory-first, variable-chain principle; cf. [[overview]]).

## The three philosophies

| Place | Competitor | Keyword | Core idea | Deep dive |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 🥇 1st | Nas Jaafar (Malaysia) | **Resistance** | Make water pass *evenly* through the bed (input vs channel; even ≠ minimal) | [[brewing/concepts/flow-resistance]] |
| 🥈 2nd | Simon Gautherin (Australia) | **Selection** | Keep the clean part, **discard** the too-strong front / muddy tail | [[brewing/concepts/clarity-pyramid]] |
| 🥉 3rd | Bavis Kwong (Hong Kong) | **Stratification** | Separate coarse & fine, **each with a job** | [[brewing/concepts/sandwich-extraction]] |

Read as a set: **addition/structure** (Bavis stratifies), **subtraction/selection** (Simon discards), **uniformity/control** (Nas evens the flow) — three levers on the same extraction.

## What the contest tests

Not "who pours prettiest." WBrC scores bean selection, roast understanding, grind, water, brew control — *and* describing the flavor to judges in 15 min. Points go to the **consistency between what's said and what's in the cup**: specific + accurate = high; pretty + off = heavily docked. This is why every top-tier action has an explicit purpose.

## Three trends (Cozy House)

1. **Hybrid extraction is going mainstream.** Pure V60 percolation is no longer the only answer; valve/no-bypass brewers (**Hario Switch, NextLevel Pulsar, Tricolate**) let a barista *sequence* aroma → acid → sweet → body as bean flavor layering gets finer. See [[brewing/concepts/flow-resistance]] (steep-then-release), [[brewing/concepts/clarity-pyramid]] (no-bypass Pulsar).
2. **Fines management moved to the front.** Low-RPM grinding, level beds, even sieving — former espresso details now decide brewing-comp cups. Good news for home brewers: instability is often a **fines** problem, not a technique problem.
3. **The bean narrative changed.** Champions string altitude + fermentation + brew into one logic instead of piling adjectives — consumers want **"why," not just "how good."**

## Green homogenization caveat

All six finalists used Panama Geisha. When the green variable is equalized, competition drifts toward **budget/resources** (Finca Nuguo, ~200 kg/yr, was used by two finalists). This is why "solve it with **structure/technique**, not a more expensive bean" (Bavis) is worth defending — and it matters for the wiki's green-selection focus: variety/terroir set the ceiling, but technique is where a fixed bean is still shaped.

## Home practice (feel all three)

Same bean, three brews: (1) **baseline**; (2) **slower** (finer ½ step or slower pour → longer stay — feel *selection* + *resistance*); (3) **longer contact** (same grind, +10s bloom, more back-pours — feel sweetness "grown"). Compare aroma / acid / sweet / body / finish. More instructive than memorising any champion's numbers.
