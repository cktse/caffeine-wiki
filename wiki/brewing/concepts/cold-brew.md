# Cold Brew

## Definition

A brewing method using room-temperature or refrigerated water in a full immersion setup for an extended period (typically 12–36 hours), as opposed to brewing with hot water and then chilling. The result is filtered and often diluted to serving strength. Distinguished from iced coffee (brewed hot, poured over ice) and iced americano (espresso diluted with cold water).

## What Cold Brew Actually Does

Common claims about cold brew are mostly supported — with one important exception.

| Claim | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Cold brew is **less bitter** | ✓ Confirmed (Batali et al. 2022, UC Davis) |
| Cold brew is **less sour** | ✓ Confirmed |
| Cold brew is **less acidic** | ✓ Confirmed — higher pH than hot brew, both chemically and sensorially |
| Cold brew has more **floral** character | ✓ Confirmed |
| Cold brew is **sweeter** | ⚠️ **Ambiguous — not directly addressed.** The study identified four temperature-driven differences; sweetness is absent from the list. But the full 27-attribute ballot is undisclosed, the SCA sweetness intensity scale did not exist until September 2023 (post-study), and reduced bitterness can produce *perceived* sweetness increase via sensory modulation even without a direct effect on sweet-tasting compounds. |
| Cold brew is **smoother** | Not formally tested; "less rubbery" was confirmed, which partially supports this claim |

**On sweetness — what we can and cannot say**: this study does not establish that cold brew is sweeter, but it also does not establish that it is not sweeter. What it does confirm is that cold brew reduces bitterness and acidity — and both of these, per sensory science ([[shared/sweetness-perception]]), can cause a *perceived* increase in sweetness even without any change in sweet-tasting compounds. So cold brew may well feel sweeter to drinkers, just not for the reason commonly assumed. Additionally, cold brewing fruity origins (e.g., Ethiopian washed) emphasizes fruity aromatics, which further enhances perceived sweetness via the aromatic pathway.

## Practical Parameters

Based on the Batali et al. (2022) equilibrium extraction study:

| Parameter | Value | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Brew ratio** | 5:1 (water:coffee by weight) | For concentrate; dilute to ~2% TDS for serving |
| **Brew time — refrigerated (4°C)** | ~36h to equilibrium | Same sensory result as ambient; less efficient |
| **Brew time — ambient (22°C)** | ~12h to equilibrium | **Recommended** — identical sensory profile to refrigerated, 24h faster |
| **Brew time — hot (92°C)** | ~1h to equilibrium | Hot brew, obviously not cold brew |
| **Filtration** | Paper filter | Removes oils and fine particles; produces cleaner result |
| **Serving** | Chilled | All cold/ambient brew samples in this study were served chilled |

**Practical note**: ambient temperature (22°C) produces the same sensory profile as refrigerated (4°C) and is 24 hours faster. There is no reason to refrigerate during the brew unless ambient temperature exceeds ~25°C or the brewing duration extends beyond 12 hours.

## Extraction Behavior

Cold water extracts less than hot, even at equilibrium:

| Temperature | Equilibrium TDS | Extraction Yield |
|---|---|---|
| 92°C (hot) | 4.27% | 22.28% |
| 22°C (ambient) | 3.98% | 20.74% |
| 4°C (refrigerated) | 3.76% | 19.55% |

Some compounds simply do not dissolve at cold temperatures regardless of brew time. This is why cold brew always requires dilution benchmarking — you cannot assume equivalent extraction from equal weights of coffee and water.

Light roasts extract slightly more than medium/dark at all temperatures.

## Best Coffees for Cold Brew

### By Roast Level

| Roast (Agtron) | Cold brew behavior | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|
| **Light (Agtron 58)** | No interaction beyond baseline; cleanest result | **Best for cold brew** |
| **Medium (Agtron 48)** | Hot version adds burnt/whiskey; cold is cleaner | Acceptable; cold is better than hot for this roast |
| **Dark (Agtron 38)** | Cold brew adds "woody" character | Avoid — cold brew is not better than hot at dark roast |

Light roast is the clear winner for cold brew — it produces the baseline floral/less bitter/less sour cold brew profile without additional roast-level complications.

### By Origin (Controlled Study Findings)

| Origin (from study) | Hot brew emphasis | Cold brew emphasis | Cold brew verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethiopia washed** | Burnt notes | **Fruity** character | **Best** — cold brew elevates the varietal's fruity/floral character |
| **El Salvador honey** | — | Woody | Neutral to negative — honey character may be better expressed hot |
| **Sumatra wet-hulled** | **Smoky** character | Less smoky | Mixed — cold brew suppresses the characteristic Sumatran profile |

**Ethiopia washed + light roast** is the clearest cold brew recommendation from the evidence. The cold brew fruity emphasis, combined with the sweetness research showing fruity aromatics enhance perceived sweetness ([[shared/sweetness-perception]]), explains why this combination feels "sweet and smooth" — the cold brew preserves fruity aromatics that are independently associated with sweetness perception.

**For Sumatra wet-hulled**: hot brewing is better if you want the characteristic earthy/smoky Sumatran profile. Cold brewing produces a different coffee — less earthy, less smoky — which may or may not be desired.

## Hierarchy of Effect: What Matters Most

The study's most practically useful finding: **origin and roast level produce larger sensory differences than brew temperature**. Cold brewing a mediocre coffee does not transform it; a high-quality Ethiopian washed at light roast tastes better both hot and cold than a mediocre coffee cold-brewed.

This directly supports the wiki's research priority of green bean selection > brewing method.

## Brewing Overview Integration

Cold brew sits outside the standard pour-over/espresso framework. It is a distinct method best understood as:
- Full immersion (not percolation)
- Temperature as the primary extraction variable (not grind size or pour technique)
- Extended time compensates for temperature
- Sweetness and acidity are not the differentiators marketing claims — floral character and reduced bitterness/sourness are the actual differences

## Open Questions

- Does paper filtration vs. metal filtration (e.g., French press mesh) significantly change cold brew sensory profile? The study used paper only.
- At what ambient temperature does cold brew quality degrade? The study used 22°C — does 28–30°C in a hot climate change the result?
- How does the 12h ambient brew compare to shorter brew times (4–8h)? Equilibrium extraction takes 12h; sub-equilibrium brews may have different profiles.
- Ethiopian washed produces more fruity in cold brew — does the same hold for Ethiopian natural, or does the fermented character behave differently at cold temperatures?
