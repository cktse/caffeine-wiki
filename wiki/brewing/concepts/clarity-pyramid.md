# Clarity Pyramid & Selective Extraction

## Definition

Two linked ideas from Simon Gautherin's 2026 WBrC (2nd) routine:

1. **Clarity Pyramid** — a four-stage philosophy: *green coffee **creates** → roasting **unlocks** → brewing **selects** → water **highlights**.* Each layer must correspond: water can't add a peach the green never had; brewing can only select among the aromas the roast preserved.
2. **Selective extraction** — the "brewing selects" stage made concrete: **discard the front**, **cut the tail**. The goal is *subtraction* — keep the legible fruit/florals, remove what muddies them — not maximum yield.

This framework maps almost one-to-one onto this wiki's own thesis (region/varietal/processing → roast → brew → water → sensory). See [[brewing/overview]] and [[overview]].

## What "clarity" means (and doesn't)

Simon's observation: the cups people most readily understand and love tend to be **high flavor legibility, lower extraction yield, lower concentration, higher minerality**. That's the **low-TDS / low-PE corner of the [[brewing/concepts/brewing-control-chart|Brewing Control Chart]]** (the 'sweet'/clarity region, and the majority consumer "dome" cluster).

**Clarity ≠ weak.** A thin cup has fewer clashing flavors, but that may just be **under-extraction**. Real clarity is when sweetness, acidity, aroma, and body are all *present* yet individually *locatable*.

## Selective extraction — the technique

| Move | What / why | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| **Front-discard** (~10g) | The first flow off the valve is the most concentrated/intense; in a long-steep, very-coarse, low-agitation brew it can mask the peach sweetness + silky texture. Caught in a separate cup, not served. | On a V60 (normal percolation, short bloom) the first drops are a *different* condition and often hold lovely aroma/acidity — don't copy the rule blindly. |
| **Tail cut-off** (~140g of 200g) | Stop before the low-concentration tail's woody/cocoa/bitter share rises and drags the clean peach/white-flower toward darker tones. | Some coffees' tails are clean sweet water; cutting too early can leave the cup over-strong and sweet/acid-imbalanced. Taste the tail separately, then decide. |

Philosophy: **brewing isn't extracting everything — it's knowing what to keep.**

## The recipe (illustration, as-claimed)

14g (Colombia Peñas Blancas Geisha 7g + Panama Finca Gales Geisha 7g) · 200g · **89 °C** · ~80ppm · **very coarse, uniform, low-fines** grind · **NextLevel Pulsar** ([[brewing/entities/nextlevel-pulsar]]):

1. Valve **closed** → 100g, steep to **1:20**.
2. → 100g, **open valve** to percolate/release.
3. **Discard the first ~10g** (separate cup).
4. **Cut ~140g**, before the cocoa tail.

## No-bypass + valve (why the Pulsar)

The Pulsar is a **no-bypass** dripper (flat bed, top dispersion cap, bottom valve): water is forced through the bed rather than slipping down the walls → more repeatable ratio/EY, and the valve gives **steep-then-percolate** control. Conceptually the same no-bypass family as Rao's **Filter3** and the **sprover** ([[brewing/concepts/espresso-fundamentals]], [[brewing/concepts/espresso-shot-styles]]). A **[[brewing/concepts/neo-switch|Hario Switch]]** is the accessible home substitute (valve to steep then release) — but it's a cone with bypass and no dispersion cap, so replicate the *logic*, not the seconds.

## Blend & roast (co-design)

Two Geishas chosen to **complement**: Colombia (warmer site → ripe stone-fruit sweetness, roasted to DTR ~10%) + Panama (cooler → delicate florals, DTR ~6.7%), both natural, ~30-day slow cold dry, **~6-week rest** to shed green/herbal notes; roasted separately, blended before brewing. Blend to fill what the cup lacks (like espresso blending), not to pile good beans together. DTR/rest figures are bean/roaster-specific — see [[roasting/concepts/roasting-parameters]].

## Water

~80ppm custom: **MgSO₄ : KCl : SiO₂ ≈ 3:2:1** — Mg-sulphate → **citrus**, KCl → **peach/stone-fruit** legibility, silica → **round/silky** texture. Simon co-founded water brand [[brewing/entities/apax-lab]]. Caveat: ppm alone isn't a water spec (alkalinity, Ca/Mg ratio, ion composition all matter); two "80ppm" waters can taste unalike. Don't eyeball mineral salts into a jug — use known-composition water or a measured concentrate. *(This is the wiki's second source with strong mineral→sensory content and still no dedicated water concept — an increasingly clear gap.)*

## Home practice (Hario Switch)

14g / 200g / 89–92 °C / coarse (near French-press but still draining evenly) / close-steep 100g to 1:20 → 200g → open. **Split three cups:** front ~10g · body ~130–140g · tail. Taste each alone, then pour front back / tail back and re-taste — you'll *see* whether discarding and cutting improved this coffee or just shrank the volume. Change **one variable at a time** (keep front or not; drip-dry vs early cut; grind ±1).

## Common failure modes
Coarse but not *uniform* (fines → simultaneous under-extraction + dryness) · temp too low (89 °C suits a specific ultra-light comp bean; normal light roast → 91–93 °C) · Switch pour too aggressive (channeling without a dispersion cap) · discarding the front reflexively (some fronts hold the best florals) · not checking strength after the cut (small yield → sour & over-strong; adjust with a little water) · buying water on ppm alone.
