# Flow, Resistance & Contact Time (Even-Bed Extraction)

## Definition

The pour-over lens behind Nas Jaafar's 2026 WBrC win ("**Resistance**"): **flow rate is set by the structure the water passes through, not by how much you pour.** Taste is decided at the *channel* (how evenly water crosses the bed), not the *input* (pour aesthetics). The goal is to make the bed's resistance **even — not minimal** — so extraction is uniform. Three coupled levers: **flow rate · resistance · contact time.**

## Why it matters — the "input vs channel" reframe

Most home practice trains the input: a steady stream, tidy circles, hitting the timer. But a beautiful pour over a clogged or uneven bed still yields a bitter cup, because water follows the path of least resistance. Diagnose from the **spent bed**: uneven height, a central crater, or a muddy surface all signal uneven resistance.

## The three classroom failure cases (Cozy House)

| Symptom | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Time is right, but **sour front / bitter tail / hollow middle** | **Uneven resistance** — a fines-packed zone under-extracts (sharp sour) while a low-resistance channel over-extracts (bitter); both in one cup | Make resistance **even**: WDT, low-RPM grind (fewer fines), level bed. Perfect total time can't rescue an uneven bed. |
| **Great aroma, watery/thin**, sweetness won't hold | Flow too fast, **contact time too short** — aromatics elute but body/sweetness compounds don't dissolve in time (too coarse / too-large single pour / jet punch-through / watering before bloom finishes) | Slow the later pours, pulse, +5–10s bloom; or a closed-valve steep |
| Copied a valve-close, got **muddy/astringent** | **Contact time is double-edged** — longer steep gives sweetness *and* bitterness; the champion's 1-min immersion works only atop low fines + level bed + 92 °C | **Copy the logic, not the parameters**: start a 20–30s close, calibrate by taste |

## Fix order (do the cheap things first)

**Level the bed → distribute the pour point → stop over-circling → *then* adjust grind.** Most people reach for the grinder first; doing the first three often removes half the problem. (Complements [[brewing/concepts/dialing-in]].)

## Even resistance vs. minimal resistance

The target is **uniformity**, not speed. WDT and low-RPM grinding don't just "reduce fines" — they make the fines/coarse distribution *even* so every zone extracts at a similar rate. This is the pour-over cousin of the espresso **channeling / suspended-particle** model (uneven flow drags under-extracted sour and over-extracted/astringent material into the same cup) — see [[brewing/concepts/espresso-fundamentals]] and [[shared/astringency]].

## Hybrid extraction: sequencing with a valve

Nas's recipe sequences **percolation then immersion** in one cup — front fast flow for aroma + bright acidity, back closed-valve steep for sweetness + body ("clean → sweet → structure"):

15g / 200g / 1:13 / 92 °C · UFO V3 on a **[[brewing/entities/hario-switch|Hario Switch]]** base · WDT · ~700μm, low-RPM grind. 0:00 pour 100g (open) → 0:58 close → 1:00 pour 100g (steep) → 2:00 open → 2:10 done.

Without a valve, buy contact time by slowing/pulsing the back pours and extending the bloom. This "steep-then-release" sequencing is the rising **hybrid-extraction** trend (Switch / Pulsar / Tricolate) — see [[brewing/concepts/wbrc-2026-brewing-philosophies]].
