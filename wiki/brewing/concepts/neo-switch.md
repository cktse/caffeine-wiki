# Hario Switch / Neo Switch

## Two Versions, Same Mechanism

| Model | Cone | Filter | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hario Switch** (original) | Glass V60 02 cone | 02 V60 paper | Reviewed by Christensen (2023); glass cone with switch base |
| **Hario Neo Switch** | Hario Neo dripper cone | 02 V60 paper | Newer model; updated switch mechanism; ceramic/plastic Neo cone replaces glass V60 |

The switch mechanism is identical: a steel ball in a silicone base is blocked/unblocked by a plastic lever. Recipes and technique transfer between both models.

## How It Works

Toggling the lever pokes the steel ball to seal or open the drain hole. When closed (switch down/in), the dripper behaves as an immersion vessel. When open, water drains as normal percolation. This enables **hybrid brewing**: deliberate sequencing of pour-over and immersion phases within a single brew.

## Why It Matters

More forgiving than a standard V60 — the immersion phase smooths out grind inconsistency and extends extraction time controllably. Unlike the Clever Dripper (pure immersion, trapezoid filters), the Switch's first pour can be done with the switch open to aggressively saturate grounds, then closed to steep.

## Coffee Chronicler Recipe (Asser Christensen)

The most widely adopted Switch recipe. Tuned for light/Nordic roasts; higher extraction than Tetsu Kasuya's original "devil Switch" technique.

**Core structure (scales 15–26 g):**

| Step | Time | Action |
|---|---|---|
| First pour | 0:00 | 50% total water — Switch **OPEN** |
| Second pour | 0:45 | 50% total water — Switch **CLOSED** |
| Drain | 2:00 | Open switch |
| Done | 2:45–3:15 | Total brew time |

**Ratios:**
- 1:15 → entry-level grinder (Encore, Hario Skerton)
- 1:16–1:17 → quality flat burr grinder

**"Sweet" variation:** Close at 0:25 instead of 0:45; open at 2:00. Reduces acidity, increases sweetness.

**Size scaling:**

| Dose | Water | Close | Open |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 g | 320 ml | 0:45 | 2:00 |
| 30 g | 480 ml | 1:00 | 2:30 |
| 45 g (03 Switch) | 720 ml | 1:30 | 2:45 |

**For natural Ethiopians:** Expect slightly longer total brew times. Consider coarsening grind or adjusting immersion phase.

## Tetsu Kasuya "Devil" Recipe

The original Switch recipe from its most prominent champion. Lower total extraction than Christensen's method — achieved by a mid-brew temperature drop that slows the immersion phase deliberately.

**Parameters:** 20 g / 280 ml / 1:14 / Medium-fine grind / Total time 3:00

| Time | Action | Cumulative water | Switch | Temp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Add 60 g | 60 g | Open | 90°C |
| 0:30 | Add to 120 g | 120 g | Open | 90°C |
| 0:40 | Cool kettle to 70°C | — | Open | cooling |
| 1:15 | Close switch; add to 280 g | 280 g | **Closed** | 70°C |
| 1:45 | Open switch; drawdown | — | Open | — |
| 3:00 | Done | | | |

**Why the temperature drop?** Design intent is "maximize extraction early, slow it in later stages." The 70°C immersion phase is low enough to meaningfully reduce extraction rate, preventing bitterness in the steeping phase. Have cold water ready before you start.

## Kasuya 2025 "New Hybrid" Recipe

Updated version of the devil recipe. Flavor target: "very sweet cup." The key structural innovation is starting with the switch **closed** — an immersion bloom — before switching to open percolation, then closing again at the end for a cooled immersion phase.

**Parameters:** 20 g / 300 ml / 1:15 / Medium-fine grind / Total time 3:30

| Time | Action | Cumulative water | Switch | Temp |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Add 40–50 g | 40–50 g | **Closed** | 90°C |
| 0:35 | Open switch (drain) | — | Open | — |
| 0:40 | Add to 120 g | 120 g | Open | 90°C |
| 1:30 | Add to 200 g | 200 g | Open | 90°C |
| 1:40 | Cool kettle to 70–80°C | — | Open | cooling |
| 2:05 | Close switch | — | **Closed** | — |
| 2:10 | Add to 300 g (100 g cool water) | 300 g | Closed | 70–80°C |
| 2:45 | Open switch; drawdown | — | Open | — |
| 3:30 | Done | | | |

**Why the closed bloom matters:** The immersion bloom (switch closed, 40–50g) lets sweet soluble compounds steep into standing water before any percolation begins. Draining at 0:35 before the main pours separates this initial sweet extract from later extraction. This front-loads sweetness and delays astringent/bitter extraction until the final cool-immersion phase.

## Recipe Comparison

| | Kasuya Devil | Christensen | Kasuya 2025 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dose | 20 g | 20 g | 20 g |
| Ratio | 1:14 | 1:15–1:17 | 1:15 |
| Start switch | Open | Open | **Closed** |
| Phase structure | open → close | open → close | close → open → close |
| Temp | 90°C → 70°C | Consistent (~92°C) | 90°C → 70–80°C |
| Immersion at cool temp | 30 sec | — | 35 sec |
| Total time | 3:00 | 2:45–3:15 | 3:30 |
| Flavor target | Balanced | Acidity/fruit | Sweet |

The immersion bloom is the defining variable for sweetness. Christensen's higher extraction comes from longer immersion at full temperature. Kasuya's temperature drop is the shared mechanism for taming bitterness in the final phase across both his recipes.

## Pure Immersion Mode (lazy method)

Add all water at once, switch closed. Steep 3–4 minutes. Open to drain. Results in a good but less nuanced cup — works well for medium/darker roasts.
