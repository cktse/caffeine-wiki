# Coffee Brewing — Overview

This wiki focuses on pour-over, espresso, and cold brew. All three reward understanding the coffee's origin, varietal, and processing method to select the right approach.

## Pour-Over Decision Table

Use this to pick starting parameters for a given coffee.

**Temperature by roast (Kasuya):** Light ~93°C · Medium ~88°C · Dark ~83°C. The origin-based table below uses light-roast assumptions; adjust down for medium/dark.

| Origin Region | Processing | Recommended Dripper | Ratio (g:ml) | Temp (°C) | Grind | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ethiopia / Kenya | Washed | V60 / Origami | 1:15–1:16 | 91–93 | Medium-fine to medium-coarse | Highlight acidity and florals; use 4:6 bright profile |
| Ethiopia | Natural | Graycano (cone) / Neo Switch | 1:15–1:16 | 93–95 | Medium (~25 clicks Comandante) | Tame fermented notes with Graycano heat retention; or Switch recipe |
| Guatemala | Washed | V60 / Origami | 1:15–1:16 | 91–93 | Medium to medium-fine | SHB altitude; wide range by region — Antigua/Huehuetenango tend bright + fruity; 4:6 standard |
| Central America (non-Guatemala) | Honey / Natural | Origami / V60 | 1:15 | 90–93 | Medium | Balance sweetness and clarity; 4:6 balanced |
| Colombia / Brazil | Washed | V60 / Origami | 1:15–1:16 | 90–93 | Medium-fine to coarse | Clean, versatile; 4:6 standard |
| Indonesia | Wet-hulled | Graycano (wave+Coin) / Neo Switch | 1:14–1:16 | 95–98 | Medium-coarse (~30 clicks Comandante) | Body-forward; Graycano wave mode for cleaner body; Neo Switch for controlled extraction |

## Cold Brew

Full immersion brewing with ambient (22°C) or refrigerated (4°C) water for 12–36 hours. Ambient temperature is as effective as refrigerated and 24 hours faster. See [[concepts/cold-brew]] for full detail.

**What cold brew actually changes** (vs. hot brew at same TDS): more floral, less bitter, less sour, less acidic. Sweetness is NOT significantly different in controlled conditions — the "cold brew is sweeter" claim is a marketing artifact.

| Factor | Cold Brew Recommendation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Best roast** | Light (Agtron ~58) | No negative roast interactions; cleanest result |
| **Avoid** | Dark roast | Cold brew adds "woody" character |
| **Best origin** | Ethiopia washed | Cold brew emphasizes fruity character → enhanced perceived sweetness via aromatic pathway ([[shared/sweetness-perception]]) |
| **Not ideal cold** | Sumatra wet-hulled | Hot brew better preserves characteristic smoky/earthy Sumatran profile |
| **Brew time** | 12h @ 22°C ambient | Equivalent to 36h refrigerated; same sensory result |
| **TDS target** | 2% serving strength | Concentrate will be ~4%; dilute to target |

**Key hierarchy**: origin and roast make larger differences than brew temperature. Cold brewing an average coffee does not elevate it.

## Strength, Extraction & Sensory — the Brewing Control Chart

Underlying every recipe is the strength/extraction map: **TDS** (strength) × **percent extraction** (PE) × **brew ratio**. The evidence-based [[concepts/brewing-control-chart]] (Guinard et al. 2023, UC Davis) shows *where sensory attributes are maximized* on that map (bright/fruity at high-TDS/low-PE; bitter/roasted at high-TDS/high-PE; 'sweet' at low-TDS/low-PE) and that there is **no single "ideal" cup** — consumers split into two preference clusters. Two takeaways that recur across the recipe pages:

- **Sweetness-forward target**: low-to-mid TDS + ~18–21% PE; extract fully, keep strength modest (dilute/[[concepts/bypass]]) to avoid the bitter/astringent corner.
- **Temperature is secondary**: at fixed TDS/PE it has little sensory impact (Batali et al. 2020) — grind and ratio move you on the chart; temperature mostly changes how you get there.

## Terroir Assessment Standard

Brew method choice affects how terroir is perceived. For comparing origins, SCA cupping (8.25g/150mL, 93°C, 3–5 min, medium-light roast Agtron 65) is the industry reference — it occupies a "middle position" between espresso (enhances bitterness/roasted notes) and filter (emphasizes fruity notes). The brew method modifies terroir expression but cannot change the underlying terroir character. See [[shared/terroir]] for the full assessment framework.
