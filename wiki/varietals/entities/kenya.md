# Kenya

## What / Who

East African country; one of the world's most celebrated specialty coffee origins. All commercial production is arabica, primarily washed process, grown in the central highlands.

## Relevance

Kenya is notable for SL28 and SL34 — two Scott Laboratories selections from the early 20th century that produce some of the world's most distinctive cup profiles: blackcurrant, bright complex acidity, tomato-like clarity. WCR DNA fingerprinting (2025) has since revealed SL28 is Bourbon-related and SL34 is Typica-related, despite both being selections from the same "French Mission" coffee stock — a finding that reframes how the Kenyan cup character is understood.

## Key Varieties

| Variety | Selection origin | Altitude | Disease | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SL28** | Scott Labs; from Tanganyika Drought Resistant | Medium–high | Susceptible to CBD, CLR, BBC | Bold beans; fine liquor; Bourbon-related (WCR); green shoot tips |
| **SL34** | Scott Labs; from French Mission | High altitude, high rainfall | Susceptible to CBD, CLR, BBC | Dark bronze shoot tips; **Typica-related** (WCR) — despite French Mission origin |
| **K7** | Lengetet Estate, Muhoroni; from French Mission | Low altitude | **Partial CLR resistance** (some races); partial CBD resistance | Good cup quality; Bourbon-related (WCR); suited for CLR-prevalent low-altitude zones |
| **Ruiru 11** | Coffee Research Foundation | All altitudes | Resistant to CBD and CLR | Released 1985; compact (2,500–3,300 trees/ha vs. 1,300 for SL varieties); cup quality similar to traditional varieties |
| **Batian** | Coffee Research Institute | All altitudes | Resistant to CBD and CLR | Released 2010; tall plant similar to SL28; large bold beans; backcross-bred (not F1 hybrid); high yield; good cup quality |

## Key Facts

- All three traditional varieties (SL28, SL34, K7) trace to **French Mission coffee** — a stock established at St. Austin's mission near Nairobi in 1899, sourced from La Réunion Bourbon via Spiritan missionaries; farms established prior to the 1930s are generally French Mission
- Scott Agricultural Laboratories (now NARL — National Agricultural Research Laboratories) made the SL selections; K7 was selected by a private estate (Lengetet Estate, Muhoroni)
- **WCR 2025 correction**: SL28 = Bourbon-related; SL34 = Typica-related; K7 = Bourbon-related. The common 2016 claim that all French Mission is "Bourbon derivative" is the pre-DNA understanding
- K7's partial CLR resistance is **natural within the Bourbon-Typica gene pool** — no Timor Hybrid introgression — predating and distinct from the Catimor approach to CLR resistance
- Coffee Research Foundation (CRF) substation in Kisii noted as growing Blue Mountain — institution now renamed Coffee Research Institute (CRI)
