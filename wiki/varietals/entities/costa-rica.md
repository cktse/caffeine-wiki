# Costa Rica

## What / Who

Central American country; not a volume giant but holds a strong specialty coffee reputation built on consistent Arabica quality, a long history of institutional support, and close association with honey processing innovation.

## Relevance

Costa Rica's specialty identity rests on two pillars: (1) its strict Arabica-only policy (Robusta ban, ~30 years, lifted 2018) which shaped export reputation and farmer behavior; and (2) honey processing, with which Costa Rica is strongly and distinctively associated. The country's institutional infrastructure (ICAFE, founded 1933) is more developed than most producing nations, creating a traceable, quality-oriented supply chain.

## ICAFE-Recognized Arabica Varieties

Caturra, Catuaí, Obatá, Venecia, Catigua MG2, Geisha, Typica, Marsellesa, Villalobos, Villa Sarchi, Costa Rica 95, F1 hybrids.

No strict legal planting requirement, but ICAFE alignment is needed for technical support programs. **Note**: Costa Rica 95 remains ICAFE-recognized despite confirmed CLR resistance failure (WCR 2025) — see [[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]].

Villa Sarchi — a Bourbon mutation native to Costa Rica — is the parent of the Sarchimor variety group. See [[concepts/introgressed-varieties]].

## Eight Growing Regions

| Region | Altitude | Temp | Cup Profile | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tarrazú / Los Santos** | 1,200–1,900m | 18°C | Sucrose-like sweetness, jasmine, orange, vanilla, dried fruit, chocolate | Harvest Nov–Mar; 3,400mm rain; 84% humidity; shade companion trees |
| **Valle Occidental** | 700–1,600m | 21.5°C | Orange, peach, honey, vanilla, chocolate | Caturra/Catuaí/Villa Sarchi; volcanic slopes; 2,500mm rain |
| **Tres Rios** | 1,200–1,650m | 21°C | Refined balanced acidity, long finish, fuller body | "Bordeaux of Costa Rica"; SHB; influenced by Irazú Volcano; mineral-rich soils |
| **Guanacaste** | 600–1,300m | 26°C | Softer acidity, moderate body, round, distinctive salty edge, bittersweet finish | Shade-grown for heat stress; 2,100–3,500mm rain; harvest Jul–Feb |
| **Turrialba** | 500–1,400m | 23°C | Gentle acidity, delicate aromatics, lighter profile | Near active Turrialba Volcano; volcanic + alluvial soils; one of earliest growing areas; harvest Jul–Mar |
| **Orosi** | 1,000–1,400m | 20°C | Chocolaty, elegant acidity, balanced sweetness + body | Caribbean + Pacific airflows; 7-month rainy season; century of history; larger bean size |
| **Brunca** | 600–1,700m | 22°C | Variable: smoother at lower elevations; greater sweetness + citrus complexity at higher | Includes Coto Brus, Buenos Aires, Pérez Zeledón; 3,500mm rain; humid |
| **Valle Central** | 1,000–1,900m | 22°C | Fruit, honey, chocolate; lively acidity; medium to fuller body | Includes capital San José; historically first growing area; volcanic ash soils; 2,500mm rain |

**Pattern**: Tarrazú and Tres Rios (highest altitude SHB) = the flagship specialty regions. Guanacaste and Turrialba (lower, warmer) = softer, lighter profiles. Orosi = chocolate + Caribbean moisture complexity.

## Honey Processing

Costa Rica is strongly associated with honey processing — some sources attribute its origin to Costa Rican producers. The country has been central to standardizing the color-label system (Black / Red / Yellow / White) and experimenting with non-standard variants.

See [[shared/honey-processing]] for the full concept page covering color labels, mucilage percentages, and cup characteristics.

## Grading System (Altitude-Based)

| Grade | Altitude |
|---|---|
| **SHB (Strictly Hard Bean)** | 1,200m+ |
| **GHB (Good Hard Bean)** | 1,000–1,200m |
| **MHB (Medium Hard Bean)** | below 1,000m |

Higher altitude = slower cherry maturation = denser, harder bean = greater flavor complexity. Most of Costa Rica's specialty output is SHB.

## ICAFE

Instituto del Café de Costa Rica (ICAFE), founded 1933. Central governing body for the coffee sector. Key roles:
- Sets minimum fair purchase prices that mills must pay producers
- Recognizes varieties eligible for technical support programs
- Supports research, processing, export, and domestic consumption
- Promotes sustainability and quality
