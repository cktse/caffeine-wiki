# Guatemala

## What / Who

Central American country; one of the world's prominent specialty coffee origins. All Arabica. Production peaked ~2000; declined after 2001 coffee crisis as farmers shifted to macadamia nuts and avocados. CLR is an ongoing and increasing problem. Historically traceable to farm level — many farmers run their own wet mills and process their own coffee.

## Relevance

Guatemala is notable in specialty coffee for having well-defined regional identities, primarily washed processing, and the full SHB (Strictly Hard Bean) altitude profile across all growing regions. Antigua is the most globally recognized name; Huehuetenango is often cited by specialty roasters for outstanding quality.

## Growing Conditions

- **Altitude**: 1,300–2,000m across all regions — all specialty Guatemalan coffee qualifies as SHB
- **Processing**: Primarily **washed** (wet process); many farmers have own wet mills
- **Varieties**: Dominated by Bourbon and Caturra (present in 7/8 regions); also Catuai, Typica, Maragogype, Pache
- **All Arabica**: No commercial Robusta

## Altitude Grading System

Guatemala uses altitude-based grading (common to Central America):

| Grade | Altitude |
|---|---|
| Prime | 750–900m |
| Extra Prime | 900–1,050m |
| Semi Hard Bean | 1,050–1,220m |
| Hard Bean (HB) | 1,220–1,300m |
| **Strictly Hard Bean (SHB)** | **>1,300m** |

All 8 Guatemalan specialty regions are at or above 1,300m = SHB by default. Harder, denser beans have higher extraction potential.

## Eight Growing Regions

| Region | Altitude | Harvest | Varieties | Character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **San Marcos** | 1,300–1,800m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Warmest & rainiest; Pacific-facing slopes; mechanical drying sometimes needed |
| **Acatenango** | 1,300–2,000m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Acatenango volcano; historically undervalued; excellent quality now recognized |
| **Atitlán** | 1,500–1,700m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Typica, Caturra, Catuai | Around Lake Atitlán; xocomil winds; labor cost / urbanization pressure |
| **Cobán** | 1,300–1,500m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Maragogype, Catuai, Caturra, Pache | Former German settlement (pre-WWII); wet rainforest; challenging drying; remote |
| **Nuevo Oriente** | 1,300–1,700m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Catuai, Caturra, Pache | Driest region; smallholders; coffee only since 1950s; borders Honduras |
| **Huehuetenango** | 1,500–2,000m | Jan–Apr | Bourbon, Catuai, Caturra | Highest non-volcanic mountains in Central America; most coffee-dependent; **outstanding quality** |
| **Fraijanes** | 1,400–1,800m | Dec–Feb | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai, Pache | Plateau near Guatemala City; volcanic soil; land shrinking as city expands |
| **Antigua** | 1,500–1,700m | Jan–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Most famous globally; Denomination of Origin "Genuine Antigua Coffee" (2000) |

**Harvest**: Most regions Dec–Mar; Huehuetenango Jan–Apr; Fraijanes Dec–Feb. No dual harvest (contrast with Colombia).

## Varieties

- **Bourbon**: Present in all 8 regions. The backbone of Guatemalan coffee.
- **Caturra**: Present in 7/8 regions. Dwarf Bourbon mutation; compact, high-yield.
- **Catuai**: Present in most regions. Caturra × Mundo Novo hybrid.
- **Typica**: Only documented in Atitlán.
- **Maragogype**: Only documented in Cobán. Giant-bean Typica mutation.
- **Pache**: Documented in Cobán, Nuevo Oriente, Fraijanes. **Confirmed Typica natural mutation** (WCR Varieties Catalog 2025): compact stature, large bean, good quality at high altitude, CLR susceptible. Best above 1,200m, in areas with <2,500mm rainfall/year.

## Taste Profile

Wide range by region: lighter, very sweet, fruity and complex → heavier, richer, chocolatey. No single "Guatemalan cup" — regional variation is the story. Hoffmann is explicit: "there are some flavour characteristics more common in certain regions, but there are no hard and fast rules for this."

## History

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| ~1747–1750 | Coffee introduced (Jesuits) |
| 1845 | Commission for Coffee Cultivation formed |
| 1856 | Chemical dyes displace indigo → coffee expands |
| 1868 | Government distributes ~1 million seeds |
| 1871 | Justo Rufino Barrios makes coffee backbone of economy; ~400,000 ha of indigenous land converted |
| 1880 | Coffee = ~90% of Guatemalan exports |
| 1930s | Jorge Ubico + United Fruit Company (UFC) consolidate power |
| 1953 | President Arbenz proposes land reform (targeting UFC land) |
| 1954 | CIA coup overthrows Arbenz; land reform abandoned |
| 1960–1996 | Civil war |
| ~2000 | Production peaks |
| 2000 | Antigua: "Genuine Antigua Coffee" Denomination of Origin granted |
| 2001 | Coffee crisis → farmers shift to macadamia, avocados |

## Notable: Antigua Fraud

Antigua coffee has long been counterfeited — "coyotes" would buy cherries from other regions (especially Acatenango) and process them in Antigua to claim the premium Antigua label. The 2000 Denomination of Origin prevents other-origin coffee from being *labelled* as Antigua, but has not stopped the practice of importing cherries across the border to be processed locally.
