# Brazil

## What / Who

The world's largest producer and exporter of coffee — ~⅓ of global production. Grows both *C. arabica* (**66.1%** of volume) and *C. canephora* (~34%; robusta + conilon). Specialty is a small but growing niche: of 1.872 M ha under coffee, only **~38,000 ha** produce certified specialty, coordinated largely through the **BSCA** (Brazilian Specialty Coffee Association), a board representative of the SCA.

## Relevance

Brazil is the backdrop for much of this wiki's varietal and processing history: birthplace of **Caturra** ([[concepts/purple-leaf-caturra]]), **Mundo Novo** ([[concepts/mundo-novo]]), **Catuaí** ([[concepts/catuai]]), and **pulped natural / cereja descascado** processing. Its specialty character is defined less by exotic genetics than by **scaled, mechanized, semidry-processed Arabica** — and, increasingly, by **disease-resistant introgressed cultivars that can still reach top-tier cup scores**. Actionable read for green buying: a Brazilian "specialty" lot is most often a Mundo Novo/Catuaí or an introgressed cultivar, semidry-processed, from Minas Gerais — expect sweetness/body-forward, nutty-chocolatey profiles rather than high-floral washed clarity, with the best lots (often Catuaí, Bourbon Amarelo, or Icatu-derived) reaching 87–90+ at COE.

## Key Facts

- **66.1% Arabica / ~34% Canephora** by volume (IBGE 2021–22). Robusta in **Rondônia**; conilon in **northern Espírito Santo**. (source: [[../sources/brazil-specialty-coffee-sera-2025]])
- **Specialty geography**: ~82% of specialty area in **Minas Gerais** — regions **Sul de Minas, Cerrado Mineiro, Matas de Minas (Zona da Mata)**; then **Mogiana** (São Paulo, incl. Alta Mogiana), **Serra da Mantiqueira**, **South Bahia**, **Norte Pioneiro do Paraná**, **Goiás**. Certified warehouses & brokers exist **only in Minas Gerais** — a chain bottleneck.
- **Geographical Indications**: Cerrado Mineiro, Serra da Mantiqueira de Minas, Oeste Baiano, Norte Pioneiro do Paraná, Alta Mogiana.
- **Cultivars**: ~**80% of Arabica = Mundo Novo + Catuaí**. On specialty (BSCA) farms — Mundo Novo + Acaiá on 110 farms; Bourbon group (yellow-fruit preferred) on 50; ***C. canephora*-introgressed on 87** (52% Híbrido de Timor-derived, 48% Icatu-derived). WCR's "exceptional" cultivars (Geisha, Pacamara, SL28/34, Casiopea, Mibirizi) are **not registered in Brazil**.
- **Processing**: **semidry (pulped natural / CD) dominates** specialty; wet only ~20%; dry is cheapest but hardest for specialty. (See [[shared/honey-processing]], [[processing/concepts/processing-terminology]].)
- **Economics**: one of the **cheapest specialty origins** — avg US$4.43/kg, mean cup score **83** (world avg 84.5). (source: [[../sources/brazil-specialty-coffee-sera-2025]])
- **Climate suitability**: Arabica optimal 19–22 °C / water deficit <100 mm; higher elevation → denser beans + more acidity (−0.6 °C/100 m). MG/SP >1,000 m; Paraná ~700 m (higher latitude compensates). Afforestation/irrigation extend the range.
- **Research base**: dense public-institute network — **IAC** (São Paulo), **EPAMIG/UFV/UFLA** (Minas Gerais), **IDR-Paraná/IAPAR**, **Procafé Foundation**, **Incaper** (Espírito Santo), **Embrapa** (coordinating consortium). These bred most Brazilian cultivars.
