# Panama

## What / Who

Central American producing country; small output (~1% of global production) but disproportionate influence in specialty markets through Geisha/Gesha coffee. All significant coffee production is in the western highlands (Chiriquí province), near Volcán Barú. Coffee is grown at 1,300–2,000m, all SHB grade.

## Relevance

Panama is the geographic context that transformed Geisha from an obscure Ethiopian landrace into the world's most expensive auction coffee. The combination of Volcán Barú's volcanic soils, altitude range, Pacific/Caribbean microclimate duality, and the SCAP competition infrastructure produced this outcome. Understanding Panama Geisha means understanding Panama's specific western terroir — not just the variety.

## Key Facts

- **Flagship variety**: [[concepts/geisha]] (Geisha/Gesha) — originally Ethiopian landrace; "rediscovered" at Hacienda La Esmeralda during the 2004 Best of Panama competition organized by SCAP
- **Record auction**: 2024 Best of Panama auction: $10,013 USD/kg for a Geisha micro-lot (SCAP, 2024)
- **Certifying body**: SCAP (Specialty Coffee Association of Panama) — organizes Best of Panama competition; promotes Panamanian specialty coffee internationally
- **All SHB**: All significant specialty production is at >1,300m; classified Strictly Hard Bean
- **Processing**: Washed processing dominates in specialty lots; natural Geisha is also produced but not studied in this source
- **Other varieties grown**: Typica, Caturra, Catuaí alongside Geisha

## Production Zones (Western Highlands)

| Zone | Elevation range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Boquete** | 1,450–2,000m | Most famous; Pacific slope of Volcán Barú; Hacienda La Esmeralda; fruity/floral/bitter Geisha character; highest altitude range |
| **Tierras Altas** | 1,300–1,850m | Also called Volcán or Volcán-Candela in coffee literature (larger town name); citrus/sweet/tart Geisha character |
| **Renacimiento** | 1,400–1,900m | Citrus/herbal/nutty Geisha character |
| **Potrerillos Arriba** | 1,600–1,800m | Small production; insufficient sample for regional profiling |

*Note: "Tierras Altas" is the official administrative district; "Volcán" (the main town) and "Volcán-Candela" (the specific micro-region for coffee) appear more commonly in coffee literature.*

## Terroir Characteristics

- **Volcán Barú** (3,478m) — Panama's highest peak; creates a rain shadow divide between Pacific and Caribbean slopes; Boquete is on the drier, cooler Pacific side
- **Volcanic soils** — rich in minerals; contributes to density and cup complexity
- **Diverse microclimates** — altitude + Pacific/Caribbean exposure creates multiple distinct environments within a ~40km radius
- **Peer-reviewed terroir evidence**: Ledezma et al. (2025) confirmed statistically significant sensory differences among Boquete, Tierras Altas, and Renacimiento Geisha from the 2023-2024 harvest using discriminant analysis — the first such evidence for Panamanian coffee. See [[sources/panama-geisha-terroir-ledezma-2025]].
