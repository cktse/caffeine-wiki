# Colombia

## What / Who

A country in northwestern South America; the world's 3rd-largest coffee producer (~8% of global output as of 2024–25; USDA). One of few nations capable of supplying fresh coffee nearly year-round due to its dual harvest cycle.

## Relevance

Colombia grows exclusively Arabica — no commercial Robusta production. Its high-altitude Andean growing conditions, volcanic soil, and dual harvest cycle produce one of the most recognizable and consistent specialty coffee origins in the world.

## Growing Conditions

- **Elevation**: 1,200–2,300m ASL (along the Andes slopes)
- **Temperature**: 17–24°C average
- **Rainfall**: 2,000–3,000mm annually
- **Soil**: Volcanic; mineral-rich, well-drained
- **Harvest**: Two cycles — primary Oct–Dec, secondary Apr–Jun
- **Area**: ~880,000 hectares across 22 producing regions

The dual harvest is enabled by Colombia spanning both northern and southern hemispheric zones, with different latitudes and elevations causing staggered cherry maturation.

## Major Producing Regions (22 total)

The **Coffee Triangle** (Caldas, Quindío, Risaralda) is the cultural heart — UNESCO World Heritage Site (2011).

Other key regions: Antioquia, Huila, Nariño, Cauca, Tolima, Valle del Cauca, Cundinamarca, Santander, Norte de Santander, Boyacá, Magdalena, Sierra Nevada, Putumayo, Caquetá, Meta, Casanare, Chocó, Bolívar, César, La Guajira.

Huila, Nariño, and Cauca are particularly prominent in specialty coffee for their high elevations and microclimate diversity.

## Varietals Grown

All Arabica. Major cultivars:

| Variety | Type | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Typica** | Traditional | Clean, floral, citrus; vulnerable to CLR |
| **Bourbon** | Traditional | Balanced, fruit/caramel/chocolate; round body |
| **Caturra** | Bourbon dwarf mutation | Bright citrus, caramel; compact, high-yield; CLR susceptible |
| **Variedad Colombia** | Introgressed (Caturra × Timor Hybrid) | Smooth, balanced, CLR-resistant |
| **Castillo** | Introgressed (derived from Variedad Colombia; dev. Cenicafé) | High yield, excellent disease resistance; **~87% of Colombian crops** (USDA 2025) |
| **Tabi** | Introgressed (Typica × Bourbon × Timor Hybrid) | Bright acidity, balanced; specialty-quality with disease resistance |
| **Catuaí** | Caturra × Mundo Novo | Mild, sweet, nutty/caramel; limited CLR resistance |
| **Pacamara** | Pacas × Maragogipe | Large bean; complex fruit, caramel, honey, milk chocolate |
| **Pink Bourbon** | Uncertain genetic origin | High sweetness, floral, tropical fruit/citrus; responds well to experimental fermentations; prominent in specialty scene |
| **Chiroso group** | Ethiopian Only (three varieties: Caturra Chiroso, Bourbon Chiroso, Chiroso) | Urrao, Antioquia; 1,950–2,100m; up to $45/pound; ~1 kg/tree/year; competition recognition since 2014; Eastern Ethiopian (Berbere) origin; names are phenotypic, NOT genetic — see [[concepts/chiroso]] |

**Note**: 87% rust-resistant variety adoption (primarily Castillo) is one of the most dramatic industry transitions in recent coffee history — from 35% in 2010 to 87% by 2025.

## Processing

Washed (wet) process is dominant — highlights bright acidity and clean clarity. Natural, honey, and anaerobic fermentation also practiced, especially by specialty producers.

**Historical note**: Colombian natural coffees were effectively banned from export until the FNC introduced a new, less strictly defined export grade in 2016 (Kornman 2022). Before this resolution, only washed coffee could be exported under official Colombian export grades. This explains why Colombian naturals are a relatively recent phenomenon in the specialty market despite Colombia's otherwise diverse growing conditions. Source: [[processing/sources/kornman-green-coffee-2022]].

A peer-reviewed study at Santa Helena farm, El Águila, Valle del Cauca (1,740m) demonstrated that cv. 'Colombia' under CO₂ modified atmosphere fermentation (24h) achieved 86.90 SCA — upper specialty range for a natural (dry-processed) coffee. This is notable because Colombia is typically associated with washed processing; the result confirms that controlled natural fermentation is viable with Colombian varieties. See [[processing/sources/fermentation-natural-coffee-moncayo-2025]].

## Grading — Excelso Standard

All Colombian coffee exported must meet the **Excelso** grade (FNC Resolution 2-2016):

- ≥95% of beans pass size 14 screen; all must pass size 12
- Moisture content ≤12.5%
- ≤24 defects per 500g sample
- Free of live insects; clean aroma; uniform color; no cup taints

Export to the US additionally requires ≥50% of beans retained on size 15 screen (New York Green Coffee Association).

## Certifications

- **Café de Colombia** mark — 100% Colombian origin guarantee (FNC)
- **PDO** (EU, 2005) — raw materials and production tied to Colombian geography
- **PGI** (EU, 2007) — coffee cultivated, processed, and packaged within Colombia

## Climate Challenges

Colombia grows primarily Arabica, which is less heat-tolerant than Robusta. Climate change is:
- Shifting viable growing zones upward (risking deforestation at higher elevations)
- Accelerating pest reproduction and disease pressure
- Increasing harvest uncertainty

Response: Castillo variety adoption, shade-grown farming, water-efficient washed processing (saves >10B liters/year; WEF 2025), Coffee, Forest & Climate Agreement (2021).
