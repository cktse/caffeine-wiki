# Indonesia

## What / Who

Southeast Asian archipelago; 4th largest coffee producer globally (USDA 2024/25; ~6% of global supply). Known in specialty coffee primarily for Sumatran Arabica and the unique Wet Hulling (Giling Basah) processing method that defines Indonesian cup character.

## Relevance

Indonesia's Arabica coffees occupy a distinctive flavor niche — low acidity, heavy body, earth/chocolate/spice — that is almost entirely the product of **Wet Hulling**, not varietal genetics alone. Understanding Indonesian coffee means understanding this processing method first. See [[shared/wet-hulling]].

## Production Overview

- Sumatra = 70–75% of Indonesia's total output
- Robusta = 80–90% of Sumatra's volume; grown in the south; Indonesia is a major global Robusta supplier
- Arabica = grown in North Sumatra and high-altitude areas of Java, Sulawesi, and Papua; specialty focus
- CLR context: original Arabica cultivation devastated by CLR in 1877 → Robusta introduced ~1900 for resistance → became dominant. This mirrors the global pattern but happened earlier in Indonesia than in Central America.

## Key Sumatra Regions

| Region | Altitude | Climate | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aceh (Gayo Highlands)** | 1,100–1,700m | 16–20°C; 2,000–3,000mm rainfall; cloud shade | Centered around Lake Tawar; marketed as "Gayo coffee"; EU PGI "Kopi Arabika Gayo" (2017) |
| **Lintong (Lake Toba area)** | 1,200–1,600m | 18–22°C; humidity often >80%; cloud shade | Southwest of Lake Toba; lake-proximate areas warmer/more humid; inland drier; subtle regional variation |

Other Arabica-producing islands: Java, Sulawesi, Papua — not detailed in current sources.

## Key Arabica Varieties in Sumatra

| Variety | Origin | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Typica** | Dutch colonial introduction (1699) | The original Indonesian Arabica; largely replaced due to CLR susceptibility; still present; clean/sweet profile; low yield |
| **Catimor** (local name: **Ateng**) | Timor Hybrid × Caturra | High yield, CLR resistant; compact; bright citrus acidity; the most widely grown Arabica variety in Sumatra today |
| **Timor Hybrid** (HDT / Tim Tim) | Natural arabica × Robusta cross; introduced Aceh 1979 | CLR resistant; nutty/herbal cup notes; balanced acidity + sweetness; important genetic parent for Catimors in the region |
| **S795 (Line S)** | Developed India, 1940s | Disease resistant; refined sweetness, fruit/nuts, brown sugar-like sweetness, occasional spice; round full body. WCR genetic classification unverified from existing wiki sources. |

## Processing: Wet Hulling (Giling Basah)

The defining characteristic of Sumatran (and broadly Indonesian) specialty coffee. Developed because the island's humid climate makes traditional drying unreliable. See the full concept at [[shared/wet-hulling]].

**Summary of what it produces**: lower acidity, heavier body, earth/wood/herbs/spice flavor notes. This is why Sumatra coffee is distinctly different from African or Central American origins at similar altitudes.

## Flavor Profile

- **Acidity**: Low (contrast with Ethiopian and Central American origins)
- **Body**: Full, syrupy, heavy
- **Notes**: Dark chocolate, earth, herbs, spice; woody/smoky undertones common
- **Mouthfeel**: Heavy, long lingering finish
- **Roast**: Medium to medium-dark recommended — softens sharper herbal edges, develops caramel/dark chocolate sweetness

## Mandheling

Mandheling is a **historical trade name**, not a variety, region, or GI-protected origin. Sourced from northern Sumatra (Aceh and Lintong). Name traces to WWII: a Japanese soldier asked what coffee he was drinking; the shop owner misunderstood and gave the name of his ethnic group (the Mandheling people).

Cup: nuts, dark chocolate, spice, herbal, woody, earthy, lightly smoky; smooth; long finish. Essentially the "classic" Sumatran profile.

## Grading System (Indonesian National Standard — SNI)

**Initial checks**: no live insects; moisture ≤12.5%; clean odor; foreign matter ≤0.5%.

| Grade | Defect count (300g sample) |
|---|---|
| **G1** | 0–11 defects |
| G2 | 12–25 |
| G3 | 26–44 |
| G4 | 45–80 (Arabica) |
| G5 | 81–150 |
| G6 | 151–225 |

**Commercial sorting terms**: Double Picked (DP) and Triple Picked (TP) — indicate how many times beans were hand-sorted. More sorting = fewer defects = cleaner cup. DP/TP are buyer-facing quality signals used alongside the grade.

Note: Six-grade system (G1–G6) vs. Ethiopia's five-grade system (G1–G5). Indonesia uses bean size as a grading factor; Ethiopia does not.
