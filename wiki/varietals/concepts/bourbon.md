# Bourbon

## Definition

One of the two foundational arabica lineages (alongside [[concepts/typica]]). Traces to seeds taken from Yemen to La Réunion (formerly Bourbon Island) by the French in the early 18th century. Isolated on La Réunion until the mid-19th century, then spread to Africa via French Spiritan missionaries and to the Americas via Brazil.

> **Not the parent of Typica.** DNA fingerprinting confirms Bourbon and Typica are parallel lineages from the same Yemen stock, not one derived from the other. (Source: [[sources/history-of-arabica-wcr]])

## Dispersal Route

Yemen → La Réunion (1708/1715/1718, French attempts; 2nd and 3rd partly successful) → Africa via Spiritan missionaries (1841–1899) → Brazil (1860) → Central America.

**African route detail:**
La Réunion → Zanzibar (1859) → Bagamoyo, Tanzania (1862) + St. Augustine, Kikuyu Kenya (1862) + Bura, Taita Hills Kenya (1893) → Saint Austin mission, near Nairobi (1899) = "French Mission coffee"

## Key Characteristics

- Higher yield than Typica
- Susceptible to major coffee diseases
- Round bean shape; green-tipped new growth (vs. Typica's bronze tips)
- Complex, sweet cup quality; often fruity with good body
- Many important mutations and selections have emerged from Bourbon

## Key Descendants

- **Caturra** — Bourbon dwarf mutation (Brazil); compact plant, higher yield, good quality
- **Mundo Novo** — natural Typica × Bourbon (Bourbon Vermelho) hybrid (Brazil); see [[concepts/mundo-novo]]
- **Catuaí** — Mundo Novo × Caturra hybrid (Brazil); see [[concepts/catuai]]
- **SL28** — Kenyan selection from French Mission coffee; **Bourbon related** (selection of "Tanganika Drought Resistant"; drought tolerant; exceptional cup quality)
- ⚠️ **SL34** — also a Kenyan Scott Lab selection, but **Typica related** per WCR DNA fingerprinting — NOT Bourbon-derived despite shared French Mission origin. See [[concepts/typica]].
- **Villa Sarchi** — Bourbon mutation (Costa Rica)
- **Pacas** — Bourbon mutation (El Salvador)
- **Bourbon Pointu (Laurina)** — low-caffeine Bourbon mutation (La Réunion)

## Kenya Connection

The "French Mission coffee" planted in the Kenyan highlands via St. Augustine mission seedlings (from La Réunion, 1862) is the genetic foundation from which SL28 and SL34 were selected by Scott Agricultural Laboratories in the early 20th century.

**Correction (2026-06-18)**: WCR DNA fingerprinting confirms SL28 and SL34 have different genetic backgrounds — SL28 is Bourbon related, SL34 is **Typica related**. Both are "French Mission" selections but the French Mission coffee arriving in Kenya apparently contained genetic diversity that WCR can now distinguish. The Kenya cup character (blackcurrant, bright acidity) may therefore be a combined Bourbon + Typica expression, or primarily a Typica one if SL34 is the dominant contributor. Source: [[sources/wcr-arabica-varieties-catalog-2025]].

## Open Questions
- SL34 is now confirmed Typica-related, not Bourbon. Does this change how we understand the "Kenyan cup character" — is the blackcurrant brightness a Typica trait at altitude, not a Bourbon one?
- Did the long isolation of Bourbon on La Réunion (until mid-19th century) create meaningful genetic drift from the original Yemen Bourbon stock?
- Was the "French Mission coffee" arriving in Kenya already a mixed Bourbon/Typica population, or did selection from a mixed lot produce the SL28/SL34 divergence?
