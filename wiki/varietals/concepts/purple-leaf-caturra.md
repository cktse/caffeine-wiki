# Purple Leaf Caturra

## Definition

**Caturra** is a natural dwarf (single-gene) mutation of **Bourbon**, discovered in Brazil (Minas Gerais / São Paulo region) around 1915–1918. The mutation confers compact stature (short internodes), which allows dense planting and easier harvesting, with high yield and good cup quality — at the cost of high input needs and susceptibility to coffee leaf rust ([[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]]). It is a parent of many later varieties (Catuaí = Caturra × Mundo Novo; Colombia/Castillo = Caturra × Timor Hybrid, see [[concepts/introgressed-varieties]]).

**"Purple Leaf"** refers to a **phenotype**, not a confirmed distinct cultivar: new-growth foliage (and sometimes cherries) show bronze/purple coloration from **anthocyanin** pigments. Many arabica varieties express bronze vs. green leaf tips as a heritable trait; a purple-leaf Caturra selection is a Caturra line fixed for the purple-tip expression. It appears here as grown in **Yunnan, China** ([[entities/china-yunnan]]).

## Why It Matters (in this domain)

- **Lineage placement:** Caturra sits firmly in the **Bourbon** lineage ([[concepts/bourbon]]) — relevant for predicting cup character (Bourbon-family sweetness/balance) and for rust-management expectations (susceptible, unlike its introgressed descendants).
- **Sensory expectation:** as a clean high-grown **washed** coffee, expect Bourbon-family sweetness with bright acidity; the label example (white floral / melon / green tea / spice) is consistent with a delicate, tea-leaning washed profile at altitude.
- **Brewing implication:** treat as a bright washed coffee — full extraction to develop sweetness, clarity managed by dilution. See the worked example in [[../brewing/sources/yunnan-purple-caturra-k72-label]] and [[../brewing/concepts/bypass]].
