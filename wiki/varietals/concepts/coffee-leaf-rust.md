# Coffee Leaf Rust (CLR)

## Definition

Coffee leaf rust is a disease caused by the fungus *Hemileia vastatrix*. It causes defoliation of coffee plants and can result in severe crop losses. It is one of the most important threats to global coffee production.

## Why Robusta Has Natural CLR Resistance

*Coffea canephora* (robusta) carries natural resistance to CLR — though it is not immune. This resistance is what drove the global spread of robusta cultivation in the late 1800s: arabica farms across Ceylon and Southeast Asia were devastated by CLR; robusta from the Congo was adopted as a resistant alternative (Java, 1900). This is the root of all arabica CLR resistance breeding: the Timor Hybrid arose naturally when robusta genes crossed into arabica on East Timor, and all modern introgressed resistance derives from that crossing.

## Natural Partial Resistance: K7

K7 (Kenya) is a rare case of **natural partial CLR resistance within the Bourbon-Typica gene pool** — no Timor Hybrid introgression. Selected at Lengetet Estate from French Mission coffee; resistant to some CLR races. This distinguishes it from both fully susceptible traditional varieties (SL28, SL34) and fully introgressed resistant varieties (Ruiru 11, Batian). Source: [[sources/kenya-coffee-varieties-kcta]].

## Why It Matters

CLR drove the most significant wave of arabica breeding in the 20th century — the creation of the Timor Hybrid-derived Catimor and Sarchimor varieties. Now those resistances are failing, creating a second wave of breeding urgency for the 21st century.

## Resistance Breeding History

| Period | Development |
|---|---|
| 1920s | [[concepts/timor-hybrid]] arises naturally — arabica with Robusta CLR-resistance genes |
| Mid-20th C | Breeders use Timor Hybrid to create Catimors and Sarchimors — widely deployed resistance |
| Late 20th C | Introgressed varieties provide protection for ~3 decades |
| Early 21st C | CLR races begin overcoming resistance in Lempira (Honduras), Costa Rica 95 (Costa Rica), IHCAFE 90 (Honduras) — all confirmed susceptible by WCR |

## Colombia: A Case Study in Mass Adoption

Colombia's response to CLR is the most aggressive varietal replacement program in recent coffee history:
- 2010: ~35% of Colombian crops were rust-resistant varieties
- 2025: ~87% of Colombian crops are rust-resistant (USDA Coffee Annual 2025)
- Primary driver: Cenicafé / FNC pushing adoption of Variedad Colombia and Castillo

This is almost entirely Catimor-derived resistance (Timor Hybrid → Variedad Colombia → Castillo). The same resistance that is now breaking down in Honduras and Costa Rica is what 87% of Colombian production now depends on. Source: [[sources/colombian-coffee-guide-1zpresso]], [[entities/fnc]].

## Current Status (as of WCR 2023)

- Resistance is breaking down because most varieties share one common parent (Timor Hybrid)
- Most existing rust-resistant varieties expected to lose resistance in near-to-medium term
- WCR tracking resistance breakdown globally; updates only on validated confirmation
- A variety confirmed broken in one country may still be resistant elsewhere (different rust races by geography)

## Practical Implications

- Farmers planting Catimor/Sarchimor varieties today may face yield collapse as resistance fails
- Varieties like Lempira were widely planted specifically because they were thought to be rust-resistant — breakdown affects large planted areas
- Cup quality of rust-resistant alternatives is a major concern (introgressed varieties historically lower quality)
- F1 hybrids are a newer approach that may provide new resistance not reliant on Timor Hybrid genetics
