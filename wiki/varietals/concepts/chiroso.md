# Chiroso

## What It Is

Chiroso is not one variety — it is a group of three Colombian endemic coffee varieties found in the Urrao locality of Antioquia, all of the same Ethiopian landrace origin:

| Variety | Abbreviation | Plant size | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Caturra Chiroso | CCH | Small (like Caturra) | Unique cpDNA haplotype; has exclusive SNP marker |
| Bourbon Chiroso | BCH | Tall (like Bourbon) | Unique cpDNA haplotype; same SNP as CCH |
| Chiroso | CHCH | Unspecified | Shares cpDNA haplotype with traditional Bourbon/Caturra — see Open Questions |

All three are in the Ethiopian Only genetic cluster. None are genetically related to Caturra or Bourbon despite the names.

## The Name Problem

The names are farmer vernacular based on phenotype, not genetics:
- "Chiroso" → from "chiro" (stretched shirt, elongated shape) or "achira" (Colombian oval cookie) → describes the elongated oval shape of the coffee fruits
- "Caturra Chiroso" → small plant size (visually like Caturra) + elongated fruits → **not a Caturra mutation**
- "Bourbon Chiroso" → tall plant size (visually like Bourbon) + elongated fruits → **not related to Bourbon**

This is a sourcing risk: buyers or roasters describing Caturra Chiroso as a "Caturra derivative" or Bourbon Chiroso as "Bourbon-lineage" are wrong. All three are Ethiopian landrace varieties in the Ethiopian Only cluster.

## Genetic Origin

### Ethiopian Only Cluster

All three Chiroso varieties belong to the Ethiopian Only cluster — confirmed by two independent methods:
1. **Montagnon et al. 2021** (nuclear SSR markers, 8-marker panel): placed Caturra Chiroso in the Ethiopian Only cluster
2. **Chica-Acosta et al. 2024** (chloroplast genome phylogenetics, cpDNA): independently confirms the Ethiopian affinity of all three varieties

The convergence of nuclear and chloroplast methods on the same classification is methodologically strong.

### Specific Origin: Eastern Ethiopia (Berbere Region)

This is finer-grained than the prior "Ethiopian Only" classification. The cpDNA phylogenetic analysis (Chica-Acosta 2024) places the Chiroso group closer to **Eastern Ethiopian** wild accessions from the Berbere area (Bale Mountains region, accessions BEG1, WLG1, JIG2) than to Western Ethiopian accessions.

**This distinguishes Chiroso from Geisha**:
- Geisha: southwestern/western Ethiopia (Gesha forest, Kaffa/Bench Maji area)
- Chiroso: eastern Ethiopia (Berbere/Bale Mountains region)

Both are Ethiopian Only. Both bypassed Yemen. But they are from different Ethiopian source populations — representing two separate Ethiopian lineage exports to the world.

### What Ethiopian Only Means

Ethiopian Only varieties escaped Ethiopia directly without passing through Yemen, the historical arabica domestication center. Most global arabica (Bourbon, Typica, SL28, SL34, Castillo, Catuai, etc.) traces to Yemen-filtered genetic material. Ethiopian Only varieties carry Ethiopian genetic diversity that was never bottlenecked through Yemen — which accounts for their distinctive sensory profiles. See [[concepts/arabica-history]] for the four-dispersal-route framework.

## How It Reached Colombia

Unknown. The mechanism and date of Chiroso's introduction to Urrao, Antioquia are not documented. It is grown at altitudes where standard arabica varieties struggle (1,950–2,100m) and has been cultivated locally for an unknown period before gaining competition recognition in 2014. No records exist of this variety outside Colombia prior to the Urrao population.

## Cultivation Profile

| Factor | Value | Context |
|---|---|---|
| Location | Urrao, Antioquia, Colombia (Central-Andean region) | Single known cultivation area |
| Altitude | 1,950–2,100m ASL | Extreme for arabica; at the practical upper limit |
| Temperature | ~16°C average | Very cool; consequence of extreme altitude |
| Yield | ~1 kg/tree/year | Very low; Caturra produces 3–5× more per tree |
| Market price | Up to $45/pound (~$99/kg) | Premium specialty; below Geisha's auction record but above most specialty |
| Competition | National + international recognition since 2014 | Cup competition signal; but no peer-reviewed sensory data yet ingested |

The extreme altitude (1,950–2,100m) with Ethiopian Only genetics likely drives the distinctive cup — extremely slow maturation at cool temperatures, very high bean density, and high acid potential. This is the same combination that makes Geisha (1,300–2,000m in Panama) distinctive, amplified further by altitude.

## Genetic Fingerprints

From Chica-Acosta 2024 (chloroplast genomics):

- **CCH + BCH unique haplotype**: these two varieties form a unique cpDNA haplotype among 26 varieties analyzed. The other 20 haplotypes belong to other arabica varieties in the dataset.
- **Exclusive SNP**: CCH and BCH share a Cytosine/Adenine mutation at position 47,413 bp in the intergenic spacer trnT(UGU)-trnL(UAA). This diagnostic SNP is not found in any other variety in the 26-variety dataset — a potential molecular authentication marker for CCH and BCH.
- **SSR pattern**: 445 SSRs (370 dinucleotide, 71 trinucleotide, 1 tetranucleotide) — consistent with Eastern Ethiopian accessions and traditional varieties (CH, HP1, Bourbon-Typica); differs from Western Ethiopian varieties (440–442 SSRs, different AT repeat count)
- **CHCH anomaly**: plain Chiroso shares a cpDNA haplotype with traditional Bourbon and Caturra — see Open Questions below

## Cup Profile

### Sensory character (Caturra Chiroso)

Source: Pazmiño-Arteaga & Ruíz-Márquez 2022 ([[sources/chiroso-sensory-pazmino-2022]]). Medium credibility (see source page for caveats).

Q-graders described all Caturra Chiroso samples as having a recognizable and characteristic profile: **sweet, citric acidity, juicy body, aromatic and floral notes**. This profile was present across all origins; intensity was the main quality differentiator between sites.

**Important caveat on these descriptors**: The study used a non-standard cupping protocol (12g/100mL at 90°C, vs. SCA standard 8.25g/150mL at 93°C — approximately 3.5× more concentrated). Absolute scores cannot be compared cross-study. Additionally, the post-harvest protocol is NOT standard washed; it is a complex 60-hour multi-stage fermentation (12h cherry ferment → depulp → 12h dry ferment → 36h water ferment). The cup profile described here reflects Caturra Chiroso under this specific extended fermentation — standard-washed Chiroso may differ.

### Intra-Antioquia terroir (Caturra Chiroso)

Three origins in Antioquia, same genetics, same 60h post-harvest protocol:

| Origin | Altitude | Temp | Rainfall | Soil | Sensory ranking |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| El Peñol | **2,100m** | 17°C | 188mm (lowest) | Andosol | **Highest overall** — strongest fragrance/aroma, highest aftertaste |
| Urrao | 1,830m | 16°C | 290mm (highest) | Inceptisol | Mid-high — similar to El Peñol on flavor/balance/acidity; lower aftertaste |
| Fredonia | 1,800m | 19°C | 238mm | Andosol | **Lowest** — weakest fragrance, lowest aftertaste |

Variation between origins > variation between lots from the same origin. Altitude is the likely primary driver (El Peñol at 2,100m significantly outperforms the two 1,800m sites). Note that El Peñol and Fredonia share the same soil type (andosol) but differ substantially in quality — consistent with altitude dominating over soil type. Three data points cannot statistically isolate the variable, but the pattern is suggestive.

### Aromatic volatile compounds (partial)

GC-MS identified 14 of 1000+ compounds in roasted Caturra Chiroso:
- **Dominant (roast-derived, not differentiating across origins)**: 2-furaldehyde (furfural; caramel/roasted), 2-furanmethanol (furfuryl alcohol; caramel/earthy), 2-methylbutanal (sweet almond/malty)
- **Minor differentiating (fermentation-derived)**: 2-butanone (fresh/fruity) and **acetoin** (3-hydroxy-2-butanone; buttery/milky/dairy) — these trace compounds explain most of the sensory variation between origins

Acetoin is a lactic acid fermentation product. Its variable abundance by origin, even with a standardized post-harvest protocol, suggests that local microflora (which vary by site agroecology) contribute to the final volatile profile — an intra-processing terroir mechanism.

The Fredonia lots showed the most consistent volatile profile between their two lots; El Peñol showed the greatest inter-lot volatile variability despite scoring highest overall. PCA captured 89.83% of variance in two components, with three cleanly separated origin clusters.

## Comparison to Geisha

The closest analog in the wiki is Geisha — also Ethiopian Only, also discovered/popularized in a specific non-Ethiopian country, also a premium specialty variety with a distinctive cup.

| Dimension | Geisha | Chiroso group |
|---|---|---|
| Ethiopian source region | SW Ethiopia (Gesha forest, Kaffa/Bench Maji) | Eastern Ethiopia (Berbere/Bale Mountains area) |
| Genetic cluster | Ethiopian Only | Ethiopian Only |
| Route to market | Ethiopia → Kenya → Tanzania → CATIE → Panama (1960s) | Unknown → Colombia, Urrao (date unknown) |
| Discovery moment | Best of Panama 2004 | Colombian competitions from 2014 |
| Number of varieties | One (though "Geisha" applied loosely) | Three (CCH, BCH, CHCH) — different morphologies |
| Altitude in production | 1,300–2,000m (Panama) | 1,950–2,100m (Colombia) |
| Sensory signature | Jasmine, bergamot, tea, citrus (well-documented; multi-study) | Sweet, citric acidity, juicy body, aromatic/floral — peer-reviewed but from 60h extended fermentation protocol, not standard washed |
| Price benchmark | Record $10,013/kg (2024 BoP) | Up to $45/pound (~$99/kg) |
| Yield | Low | Very low (~1 kg/tree/year) |

Key difference: Chiroso's altitude (1,950–2,100m) exceeds Geisha's typical Panamanian range (1,300–2,000m). If the altitude-quality mechanism holds, Chiroso may produce even greater acid potential and density. The Ethiopian regional origin distinction (east vs. southwest) suggests different flavor precursor genetics despite both being Ethiopian Only.

## Open Questions

- **What is the Caturra Chiroso cup profile under standard washed processing?** Pazmiño-Arteaga 2022 (now ingested) describes the profile under a 60h multi-stage extended fermentation protocol — not standard washed. A study using SCA-standard cupping on light-washed Caturra Chiroso does not yet exist in this wiki. The "sweet, citric acidity, juicy body, aromatic/floral" profile may be amplified by the extended fermentation.
- **What are the cup profiles of BCH (Bourbon Chiroso) and CHCH (Chiroso)?** The Pazmiño-Arteaga 2022 study covers Caturra Chiroso only. The two other varieties have different morphologies and (for CHCH) a different cpDNA haplotype — their sensory profiles may differ. No sourced data exists.
- **Why does CHCH (plain Chiroso) share a cpDNA haplotype with Bourbon and Caturra?** Its nuclear SSR places it firmly in the Ethiopian Only cluster. Possible explanations: (1) cpDNA haplotype resolution too coarse to separate these lineages; (2) historical maternal hybridization in CHCH's lineage; (3) cpDNA and nuclear genome carry different historical signals. The paper doesn't resolve this tension.
- **How did Chiroso reach Urrao?** No introduction record exists. Was it in a germplasm collection and planted by farmers? A direct Ethiopia introduction? Arrived via Kenya or CATIE like other Ethiopian Only escapees?
- **Do CCH, BCH, and CHCH produce meaningfully different cups?** They have different morphologies and two different cpDNA haplotypes (CCH+BCH vs. CHCH). The cup quality may differ — the three should not be assumed interchangeable for sourcing.
- **Are there other Chiroso populations outside Urrao?** The study sampled only one farm. Given the variety's competition exposure since 2014, other producers may have planted it.
- **Does Pink Bourbon have any relationship to Chiroso?** Both are in Colombia, both have uncertain/non-traditional genetics, both command specialty premiums. Pink Bourbon's genetic origin is formally uncertain (not in WCR 2025 catalog). Worth testing if any source connects them.
- **At 1,950–2,100m with ~16°C, how does extreme altitude interact with Ethiopian Only genetics?** A natural experiment: same Ethiopian genetics as Geisha but grown 500-1000m higher. What does this do to acid profile, diurnal range effects, and maturation chemistry?
