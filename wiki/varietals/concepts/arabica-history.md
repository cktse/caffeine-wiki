# Arabica — Dispersal History

A timeline of how *Coffea arabica* moved from its origin in Ethiopia to global cultivation, based on DNA fingerprinting by World Coffee Research and SSR cluster analysis by Montagnon et al. (2021).

## Origin

*C. arabica* evolved in the forests of southwestern Ethiopia — the greatest genetic diversity of the species is still found there. Seeds were first taken from Ethiopia to Yemen (likely via the Hararghe / East Ethiopia trade corridor), where coffee was first cultivated as a crop from approximately the 15th century. Yemen was the primary domestication center. The two major lineages — Bourbon and Typica — are both rooted in Yemen material, but not all cultivated arabica went through Yemen.

## Four Dispersal Routes (Montagnon et al. 2021)

Prior understanding recognized three routes. Montagnon et al. (2021) confirmed a fourth:

| Route | Path | Genetic cluster | Key varieties |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Yemen → India → Java/Americas | Yemen Typica-Bourbon | Typica, all Americas coffee, Kent, Coorg |
| 2 | Yemen → Bourbon Island (La Reunion) → Africa/Americas | Yemen Typica-Bourbon | Bourbon, SL28, French Mission varieties |
| 3 | "Escapee": Ethiopia directly to world (bypassing Yemen) | Ethiopian Only | **Geisha**, Java, Chiroso, SL-06 |
| 4 (NEW) | Yemen → East Africa directly (late 19th/early 20th C) | Yemen SL-34 / SL-17 | SL-34, SL-09, SL-17, K-7 |

**Route 3 significance**: Geisha, Java, and Chiroso all went directly from SW Ethiopia to East Africa without passing through Yemen — genetically confirmed by their placement in the Ethiopian Only cluster (no Yemen samples).

**Route 4 significance**: SL-34 traces to the Yemen SL-34 cluster via French Mission seeds brought directly from Aden (Yemen port). K-7 and SL-17 are in the SL-17 cluster, which has Ethiopian accessions but no Yemen samples — either a Yemen lineage now unsampled/extinct, or a direct Ethiopia to East Africa introduction.

## Dispersal Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| ~15th C | Coffee first cultivated in Yemen from Ethiopian seeds (likely Hararghe origin) |
| 1670 | Baba Budan smuggles seeds from Yemen to India; likely included both Bourbon and Typica groups |
| 1690 | Dutch attempt direct Yemen to Batavia (Jakarta) introduction; plants die 1699 after earthquake |

| 1727 | to Northern Brazil |

| 1735 | Santo Domingo |
| 1740 | El Salvador (early cultivation) |
| 1748 | Cuba |
| 1760-70 | Southern Brazil |
| 1779 | Costa Rica |
| 1840 | El Salvador (formal) |
| 1841 | Spiritan mission at La Reunion |
| 1859 | to Zanzibar mission |
| 1860 | **Bourbon first arrives in Americas** — southern Brazil, near Campinas |
| 1862 | to Bagamoyo mission (coastal Tanzania); St. Augustine mission (Kikuyu, Kenya) |

| Early 21st C | CLR resistance breakdown observed in Lempira (Honduras), Costa Rica 95, and others |

## Key Genetic Insights

From WCR DNA fingerprinting ([[sources/history-of-arabica-wcr]]):

1. **Typica and Bourbon are parallel lineages** — both descended from Yemen stock, not one from the other
2. **Typica separated from Bourbon in Java (1696-99)** — not directly from Yemen as was previously believed
3. **All Americas Typica traces to a single Java plant** — genetic bottleneck in 1706
4. **Indian varieties Coorg and Kent are in the Yemen Typica-Bourbon genetic cluster** — confirms their Yemen origin
5. **Brazil is 97.55% Typica/Bourbon-derived** — profound genetic bottleneck at the scale of 40% of world production

From Montagnon et al. 2021 SSR cluster analysis ([[sources/yemen-genetic-diversity-montagnon-2021]]):

6. **Yemen retains the genetic diversity it gave to the world** — the Yemen Typica-Bourbon cluster is still present in Yemen 300 years after dispersal (answering the prior open question)
7. **New-Yemen cluster**: 24 accessions unique to Yemen, not found anywhere else globally; 600+ years of stress adaptation (extreme temperature, low rainfall); potential breeding reservoir for climate resilience and cup quality
8. **Ethiopia's genetic diversity is ~2.7x greater** than all non-Ethiopian clusters (7.0 alleles/marker Ethiopian Only vs. 2.63 for all other clusters combined)
9. **Chiroso** (three Colombian varieties: CCH, BCH, CHCH; Urrao, Antioquia) is in the Ethiopian Only cluster — another Route 3 escapee. Origin refined to **Eastern Ethiopia (Berbere/Bale Mountains region)** by cpDNA phylogenetics (Chica-Acosta 2024), independent corroboration of Montagnon 2021 nuclear SSR findings. Distinct from Geisha's southwestern Ethiopian (Kaffa/Bench Maji) origin. See [[concepts/chiroso]].
10. **SL-28 is in the Yemen Typica-Bourbon cluster** — consistent with its French Mission Bourbon origin; same DNA fingerprinting as Coorg

## Yemen's Genetic Clusters (Summary)

| Cluster | Notes |
|---|---|
| Yemen Typica-Bourbon | Main historical route; Bourbon + Typica + SL28 + Kent + Moka; still present in Yemen today |
| Yemen SL-34 | Distinct Yemen subpopulation; SL-34 + SL-09 via French Mission from Aden |
| New-Yemen | 24 accessions; unique; unknown Ethiopian ancestors; potential climate-adaptation genes |

## Open Questions

- Are the New-Yemen cluster's Ethiopian ancestors still extant in Ethiopia but unsampled, or genuinely extinct?
- What is the cup quality profile of New-Yemen genetics? No sensory data exists yet.
- How many distinct genotypes were in the 1706 Amsterdam plant shipment from Java?
- Is Hararghe / East Ethiopian Rift the specific source population for Yemen's introduced coffee? Scalabrin et al. (2020) and Montagnon et al. (2021) both point this direction but have not confirmed.
- Does the SL-17 cluster represent a direct Ethiopia to East Africa introduction, or a Yemen lineage now extinct/unsampled in Yemen?
