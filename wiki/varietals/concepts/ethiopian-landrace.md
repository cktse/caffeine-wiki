# Ethiopian Landrace

## Definition

Landrace varieties are domesticated, locally adapted traditional varieties that developed through long-term adaptation to specific natural and cultural environments. In coffee, most landrace varieties originate from the forests of Ethiopia — where *C. arabica* evolved — and were domesticated through human selection over centuries.

## Why It Matters

Ethiopia holds the greatest genetic diversity of *C. arabica* globally. All other arabica — Typica, Bourbon, and their descendants — derive from a small subset of this Ethiopian diversity (filtered through Yemen, then radically bottlenecked in the Amsterdam plant and La Réunion introductions). Ethiopian landrace varieties represent genetic wealth that has not been through these bottlenecks.

## Characteristics

- Generally associated with very high cup quality
- Lower yields than improved varieties
- Extreme flavor diversity — from floral and tea-like (Yirgacheffe-type) to fruity and wine-like (Sidama, Harrar)
- Not a single variety — an enormous population of genetically diverse individuals
- Often marketed as "heirloom" or "indigenous" varietals in specialty coffee

## Naming Complexity

Ethiopian landrace varieties are often labeled by region (Yirgacheffe, Sidama, Harrar, Limu) or lot name rather than a standardized varietal name. The underlying genetics are frequently undocumented or unsequenced. This creates a challenge: "Ethiopian heirloom" is both a genuine descriptor and a marketing label that may mean different things in different contexts.

## JARC Improved Varieties — Distinct from Landrace

The Jimma Agricultural Research Center ([[entities/jarc]]) has developed formally bred improved varieties from Ethiopian genetic stock. These are **not** unselected landraces — they are intentionally selected for yield, disease resistance, and cup quality:

- **74110**: Floral clarity, fruit-forward sweetness; strong specialty competition results
- **74158**: Balanced profile; natural sweetness

Key distinction: when buying "Ethiopian heirloom" you get unselected genetic diversity; when buying JARC 74 Series you get a stable, intentionally bred variety derived from that same genetic pool. Cup quality is comparable; the JARC varieties add predictability. Source: [[sources/ethiopian-coffee-guide-1zpresso]].

## Cultivation Systems

Ethiopian coffee comes from four management levels that affect genetic diversity in the cup:

| System | Input | % of production |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | None (wild harvest) | ~10% |
| Semi-forest | Selective thinning | ~35% |
| Garden | Smallholder plots, intercropped | ~50% |
| Plantation | Managed estates, improved varieties | ~5% |

Garden coffee (50%) represents most exported Ethiopian coffee. Forest and semi-forest coffees preserve the most unselected genetic diversity.
