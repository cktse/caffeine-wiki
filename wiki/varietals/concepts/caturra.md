# Caturra

## Definition

A natural **dwarf single-gene mutation of [[concepts/bourbon|Bourbon]]**, discovered in Brazil (Minas Gerais / São Paulo) around 1915–1918. The mutation confers **compact stature** (short internodes) — enabling dense planting and easier harvesting — with high yield and good cup quality, at the cost of high input needs and susceptibility to coffee leaf rust ([[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]]). WCR genetic group: **Bourbon-related**; quality potential rated **"good."**

A workhorse of Latin-American production and the parent of several major cultivars:
- **[[concepts/catuai|Catuaí]]** = Mundo Novo × Caturra
- **Colombia / Castillo** = Caturra × Timor Hybrid (see [[concepts/introgressed-varieties]])
- **Catimor** group generally = Caturra × Timor Hybrid

> **Not to be confused with "云南紫叶卡杜拉 / Yunnan Purple-Leaf Caturra" (德热296 / Dr296)** — despite the name, that's a **Catimor** (introgressed), not a Caturra. See [[concepts/dr296]]. Colombia's **Purple Caturra**, by contrast, *is* a genuine Caturra colour variant.

## Why It Matters (in this domain)

- **Lineage:** firmly Bourbon — predicts Bourbon-family sweetness/balance, and the rust-susceptibility that motivated its introgressed descendants.
- **Agronomics:** the dwarf trait made high-density, mechanization-friendly planting possible — a major reason Caturra and its descendants dominate Latin-American cultivation.
- **Sourcing read:** "Caturra" on a label signals a clean, balanced Bourbon-family cup — a reliable specialty backbone rather than an exotic differentiator (cf. Geisha, Chiroso).
