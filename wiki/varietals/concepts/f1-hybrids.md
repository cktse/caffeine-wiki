# F1 Hybrids

## Definition

F1 hybrids in coffee are created by crossing two *genetically distinct arabica parents* and using the first-generation (F1) offspring as the commercial variety. They are not the same as introgressed varieties (which cross arabica with Robusta).

## Why They Matter

F1 hybrids combine traits from both parents — cup quality, yield, and disease resistance — and often show "hybrid vigor": significantly higher production than non-hybrid varieties. They represent a newer breeding approach that doesn't rely solely on the [[concepts/timor-hybrid]] lineage for disease resistance.

## Critical Propagation Constraint

**Seeds from F1 hybrid plants will NOT replicate the parent plant.** This is called "segregation" — the offspring generation (F2) loses the predictable trait combination of the F1. Farmers must:
- Obtain new seedlings from trusted nurseries each generation, OR
- Use clonal propagation (tissue culture or cuttings) to reproduce the F1 exactly

This is a significant practical constraint for small-scale farmers. F1 seedlings are typically more expensive than conventional varieties.

## Examples

- **Centroamericano** — Timor Hybrid × Sarchi (complex); Central America
- **Milenio** — F1 hybrid; high yield, disease resistance
- Others in WCR's variety catalog
