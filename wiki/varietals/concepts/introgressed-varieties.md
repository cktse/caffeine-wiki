# Introgressed Varieties (Catimor / Sarchimor)

## Definition

"Introgressed" arabica varieties are those that carry genetic material from another species — primarily *Coffea canephora* (Robusta). "Introgressed" means "brought over." The genetic transfer comes via the [[concepts/timor-hybrid]], a natural 1920s arabica × Robusta hybrid from East Timor.

## The Two Main Groups

| Group | Parent cross | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Catimor** | Timor Hybrid × Caturra (dwarf Bourbon) | Many distinct varieties; compact, high-yield |
| **Sarchimor** | Timor Hybrid × Villa Sarchi (Bourbon mutation) | Many distinct varieties |

**Important:** Catimor and Sarchimor are *group names for many distinct varieties with similar parentage* — not single varieties. Examples of named Catimor varieties: Lempira (Honduras), Costa Rica 95, Variedad Colombia, Castillo (Colombia).

## Colombian Introgressed Varieties (in depth)

Colombia has the most dramatic adoption story: rust-resistant varieties went from **35% to 87% of crops** between 2010 and 2025 (USDA Coffee Annual 2025), driven primarily by Cenicafé (Colombia's National Coffee Research Center) and the FNC.

| Variety | Parentage | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Variedad Colombia** | Caturra × Timor Hybrid | Colombian national name for this Catimor cross; parent of Castillo |
| **Castillo** | Derived from Variedad Colombia; refined by Cenicafé | High yield, excellent CLR resistance; now dominates Colombian cultivation. Flavor: smooth, balanced, sweet to chocolatey. **Castillo 2.0** introduced late 2024 — large-scale adoption not yet begun. |
| **Tabi** | Typica × Bourbon × Timor Hybrid | Tri-hybrid; doesn't fit cleanly into Catimor/Sarchimor groups. Combines traditional lineage quality (Typica + Bourbon) with Timor Hybrid disease resistance. Cup: bright acidity, balanced, clean. Specialty-grade viable. |

## Brazilian Introgressed Varieties (in depth)

Brazil has **two distinct introgression routes**, and — importantly — its best introgressed cultivars reach **Cup of Excellence top tables**, evidence that introgression does not preclude exceptional cup quality.

**Route 1 — Híbrido de Timor derived** (same HdT ancestry as Catimor/Sarchimor). ~52% of introgressed plantings on BSCA specialty farms. Brazilian cultivars: **Tupi IAC 1669-33, Catiguá MG1 / MG2 / MGS Catiguá 3, IAC 125 RN, Acauã, Obatã IAC 1669-20, Oeiras MG 6851, Sarchimor MG8840, IPR 98, Iapar 59, Paraíso MG H419-1, Araponga MG1, Arara**. (Iapar 59 = a Sarchimor-lineage cultivar; WCR rates it "low," but Brazilian breeders and COE results dispute a blanket low rating.)

**Route 2 — Icatu derived** (NOT via Timor Hybrid). **Icatu** was made by IAC crossing **Bourbon Vermelho × a chromosome-doubled *C. canephora***, then backcrossing to **Mundo Novo** — a separate, deliberate arabica × canephora introgression. ~48% of introgressed plantings. Cultivars: **Icatu Amarelo IAC 2944, Icatu Vermelho IAC 4045, Icatu Precoce**, and **Catucaí** (Icatu × Catuaí; e.g. Catucaí Amarelo 2SL, Catucaí 785-15).

**COE Brazil evidence (2019–2022)** — introgressed cultivars were **26.7% / 43.3% / 29.0% / 33.3%** of top lots by year, scoring **>87.16**, with **Catucaí 785-15 = 90.03 (2020)** and **Catucaí Amarelo 2SL = 90.53 (2022)** — i.e. **>90, "exceptional" territory**. Chosen primarily for **CLR resistance**, but clearly capable of top-tier quality. (source: [[sources/brazil-specialty-coffee-sera-2025]])

> **Catiguá MG2 appears in two domains:** here as a Brazilian HdT-derived COE performer, and in the processing domain as the cultivar studied in [[processing/sources/siaf-catigua-mg2-silva-2024]] (self-induced anaerobic fermentation). Same variety — a useful varietal × processing bridge.

## Other Introgressed Varieties

- **Batian** (Kenya) — complex multi-cross involving Timor Hybrid
- **Catimor / "Ateng"** (Indonesia/Sumatra) — local name "Ateng" used in Sumatra; the dominant Arabica variety grown in Sumatra today; same Timor Hybrid × Caturra parentage as other Catimors; cup presents bright citrus acidity (distinct in the Indonesian context, where Wet Hulling typically suppresses acidity)
- **S795 / Line S** (India/Indonesia) — developed India 1940s; disease resistant; cup: refined sweetness, fruit, nuts, brown sugar, occasional spice, full round body; widely grown in Sumatra; WCR genetic classification not yet verified from wiki sources
- **RAB C15** (India) — *not* Timor Hybrid based; created from Arabusta (different arabica × robusta cross) × Kent variety
- **德热 / Dr series** (Yunnan, China) — DTARI (德宏热带农业科学研究所) selections in the **Catimor CIFC 7963** family, nationally certified: **[[concepts/dr296|Dr296]]**, Dr3, Dr132, Dr48-1, Dr199-1. Dr296 is the purple-leaf one marketed as "Yunnan Purple-Leaf Caturra" — a **misnomer** (it's a Catimor, not a Bourbon-Caturra). Confirms that most of Yunnan's cultivation, including its "specialty" purple-leaf lots, sits in the introgressed Catimor family. Sources: [[sources/dr296-guchaju-2024]] + official MoA germplasm registry (corroborated).

## Trade-offs

| Trait | Introgressed varieties |
|---|---|
| Disease resistance (CLR) | Originally high — now breaking down |
| Yield | High |
| Cup quality | Historically lower than Bourbon/Typica; **improving — Brazilian Icatu/Catucaí and HdT-derived cultivars now score >90 at COE** |
| Plant vigor | Good; compact |

## The Resistance Crisis

Most introgressed varieties obtained their CLR resistance via the same shared parent (Timor Hybrid). Early 21st century: rust strains are overcoming this resistance — Lempira (Honduras) and Costa Rica 95 confirmed infected. Most experts expect existing resistance to fail in near-to-medium term globally.

See [[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]] for the disease and [[concepts/timor-hybrid]] for the genetic mechanism.
