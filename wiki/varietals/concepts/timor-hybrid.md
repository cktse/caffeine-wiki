# Timor Hybrid (Tim Tim / Híbrido de Timor)

## Definition

A natural hybrid between *Coffea arabica* and *Coffea canephora* (Robusta) that arose spontaneously on the island of East Timor in the 1920s. It is an arabica plant that carries Robusta genetic material — specifically, genes that confer resistance to coffee leaf rust (*Hemileia vastatrix*).

## Why It Matters

The Timor Hybrid is arguably the most consequential plant in 20th-century coffee breeding. It was the first arabica with genetic resistance to coffee leaf rust, and breeders used it as the common parent in creating the entire Catimor and Sarchimor variety groups — varieties that now protect coffee production across Central America, Africa, and Asia.

## Origin

Spontaneous sexual reproduction between *C. arabica* and *C. canephora* on East Timor, 1920s. Natural introgression — not laboratory created.

The Timor Hybrid was also introduced deliberately to other producing countries as a breeding and cultivation resource: it was introduced to the **Aceh region of Sumatra in 1979**, where it is still grown today (locally called HDT / Tim Tim) and contributes CLR resistance in Sumatran Catimors. Source: [[varietals/sources/sumatra-coffee-1zpresso]].

## Use in Breeding

Breeders recognized the CLR resistance value and selected multiple distinct lines of the Timor Hybrid, then crossed them:

| Cross | Result |
|---|---|
| Timor Hybrid × Caturra (dwarf Bourbon) | **Catimors** |
| Timor Hybrid × Villa Sarchi (Bourbon mutation) | **Sarchimors** |
| Complex multi-crosses involving Timor Hybrid | **Batian** (Kenya) |

The Catimor and Sarchimor names describe *groups of many distinct varieties* with similar parentage, not single varieties.

## The Resistance Problem

Because nearly all rust-resistant varieties share the Timor Hybrid as a common parent, they share the same resistance genetics. Coffee leaf rust (*H. vastatrix*) has begun overcoming this resistance in some geographies:
- Lempira (Honduras): historically resistant, now being infected
- Costa Rica 95: same issue
- Widely expected that most existing introgressed varieties will lose CLR resistance in near-to-medium term

This is a systemic agricultural risk for the coffee industry.
