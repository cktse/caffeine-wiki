# Geisha / Gesha

## Definition

An Ethiopian landrace arabica variety originating from the Gesha forest in Ethiopia's Kaffa/Bench Maji region (southwest Ethiopia). Commercially the most valuable arabica variety by auction price; known for an unmistakable aromatic fingerprint (jasmine, bergamot/Earl Grey, white peach, lemon, orange blossom, tea-like, light body) that distinguishes it from all other arabica varieties.

**Spelling note**: "Gesha" follows the Ethiopian place name; "Geisha" is the commercial spelling that became dominant after Panama's 2004 competition exposure. Both refer to the same variety.

## Why It Matters

Geisha is the clearest proof of concept for Ethiopian landrace genetics producing cup quality that commercially eclipses all processed or hybrid varieties. Its "fingerprint" — bergamot/jasmine aroma across all origins and production methods — is one of the few documented variety-level constants in coffee sensory science. It is also the primary vehicle for understanding Panama's coffee terroir, as virtually all specialty Panama research centers on Geisha washed lots.

## Variety Background

- **WCR genetic category**: Ethiopian Landrace
- **Origin**: Gesha forest, Kaffa/Bench Maji region, southwest Ethiopia — the same UNESCO Biosphere Reserve area documented for wild arabica genetic diversity. See [[entities/ethiopia]].
- **Diaspora path**: Ethiopia → Kenya (1930s disease-resistance program) → Tanzania → Costa Rica (CATIE research center, 1950s–60s) → Panama (introduced to Hacienda La Esmeralda, Boquete, 1960s as a rust-resistant candidate)
- **"Rediscovery"**: At CATIE and in Panama, Geisha was initially valued for CLR tolerance, not cup quality. Its cup potential was "rediscovered" in 2004 when Hacienda La Esmeralda entered it in the Best of Panama competition and it won by an enormous margin.
- **Typical altitude**: Best expression at 1,500–2,000m; all Panama sources are >1,300m

## Sensory Fingerprint

Geisha has an identifiable variety-level aromatic signature that persists across origins, processing methods, and roast profiles:

- **Core descriptors**: Jasmine, bergamot (Earl Grey tea character), white peach, lemon, orange blossom, lavender
- **Confirmed fingerprint**: Bergamot was present across ALL four Panama production zones studied (Ledezma et al. 2025) at a frequency too high to be a regional differentiator — it appeared in every sample regardless of zone
- **Tea-like quality**: Light, transparent body with delicate floral/citrus layering — distinct from heavier arabica profiles
- **Brightness**: Clean, bright acidity without astringency

## Panama Regional Terroir Profiles (Washed, 2023-2024)

Source: [[sources/panama-geisha-terroir-ledezma-2025]] — consumer panel (RATA), not SCA cupping scores.

| Region | Aroma | Flavor | Taste |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Boquete** (1,450–2,000m) | Fruity, floral | Dried fruit, cocoa, fermented | Bitter |
| **Tierras Altas/Volcán** (1,300–1,850m) | Floral, fruity, citrus, herbal; butter, dried fruit, fermented | Citrus, herbal | Sour, tart, sweet |
| **Renacimiento** (1,400–1,900m) | Citrus, cocoa, herbal, nutty | Citrus, herbal, cocoa, nutty | — |

Terroir differences confirmed statistically (discriminant analysis, 95% CI). Macro physicochemical parameters (roast degree, density, pH) did not differ significantly — terroir is in the volatile compounds.

## Cup Quality and Commercial Context

- **Record price**: $10,013 USD/kg at 2024 Best of Panama auction
- **Competition track record**: Consistently the top category in Best of Panama since 2004
- **Grade**: All specialty Panama Geisha is SHB (>1,300m); density >690 g/L
- **Processing**: Both washed and natural Geisha are produced; washed lots are more common in high-end auctions. All Panama research indexed here is washed.
- **Roasting**: Light to light-medium preferred — preserves the bergamot/jasmine aromatic precursors. "Roasting amplifies or suppresses terroir precursors already present in the green bean; it does not create them." (Ledezma et al. 2025 discussion)

## Closest Analog: Chiroso

The Chiroso group (three Colombian endemic varieties from Urrao, Antioquia) is the closest comparable variety to Geisha in this wiki. Key similarities and distinctions:

| Dimension | Geisha | Chiroso group |
|---|---|---|
| Genetic cluster | Ethiopian Only | Ethiopian Only |
| Ethiopian origin | SW Ethiopia — Gesha forest, **Kaffa/Bench Maji** | Eastern Ethiopia — **Berbere/Bale Mountains** region |
| Production country | Panama (primary) | Colombia — Urrao, Antioquia |
| Altitude | 1,300–2,000m | 1,950–2,100m (higher than Geisha's typical range) |
| Sensory identity | Jasmine, bergamot, tea — documented | Distinctive/high-quality — not yet peer-reviewed in wiki |
| Price benchmark | $10,013/kg auction record | Up to $45/pound (~$99/kg) commercial |

Both are Ethiopian Only Route 3 escapees, but from different Ethiopian source populations. See [[concepts/chiroso]].

## Open Questions

- What is the biochemical basis for the bergamot/jasmine fingerprint? Linalool, geraniol, and other terpenes are likely candidates — no HPLC/GC-MS data indexed for Geisha specifically.
- How does Ethiopian Geisha differ sensorially from Panamanian Geisha? The variety was grown in Panama for 40+ years before the 2004 "rediscovery" — adaptation to Panamanian conditions may have occurred.
- How does natural-processed Geisha compare to washed Geisha in the same zone? Not studied in indexed sources.
- How consistent is the Boquete/Tierras Altas/Renacimiento terroir profile across harvest years? Ledezma et al. is a single 2023-2024 harvest study.
- Is Geisha's cup quality advantage primarily genetic, or is it dependent on specific altitude/soil conditions that happen to align with its current production zones?
