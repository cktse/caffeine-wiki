# Unveiling a unique genetic diversity of cultivated Coffea arabica L. in its main domestication center: Yemen

**Source**: Montagnon, C., Mahyoub, A., Solano, W., Sheibani, F. 2021. *Genetic Resources and Crop Evolution* 68: 2411–2422. DOI: 10.1007/s10722-021-01139-y. Springer.
**Published**: 2021-02-15
**Credibility**: **High** — peer-reviewed Springer genetics journal; validated SSR method (8 markers, 7.4 alleles/marker; validated by Pruvot-Woehl et al. 2020 across 2,533 samples); discriminant analysis confirms cluster robustness (91% overall, 100% for New-Yemen); established lead author Montagnon; CATIE germplasm expertise (Solano). **Conflict of interest flag**: funded entirely by Qima Coffee (a commercial Yemen coffee company). The New-Yemen cluster is the most commercially interesting finding AND the one most exposed to sampling bias — 45 Yemen samples are from Qima's breeding population, not a comprehensive national survey. Core findings (Ethiopia as origin, Yemen as domestication center, Geisha in Ethiopian Only cluster) are consistent with prior independent research and are credible; New-Yemen cluster size/diversity findings should be treated with commercial-funder caution.

## Summary

First genetic diversity study of arabica in Yemen. 137 samples analyzed: 72 Ethiopian accessions (FAO/Orstom 1966–68 surveys), 20 worldwide cultivars, 45 Yemen Qima breeding population trees. Five genetic clusters identified using 8 SSR markers and neighbor-joining tree / PCoA / discriminant analysis. Yemen confirmed as the primary domestication center for the world's cultivated arabica. A previously unknown genetic cluster unique to Yemen — the "New-Yemen" cluster — was discovered, representing a potential climate-resilience and cup quality breeding reservoir not found in any worldwide cultivated variety.

## Key Claims

- **Five genetic clusters** fully described for the first time in a Yemen-inclusive global study
- **Yemen still holds most of the genetic diversity it delivered to the world 300 years ago** (Yemen Typica-Bourbon cluster intact)
- **New-Yemen cluster**: 24 samples, unique to Yemen, 100% correctly classified via discriminant analysis, not related to any known worldwide cultivar or Ethiopian accession — either never left Yemen or was lost en route
- **Geisha confirmed in Ethiopian Only cluster**: bypassed Yemen entirely; introduced directly from SW Ethiopia to Kenya in the early 20th century (citing Koehler 2017)
- **Chiroso** (Colombian specialty variety with exceptional cup quality) also in Ethiopian Only cluster — another "Ethiopian escapee" that bypassed Yemen
- **Four dispersal routes** identified (updates prior understanding of three routes):
  1. Yemen-India (→ Typica)
  2. Yemen-Bourbon Island (→ Bourbon)
  3. "Escapee" route: Ethiopian landraces directly to the world without Yemen (Geisha, Java, Chiroso, SL-06, Mibirizi T.02702)
  4. **NEW**: Direct Yemen-East Africa route (late 19th / early 20th C), producing SL-17, K-7, SL-34, SL-09
- SL-34 is in the "Yemen SL-34" cluster — distinct genetic subpopulation from the "Yemen Typica-Bourbon" cluster; French Mission (seeds from Yemen Aden) confirmed as origin
- SL-28 is in the Yemen Typica-Bourbon cluster (same cluster as Typica + Bourbon)
- K-7 and K-758 are in the SL-17 cluster — alongside Ethiopian accessions and no Yemen samples; may reflect a direct East Africa introduction or a Yemen lineage now absent from surveyed Qima population
- Yemen farmer names (Udaini, Jufaini, Dawairi) do not correspond to genetic clusters — names are based on visual traits (stature, leaf shape), not genetic identity
- Ethiopia genetic diversity is ~2.7× higher than all non-Ethiopian clusters combined (7.0 alleles/marker Ethiopian Only vs. 2.63 alleles/marker for all other clusters combined)
- Yemen growing conditions — extreme temperature ranges, very low rainfall — have produced 600+ years of stress-adapted genetics in the New-Yemen cluster

## Five Genetic Clusters

| Cluster | Contains | WW Cultivar members | Yemen samples | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Ethiopian Only (EO)** | 68 Ethiopian accessions + 5 WW cultivars | Geisha (T.02722), Java, Chiroso, SL-06, Mibirizi (T.02702) | 0 | Bypassed Yemen; "escapee" route |
| **SL-17** | 4 Ethiopian accessions + WW cultivars | SL-14, SL-17, K-7, K-758, Mibirizi (T.03622) | 0 | Yemen absent — either bypassed Yemen or lineage lost/unsampled in Yemen |
| **Yemen Typica-Bourbon** | WW cultivars + Yemen samples | Bourbon, Typica, SL-28/Coorg, Kent, KP-263, KP-532, Bronze 009, Moka | 13 | Main historical dispersal route to the world; Typica via India, Bourbon via La Réunion |
| **Yemen SL-34** | WW cultivars + Yemen samples | SL-09, SL-34 | 8 | SL-34 from French Mission seeds brought from Yemen (Aden); distinct from Yemen Typica-Bourbon |
| **New-Yemen** | Yemen samples only | None | 24 | Unique genetic cluster; not found anywhere else worldwide; unknown Ethiopian ancestors |

## Contradictions / Nuances vs. Existing Wiki

- **K-7 cluster placement**: Montagnon 2021 places K-7 in the SL-17 cluster (alongside Ethiopian accessions, no Yemen samples). WCR 2025 classifies K-7 as "Bourbon related." The two sources use different resolution methods and sampling. The SL-17 cluster absence from Yemen samples may reflect Qima's limited sampling rather than K-7 genuinely bypassing Yemen. **Resolution**: retain WCR "Bourbon related" classification as the primary, note SL-17 cluster placement as a methodological nuance.
- **SL-34 cluster position**: Montagnon places SL-34 in a SEPARATE Yemen cluster from Typica and Bourbon. WCR 2025 says "Typica related." These are compatible: SL-34 transited Yemen (French Mission seeds from Aden) but via a different genetic subpopulation than the Typica/Bourbon lineage. The WCR "Typica-related" designation operates at a higher taxonomic level.
- **Open question answered**: "What happened to the genetic diversity in Yemen before dispersal?" — Yemen still holds the Yemen Typica-Bourbon cluster intact, 300 years after dispersal.
