# Costa Rican Coffee Guide — 1Zpresso (2026)

## Summary

A 2026 brand marketing article covering Costa Rican coffee history, ICAFE variety policy, eight growing regions, honey processing color labels, altitude-based grading, and ICAFE as a governing institution. **Source credibility: Low-Medium** — same 1Zpresso publisher. No factual errors found. Primary value: resolves the dead Costa Rica entity link; provides the most detailed honey processing color label breakdown in this wiki; establishes Costa Rica's long Arabica-only policy; eight regional profiles with altitude and cup data.

## Key Claims

- Coffee planting in Costa Rica: 1779; commercial production: 1808; first export (to Panama): 1820
- Government promoted planting in 1821 — reportedly distributed seedlings for free
- By 1829, coffee surpassed sugar, cacao, and tobacco as top export
- Mid-19th C: British commercial ties funded the Atlantic Railway and National Theatre
- **Robusta ban**: Costa Rica maintained ~30-year restrictions on Robusta planting (to protect Arabica export reputation); lifted in 2018. As of Sep 2025, still no commercial Robusta data — remains pre-commercial
- **ICAFE-recognized varieties**: Caturra, Catuaí, Obatá, Venecia, Catigua MG2, Geisha, Typica, Marsellesa, Villalobos, Villa Sarchi, Costa Rica 95, F1 hybrids
- **Costa Rica 95 still ICAFE-recognized** despite confirmed CLR resistance failure (see [[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]]) — flag for buying decisions
- **Honey processing**: "strongly associated with Costa Rica"; source does not claim formal invention but notes it developed later than washed/natural and Costa Rica has been central to its standardization. Source: [[shared/honey-processing]]
- **Grading**: SHB (1,200m+), GHB (1,000–1,200m), MHB (<1,000m) — altitude-based
- **ICAFE**: Founded 1933; sets minimum fair purchase price; supports research

## Honey Processing Color Labels (see [[shared/honey-processing]] for full treatment)

| Color | Mucilage retained | Cup character |
|---|---|---|
| Black | ~75–100% | Deeper sweetness, richer fruit, softer acidity |
| Red | ~50% | Pronounced sweetness, fuller mouthfeel, gentler acidity |
| Yellow | ~25% | Balanced sweetness, floral-fruity, livelier acidity, cleaner |
| White | ~10% | Brighter acidity, softer sweetness, lighter fermentation, fresher |

Plus non-standard variants: Raisin Honey (double drying+fermentation cycle) and Extended Fermentation Honey (more mucilage + extended fermentation time).

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No errors found. Consistent with wiki.
- **Costa Rica 95 CLR status**: The source lists it as ICAFE-recognized for planting. Our wiki documents it as confirmed CLR-susceptible (WCR 2025). These aren't contradictory — ICAFE recognition may lag WCR confirmation, or some resistance may persist outside Honduras/Costa Rica. Worth monitoring.
- **Honey origin claim**: Source says "strongly associated" with Costa Rica but doesn't formally attribute invention. User note suggests some sources do attribute honey process origin to Costa Rica. Documented carefully in [[shared/honey-processing]] as "strong association" pending a more authoritative source.
