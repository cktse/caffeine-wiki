# Production and trade of specialty coffee in Brazil (Sera et al. 2025)

**Authors**: Gustavo Hiroshi Sera, Bruno Volsi, Valdir Mariucci Júnior, Carlos Theodoro Motta Pereira, Tiago Santos Telles
**Journal**: *Scientific Reports* (Nature portfolio), 2025-11-27 — doi:10.1038/s41598-025-26620-x; open access (CC BY-NC-ND)
**Credibility**: **High** — peer-reviewed; authors are Brazilian public-institute coffee researchers (IDR-Paraná / IAPAR lineage); primary data from BSCA (175 farms, 2023) + IBGE agricultural census + national cultivar registry (RNC). Descriptive/agronomic/economic; sensory claims are secondary and cited to other work.

## Summary

First systematic study of Brazil's specialty-coffee segment: where it is grown, which cultivars and processing methods dominate, certifications, and the supply chain — using data from all 175 BSCA-associated farms. Fills the wiki's biggest origin gap (no Brazil entity existed). Three findings matter most here: **semidry / pulped-natural ("cereja descascado / CD") processing dominates** Brazilian specialty; ***C. canephora*-introgressed Arabica cultivars are frequent on specialty farms and place at the top of Cup of Excellence Brazil** (scores >90), countering the "introgression = lower quality" assumption; and the paper **explicitly critiques WCR's quality tiers as having an unclear basis**.

## Key Claims

### Production geography (IBGE 2021–2022)
- Brazil = ~⅓ of global production; **66.1% Arabica / ~34% Canephora** by volume.
- Arabica "very high" (>100,000 Mg) zones: **Sul de Minas, Cerrado Mineiro, Matas de Minas (Zona da Mata)** in Minas Gerais; **southern Espírito Santo**; **Mogiana** (NE São Paulo). South Bahia "high." Norte Pioneiro (Paraná) "medium-high."
- Canephora: **robusta in Rondônia; conilon in northern Espírito Santo** (+ Acre, south Bahia). Grown where heat/drought tolerance matters.
- Climate suitability (per cited work): Arabica optimal annual temp **19–22 °C**, water deficit <100 mm; Canephora tolerates >21 °C and larger deficits. **−0.6 °C per +100 m** elevation; higher elevation → denser beans, more acidity. MG/SP sites >1,000 m; Paraná ~700 m (compensated by higher latitude).

### Specialty footprint
- Of 1.872 M ha of Brazilian coffee, only **~38,000 ha** produce certified specialty. **~82% of specialty area is in Minas Gerais** (best-developed supply chain: brokers, warehouses, cooperatives). São Paulo ~10% (Mogiana). Then Goiás, Paraná.
- Brazil is **one of the cheapest specialty origins**: avg **US$4.43/kg**, mean score **83** (world avg 84.5). Commodity Arabica US$2.77/kg (NY 2021/22); Canephora US$1.38/kg (London).

### Cultivars (BSCA farms)
- **80% of Brazilian Arabica = Mundo Novo + Catuaí groups.** On BSCA specialty farms: **Mundo Novo + Acaiá on 110 farms** (Acaiá is Mundo Novo-derived); **Bourbon group on 50** (yellow-fruit Bourbon preferred, 3rd most planted).
- **Introgressed (*C. canephora*) Arabica on 87 farms**: **51.72% Híbrido de Timor-derived** (Tupi, Catiguá MG1/MG2/MGS3, IAC 125 RN, Acauã, Obatã IAC 1669-20, Oeiras, Sarchimor MG8840, IPR 98, Iapar 59, Paraíso MG H419-1, Araponga MG1); **48.28% Icatu-derived** (Icatu Amarelo IAC 2944, Icatu Vermelho IAC 4045, and Catucaí = Icatu × Catuaí). Preference driven by **CLR resistance**.
- **COE Brazil 2019–2022**: introgressed cultivars were **26.7% / 43.3% / 29.0% / 33.3%** of top lots; scored **>87.16**, with **Catucaí 785-15 = 90.03 (2020)** and **Catucaí Amarelo 2SL = 90.53 (2022)**. Top COE lots overall dominated by **Catuaí group**, then Bourbon Amarelo and Geisha; **Mundo Novo absent from COE tops 2019–22**.

### WCR quality-tier critique
- WCR classifies Arabica quality potential in **five tiers: very low / low / good / very good / exceptional**. WCR "exceptional" = Casiopea, Geisha, Mibirizi, Pacamara, SL28, SL34 — **none registered for cultivation in Brazil (RNC)**.
- Only **8 Brazilian RNC cultivars appear in the WCR catalog**: Typica/Bourbon/Maragogipe = "very good"; Caturra/Catuaí/Mundo Novo/Obatã Vermelho = "good"; Iapar 59 = "low."
- Paper's critique: **"the basis used by [WCR] to define the drinking potential of these cultivars is unclear"**; Typica/Bourbon derivatives produce high-quality coffee even when not rated "exceptional." (Directly relevant to this wiki's standing open question on whether the WCR quality variable maps to specialty cup quality.)

### Processing
- Three categories: **dry** (fruit dried whole), **semidry** (= *natural pulped / pulped cherry / **cereja descascado (CD)***; skin removed, mucilage retained on parchment), **wet** (mucilage removed). Semidry is the Brazilian honey/pulped-natural family.
- **Semidry dominates** Brazilian specialty (alone or combined). Of 175 farms: 39 used one method, 52 used two, 52 used all three, 32 unclassifiable. **Wet only 19.82%.** Rationale: Brazilian harvest mixes ripe/unripe fruit; mechanical depulping separates ripe cherries → avoids astringent/vegetal immature-bean notes; fuller body than washed.
- Dry is cheapest but **hardest for specialty** (high moisture, long drying, microbial risk); selective harvest can rescue it. Paper's balanced conclusion: **literature does not establish superiority of any one method** — quality tracks postharvest conditions and environment more than the method label.

### Certifications & chain
- BSCA quality seal requires Q-grader-style cupping **≥80** (three tasters + technical officer; unannounced audit). Of 175 farms: Rainforest Alliance 74, Utz 74, Certifica Minas 34, IBD 9, Starbucks C.A.F.E. 1; **29 had only the BSCA seal**. Certifications ≠ specialty (quality is sensory-defined).
- **Geographical indications**: Cerrado Mineiro, Serra da Mantiqueira (MG), Oeste Baiano, Norte Pioneiro do Paraná, Alta Mogiana (SP).
- Certified warehouses & brokers exist **only in Minas Gerais** — a structural bottleneck concentrating the chain there.

## Contradictions / Open Questions
- **Reinforces, does not contradict:** the wiki's existing note that introgressed cup quality is "improving" is now backed by hard COE evidence (>90 scores). Partially answers the open question of whether well-selected introgressed varieties reach top-tier cup quality — in Brazil, yes.
- **Catiguá MG2 cross-link:** appears here as a Brazilian HdT-derived COE performer, and separately in the wiki's processing domain as the variety in [[processing/sources/siaf-catigua-mg2-silva-2024]] (SIAF study). Same cultivar, two domains.
- WCR quality-tier basis flagged as "unclear" by these authors — a credibility caveat to carry when citing WCR quality potential.
- Sensory descriptors per cultivar are thin here (paper is agronomic/economic); specific Brazilian cup profiles need a dedicated sensory source.
