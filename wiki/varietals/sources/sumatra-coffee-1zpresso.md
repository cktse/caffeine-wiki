# Sumatra Coffee — 1Zpresso (2026)

## Summary

A 2026 brand marketing article covering Sumatran coffee: history, key regions (Aceh/Gayo, Lintong), four Arabica varieties, Wet Hulling (Giling Basah) processing, flavor profile, Mandheling trade name, and the Indonesian grading system. **Source credibility: Low-Medium** — same 1Zpresso publisher. No factual errors found; consistent with existing wiki content. Primary value: resolves the dead Indonesia entity link; provides the first detailed description of Wet Hulling for a cross-domain shared page; documents S795 and Catimor's local name "Ateng"; adds Timor Hybrid introduction date in Aceh (1979).

## Key Claims

- Indonesia = 4th largest coffee producer globally (USDA 2024/25; ~6% of global supply)
- Sumatra = 70–75% of Indonesia's total production; Robusta 80–90% of Sumatra output; Arabica is the specialty focus
- Dutch introduced Arabica to Jakarta 1696 (failed), 1699 (succeeded); exporting to Europe by 1711
- 1877: CLR devastated Indonesian Arabica; Robusta introduced ~1900 for resistance → became dominant
- Sumatra coffee planting began around Lake Toba in 1888
- **Aceh (Gayo Highlands)**: 1,100–1,700m; Lake Tawar; 16–20°C; 2,000–3,000mm rainfall; EU PGI "Kopi Arabika Gayo" (2017)
- **Lintong (Lake Toba area)**: 1,200–1,600m; 18–22°C; humidity often >80%
- **Varieties**: Typica (legacy, largely replaced), Catimor (locally "Ateng"; high yield, citrus acidity), Timor Hybrid (HDT / Tim Tim; introduced Aceh 1979; nutty/herbal), S795 (developed India 1940s; disease resistant; sweetness, fruit/nuts, brown sugar, spice, full body)
- **Wet Hulling (Giling Basah)**: pulping → partial drying (20–40% moisture) → hulling while wet → final drying (10–12%). Result: lower acidity, heavier body, earth/wood/herbs/spice. See [[shared/wet-hulling]]
- Flavor: low acidity; full body; dark chocolate, earth, herbs, spice; syrupy mouthfeel; long finish
- **Mandheling**: historical trade name (not a variety or GI origin); sourced from Aceh and Lintong; WWII naming story; nuts, dark chocolate, spice, herbal, woody
- **Roast**: Medium to medium-dark recommended; softens herbal edges, enhances caramel/dark chocolate
- **Grading**: SNI standard; G1 (0–11 defects) to G6 (151–225); also DP (Double Picked) and TP (Triple Picked) commercial sorting terms; moisture max 12.5%
