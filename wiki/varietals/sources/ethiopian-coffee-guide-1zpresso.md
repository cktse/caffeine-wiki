# Ethiopian Coffee Guide — 1Zpresso (2025)

## Summary

A 2025 brand marketing article from 1Zpresso covering Ethiopian coffee origin, varieties, six growing regions, processing methods, grading systems, and cultivation systems. **Source credibility: Low-Medium** — same publisher as the Colombian guide; generally accurate on facts but written for general audiences, with one confirmed factual error. Stronger on regional flavor profiles and grading structure than on variety genetics. Useful for creating the Ethiopia entity page and grounding the six regions with altitude and cup data.

## Key Claims

- Ethiopia is 5th in global coffee production (USDA 2024/2025 season; ~6% of total); 3rd largest Arabica exporter after Brazil and Colombia
- Coffee contributes ~one-third of Ethiopia's total export revenue; ~15 million livelihoods
- Growing altitude: 1,500–2,400m; temperatures 18–24°C
- **74 Series (JARC varieties)** — new to wiki: developed by Jimma Agricultural Research Center (JARC) for yield, disease resistance, and cup quality; 74110 and 74158 most notable; floral clarity, fruit-forward sweetness; high scores in international competitions
- Geisha: originally from Gesha forest in SW Ethiopia; introduced to Panama (described as "for CLR resistance"; see note below); jasmine florals, honeyed sweetness
- Six growing regions: Sidama, Yirgacheffe, Jimma, Guji, Harrar, Limu (see [[entities/ethiopia]] for detail)
- Grading: G1 (<5 defects) to G5 (47–86 defects); CLU (Coffee Liquoring Unit) system for specialty; Q1 ≥85 pts / Q2 80–84.99 (CQI Q system)
- ECX (Ethiopian Commodity Exchange, est. 2008): standardized grading, improved market access; BUT pooled beans from multiple farms, destroying traceability; 2017 reform allowed direct farm-to-exporter purchases
- Four cultivation systems: Forest (10%), Semi-forest (35%), Garden (50%), Plantation (5%)
- Climate change: suitable arabica zones shifting to higher elevations; wild arabica could decline ~50% by 2088; robusta areas expected to expand

## Contradictions / Errors

- **Bourbon lineage**: Source states "Bourbon — Historically a natural mutation of Typica." This is incorrect and contradicts WCR DNA evidence. Bourbon and Typica are parallel lineages from Yemen; neither is derived from the other. Same error as in colombian-coffee-guide-1zpresso. Do not propagate.
- **Geisha to Panama**: Source says Geisha was introduced to Panama "for its resistance to coffee leaf rust." Oversimplified — Geisha was distributed via CATIE in Costa Rica in the 1960s; its cup quality (not primarily CLR resistance) is what made it globally famous after Hacienda La Esmeralda's wins from 2004. WCR classifies its CLR resistance as only "Intermediate." The CLR story is real but partial.
