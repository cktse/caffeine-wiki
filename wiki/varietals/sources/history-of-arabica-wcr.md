# History of Arabica (World Coffee Research)

## Summary

Authoritative genetic history of *Coffea arabica* from its Ethiopian origin through global dispersal. Based on DNA fingerprinting and genetic studies. Covers the Typica and Bourbon dispersal routes, the four main genetic categories of arabica (Ethiopian Landrace, Bourbon/Typica Group, Introgressed, F1 Hybrid), the creation of the Timor Hybrid and its role in rust resistance, and the current breakdown of rust resistance in introgressed varieties.

## Key Claims

- *C. arabica* native to Ethiopia; greatest genetic diversity found there
- Seeds first moved from Ethiopia to Yemen; from Yemen, both Bourbon and Typica lineages originated
- DNA evidence: Typica and Bourbon are parallel lineages from Yemen stock — Typica separated when Dutch moved seeds from India (not Yemen) to Java in 1696–99. They are NOT parent/mutation of each other.
- A SINGLE coffee plant (taken from Java to Amsterdam in 1706) is the genetic ancestor of all Typica that colonized the Americas
- Bourbon stayed isolated on La Réunion until mid-19th century; spread to Africa via French missionaries (Spiritans)
- Bourbon arrived in Kenya via La Réunion seeds planted at St. Augustine mission (Kikuyu) — these seeded the Kenyan highlands
- In Brazil (40% of world production), 97.55% of cultivars derive from Typica and Bourbon — significant genetic bottleneck
- Timor Hybrid (1920s, East Timor): spontaneous *C. arabica* × *C. canephora* cross; introduced Robusta genetics giving leaf rust resistance → used to create Catimors (× Caturra) and Sarchimors (× Villa Sarchi)
- Rust resistance in Catimor/Sarchimor varieties is breaking down — most share one parent (Timor Hybrid); expected to fail in near-to-medium term
- F1 hybrids: cross of two genetically distinct Arabica parents; higher yield; seeds will not replicate parent traits (segregation) — must propagate clonally

## Correction to Existing Wiki

`wiki/varietals/overview.md` said "Bourbon — mutation of Typica." **This is incorrect.** DNA evidence confirms Bourbon and Typica are parallel lineages from the same Yemen stock, not one derived from the other. Corrected in overview.

## Four Arabica Categories

| Category | Characteristics | Disease susceptibility |
|---|---|---|
| Ethiopian Landrace | High cup quality, lower yield, wild/semi-wild origin | — |
| Bourbon & Typica Group | Standard/high cup quality | Susceptible to major diseases |
| Introgressed (Catimor/Sarchimor) | Rust-resistant (historically); now breaking down | Resistance failing |
| F1 Hybrid | High yield; must be clonally propagated | Varies |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- Correction: Bourbon is NOT a mutation of Typica (corrected in wiki)
- The "French Mission coffee" in Kenya derives from Bura seedlings (Taita Hills) → Saint Austin mission (near Nairobi, 1899). This is a specific named coffee to note.
- Baba Budan's 1670 smuggling of seeds from Yemen to India appears to have introduced both Bourbon and Typica groups — predating the Dutch introductions. The Dutch introduction to Java in 1696–99 is where the Typica branch *separated*, not where it originated.
- CLR resistance breakdown: most existing rust-resistant varieties will likely lose resistance in near-to-medium term. Significant implications for origin availability.
