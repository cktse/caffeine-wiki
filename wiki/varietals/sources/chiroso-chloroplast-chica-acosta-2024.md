# Chloroplast Genome Assembly of Caturra Chiroso, Bourbon Chiroso, and Chiroso

**Source**: Chica-Acosta, M., Ibarra-Arcila, H.E., Martínez, J.G. 2024. *Journal of Plant Biochemistry and Biotechnology* 33: 710–715. Springer.
**DOI**: 10.1007/s13562-024-00934-9 *(note: the raw file frontmatter incorrectly shows 10.38141/10779/0337, which is the DOI for reference [^1] Castillo 2005 — this is a metadata error in the clipping)*
**Published**: 2024-11-08
**Funding**: Institución Universitaria Colegio Mayor de Antioquia (Grant FCSA44); Open Access via Colombia Consortium. No commercial conflict of interest.
**Credibility**: **Medium-High** — peer-reviewed Springer journal; sound cpDNA methodology (nextRAD sequencing + NOVOPlasty assembly + multiple phylogenetic methods); no COI; independently corroborates Montagnon 2021 using a completely different marker type (cpDNA vs. nuclear SSR). Limitations: very small sample (6 plants, 2 per variety, from 1 farm); chloroplast = maternal lineage only (no nuclear genome coverage); no cup quality data; not PMC-indexed; Colegio Mayor de Antioquia is a regional Colombian institution.

## Summary

First genomic characterization of all three Colombian Chiroso varieties — Caturra Chiroso (CCH), Bourbon Chiroso (BCH), and Chiroso (CHCH) — using complete chloroplast genome sequencing. The phylogenetic analysis confirms all three are more closely related to Eastern Ethiopian-origin varieties (Berbere region) than to Western Ethiopian varieties, and provides independent corroboration of Montagnon 2021's nuclear SSR-based Ethiopian Only classification. Key additional finding: CCH and BCH share a unique chloroplast haplotype with an exclusive diagnostic SNP — a potential molecular authentication marker.

## Key Claims

- All three Chiroso varieties belong to the Ethiopian Only genetic cluster — NOT mutations or descendants of Caturra or Bourbon, despite the names. Names reflect phenotype only (fruit shape and plant size)
- Their names derive from local farmer vernacular: "chiroso" describes elongated oval fruit resembling "achira" (a Colombian cookie) or "chiro" (a stretched shirt); "Caturra" = small size; "Bourbon" = tall size — genetic relationships are absent
- Caturra Chiroso was already in Montagnon 2021 as an unnamed ingroup "Chiroso" — this study extends the analysis to all three varieties with cpDNA, using a different marker system
- Origin within Ethiopia: more closely related to **Eastern Ethiopian** varieties (Berbere/Bale Mountains area accessions: BEG1, WLG1, JIG2) than to Western Ethiopian varieties (GISF1, LAG3, ANF1, etc.) — this is more specific than Montagnon 2021's "Ethiopian Only"
- CCH + BCH form a **unique cpDNA haplotype** among 26 varieties analyzed (20 haplotypes total)
- CCH and BCH share an **exclusive SNP** at position 47,413 bp (C/A mutation in intergenic spacer trnT(UGU)-trnL(UAA)) — potential molecular authentication marker
- CHCH (plain Chiroso) shares a cpDNA haplotype with traditional Bourbon and Caturra — see Contradictions section
- Cp genome: 155,188 bp; 131 genes (86 protein-coding, 8 rRNA, 37 tRNA); 4 subregions — standard for *C. arabica*
- 445 SSRs in cpDNA (370 dinucleotide, 71 trinucleotide, 1 tetranucleotide) — consistent with Eastern Ethiopian varieties; differs from Western Ethiopian varieties (440–442 SSRs; different AT dinucleotide count)
- Cultivation: Urrao locality, Antioquia; 1,950–2,100m ASL; ~16°C; ~1 kg/tree/year; up to $45/pound market price; recognized in competitions since 2014

## Contradictions / Nuances vs. Existing Wiki

**CHCH chloroplast haplotype tension**: Montagnon 2021 (nuclear SSR) places all Chiroso varieties in the Ethiopian Only cluster. This paper shows that CHCH (plain Chiroso) shares a chloroplast haplotype with traditional Bourbon and Caturra — despite its Ethiopian Only nuclear assignment. Three possible explanations:
1. cpDNA resolution at the haplotype level is insufficient to distinguish the Eastern Ethiopian Cp lineage from the Bourbon/Caturra Cp lineage (convergence at the haplotype level)
2. Historical cpDNA introgression in CHCH's maternal lineage
3. CHCH's nuclear and chloroplast genomes reflect different ancestry (possible if there was hybridization at some point in its history)

The paper notes this without fully resolving it. The nuclear SSR classification (Ethiopian Only) is more informative for overall genetic identity; the cpDNA haplotype is a maternal-lineage-specific observation. **Wiki resolution**: retain Ethiopian Only for all three Chiroso varieties as the primary genetic classification; flag the CHCH cpDNA ambiguity as an open question.

**Eastern vs. Western Ethiopian origin**: The paper's phylogenetic analysis distinguishes Eastern Ethiopian (Berbere region) from Western Ethiopian accessions. This refines the "Ethiopian Only" classification from Montagnon 2021. Geisha originates from southwestern Ethiopia (Gesha forest, Kaffa/Bench Maji) — a different part of Ethiopia. Both are in the Ethiopian Only cluster but from geographically distinct source populations.

**Caturra Chiroso ≠ Caturra**: The paper explicitly states "Caturra Chiroso is not a mutation of Caturra but rather an Ethiopian landrace." Any source or retailer describing Caturra Chiroso as a Caturra derivative is wrong.
