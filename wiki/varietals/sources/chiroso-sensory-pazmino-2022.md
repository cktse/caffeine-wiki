# Sensory Evaluation and Aromatic Volatile Compounds of Caturra Chiroso Coffee from Three Geographic Origins in Antioquia

**Source (original title)**: Pazmiño-Arteaga, J.D. & Ruíz-Márquez, A.F. "Evaluación sensorial y de compuestos volátiles aromáticos del café (Coffea arábica var. Caturra Chiroso) de tres orígenes geográficos de Antioquia." *Ciencia y Tecnología Agropecuaria* 24, no. 1. 2022. Agrosavia. DOI: 10.21930/rcta.vol24_num1_art:2846
**Language**: Spanish (translated for this ingest)
**Funding**: SENA (Servicio Nacional de Aprendizaje) and Centro de Recursos Naturales Renovables La Salada. No conflict of interest declared.
**Credibility**: **Medium** — peer-reviewed in Agrosavia's Ciencia y Tecnología Agropecuaria (legitimate regional Colombian agricultural science journal); no COI; 3 certified Q-graders; standard GC-MS analytical method; Pazmiño-Arteaga cited in Chica-Acosta 2024 as a credible Chiroso researcher. Limitations: not PMC/Scopus indexed; small sample (6 lots from 3 municipalities); headspace GC-MS identifies only 14 of 1000+ coffee volatiles; cupping protocol deviates from SCA standard (12g/100mL at 90°C vs. SCA 8.25g/150mL at 93°C — ~3.5× higher concentration); no absolute SCA scores reported (radar chart only). Internal comparisons are valid; cross-study score comparison is not.

## Summary (translated from Spanish)

First peer-reviewed sensory and volatile compounds study of Caturra Chiroso coffee, comparing three Antioquia geographic origins under an identical post-harvest protocol. The paper establishes that: (1) Caturra Chiroso has a recognizable and distinctive cup profile; (2) geographic origin produces statistically distinct sensory and volatile profiles even with identical genetics and post-harvest procedure — confirming agroecological terroir; (3) altitude is the strongest differentiating factor (El Peñol at 2,100m scored highest). This is the first peer-reviewed source for the Chiroso cup profile and the first documented intra-Antioquia terroir study for this variety.

## Key Claims (translated)

- Caturra Chiroso has a characteristic and recognizable cup profile: **sweet, citric acidity, juicy body, aromatic and floral notes**; Q-graders confirmed this profile was present across all samples, with intensity as the main quality differentiator between origins
- Three geographic origins produced three statistically distinct sensory groups despite identical genetics (Caturra Chiroso) and identical post-harvest protocol — **terroir confirmed within a single variety and post-harvest protocol**
- Quality ranking: El Peñol (2,100m) > Urrao (1,830m) > Fredonia (1,800m)
- Fredonia scored lowest for fragrance/aroma and aftertaste; Urrao and El Peñol scored similarly on flavor, balance, and acidity (p > 0.05); differed on aftertaste and fragrance/aroma (p = 0.038)
- Variation between geographic origins was greater than variation between lots from the same origin — post-harvest protocol reproducibility was validated
- 14 volatile compounds identified; dominant (bulk): 2-furaldehyde, 2-furanmethanol, 2-methylbutanal (roasted coffee, caramel, sweet almond notes); differentiating (trace, fermentation-derived): 2-butanone and acetoin (fresh/fruity/buttery/dairy notes)
- PCA of volatiles: 89.83% variance captured by first two components; three clean origin clusters
- "Quality management of coffee is site-specific" — agroecological variables (wind, rainfall patterns, soil, solar hours, microbial dynamics) drive sensory differences even when genetics and post-harvest are controlled
- Fredonia (1,800m): most consistent volatile profile between lots; El Peñol (2,100m): highest quality but most inter-lot volatile variability

## The Three Origins (translated)

| Origin | Subregion | Altitude | Temp (harvest) | Rainfall (harvest months) | Soil type |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| El Peñol (P) | Eastern Antioquia | **2,100m** | 17°C | 188mm (lowest) | Andosol (sedimentary + volcanic) |
| Urrao (U) | Southwestern Antioquia | 1,830m | 16°C | 290mm (highest) | Inceptisol (sedimentary + marine volcanic) |
| Fredonia (F) | Southwestern Antioquia | 1,800m | 19°C | 238mm | Andosol (central slopes, volcanic) |

**Note**: El Peñol and Fredonia share andosol soil type but differ in altitude (2,100m vs 1,800m) and produce different quality outcomes. El Peñol and Urrao differ in altitude and soil type but produce similar sensory results. The pattern is more consistent with altitude > soil type as the primary quality driver — consistent with [[shared/terroir]] (Williams 2022 altitude quantification). However, three data points cannot statistically isolate the variables.

## Post-Harvest Protocol (standardized across all origins; translated)

The protocol is NOT standard washed. It is a complex multi-stage fermentation totaling ~60 hours:
1. Cherry selection: optimal maturity ("reddish-purple, smooth, firm"; >99% healthy)
2. Float sorting: defective cherries discarded
3. **Cherry fermentation (12h)**: whole cherries sealed in plastic container filled >90% to limit air exposure (anaerobic tendency)
4. Depulping (dry): cherries pulped without water
5. **Dry fermentation (12h)**: depulped beans sealed in hygienic container (>90% full)
6. **Water fermentation (36h)**: depulped beans submerged in clean water in sealed container
7. Washing: per FNC recommendations
8. Sun drying: on raised drying beds until <12% moisture

**Important implication**: The cup profile described here reflects Caturra Chiroso under a 60-hour extended multi-stage fermentation process — NOT under light or standard washed fermentation. Cup profile comparisons with standard-washed Chiroso would require a separate study.

## Volatile Compounds Identified (14 total)

| Compound | CAS | Sensory character | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2-Furaldehyde (furfural) | 98-01-1 | Roasted coffee, caramel, burned | Dominant; roast-derived |
| 2-Furanmethanol (furfuryl alcohol) | 98-00-0 | Roasted coffee, caramel, earthy | Dominant; roast-derived |
| 2-Methylbutanal | 96-17-3 | Sweet almond, fruity, malty | Dominant; roast/Maillard-derived |
| 2-Butanone (methyl ethyl ketone) | 78-93-3 | Fresh, fruity | Minor; **differentiating**; fermentation-derived |
| 3-Hydroxy-2-butanone (acetoin) | 513-86-0 | Buttery, milky, dairy, fruity | Minor; **differentiating**; fermentation-derived |
| 9 other compounds | — | Varied | Named in figure, not in text |

Most abundant compounds contribute to the generic "roasted coffee" character across all origins. The minor differentiating compounds (2-butanone, acetoin) are fermentation products whose varying abundance by origin explains the sensory differences between El Peñol, Urrao, and Fredonia.

## Methodological Notes

- **Cupping deviation from SCA standard**: 12g/100mL at 90°C. SCA standard is 8.25g/150mL at 93°C. The Pazmiño-Arteaga protocol is approximately 3.5× more concentrated. The 4-minute steep time is similar to SCA. Temperature is 3°C lower than SCA standard. This means absolute scores cannot be compared with studies using the SCA protocol; internal comparisons between the three origins remain valid.
- **GC-MS coverage**: Headspace GC-MS (HTA HT2800T autosampler, Shimadzu GC-2010, quadrupole detector, Carbowax 20M column). Identified 14 compounds from 1000+ present in roasted coffee. SPME-GC-MS or SBSE methods would capture far more compounds. Results are partial but internally valid.
- Harvest period: September–December 2020

## Contradictions / Nuances vs. Existing Wiki

- **Cup profile complements but doesn't resolve the Geisha comparison**: The sensory profile (sweet, citric acidity, juicy body, floral/aromatic) has some overlap with Geisha's profile (jasmine, bergamot, citrus, tea-like). However, these terms come from different grader vocabularies and different cupping protocols — direct comparison is not possible without a standardized head-to-head study.
- **Soil evidence nuance for terroir**: El Peñol (highest quality) and Fredonia (lowest quality) share andosol soil type; El Peñol and Urrao (similar quality) differ in soil type. This is consistent with altitude being a stronger differentiator than soil type for quality within Chiroso, which aligns with Williams 2022. But three data points cannot isolate this statistically — cite as suggestive, not conclusive.
- **The 60h fermentation protocol**: The paper designed this protocol to "standardize" post-harvest across farms. But using a non-standard protocol (60h multi-stage fermentation) means the cup profile is specific to this processing approach. Standard washed Chiroso may taste different. This is not a contradiction in the paper, but affects how we should characterize the Chiroso cup profile.
