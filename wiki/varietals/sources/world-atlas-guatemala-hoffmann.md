# World Atlas of Coffee — Guatemala (James Hoffmann, 2018)

## Summary

Authoritative chapter on Guatemala from James Hoffmann's World Atlas of Coffee (2nd edition, 2018). Covers political history of coffee in Guatemala from ~1750 to 2001 crisis, altitude-based grading system, eight distinct growing regions with altitudes/varieties/harvest windows, taste profile overview, and traceability notes. Hoffmann is one of the most respected voices in specialty coffee; this is a primary reference.

**Source credibility**: Very high. Hoffmann's World Atlas is a standard specialty coffee reference. Data is 2013–2018 vintage; production figures may be outdated but regional characteristics are stable.

## Key Claims

- Coffee introduced ~1750 (Jesuits); accounts from 1747; commercially important only after 1856 (chemical dye invention killed indigo market)
- By 1880: coffee = ~90% of Guatemala's exports
- Production peaked ~2000; declined after 2001 coffee crisis (farmers shifted to macadamia nuts, avocados)
- CLR is an ongoing and increasing problem for Guatemalan producers
- Guatemala primarily grows **washed** coffee — many farmers have their own wet mills; traceability to farm level is standard
- Taste profile spans wide range: light, sweet, fruity, complex → heavier, richer, chocolatey depending on region
- 8 defined growing regions with markedly different altitudes, climates, and microclimatic signatures
- Antigua is the most famous region globally; holds Denomination of Origin "Genuine Antigua Coffee" (2000) due to fraud
- Huehuetenango has the highest non-volcanic mountains in Central America; most coffee-dependent region; produces "astonishing coffees"
- All specialty Guatemalan coffee qualifies as SHB (Strictly Hard Bean) — grown above 1,300m

## Altitude Grading System

| Grade | Altitude |
|---|---|
| Prime | 750–900m |
| Extra Prime | 900–1,050m |
| Semi Hard Bean | 1,050–1,220m |
| Hard Bean (HB) | 1,220–1,300m |
| **Strictly Hard Bean (SHB)** | **>1,300m** |

All 8 Guatemalan growing regions start at ≥1,300m → all specialty Guatemalan coffee is SHB by default. Harder beans = denser = higher extraction potential.

## Eight Growing Regions

| Region | Altitude | Harvest | Key Varieties | Notable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **San Marcos** | 1,300–1,800m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Warmest & rainiest; Pacific-facing; mechanical drying common |
| **Acatenango** | 1,300–2,000m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Near Acatenango volcano; historically undervalued (cherries sold to Antigua) |
| **Atitlán** | 1,500–1,700m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Typica, Caturra, Catuai | Around Lake Atitlán; xocomil winds; labor cost pressure |
| **Cobán** | 1,300–1,500m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Maragogype, Catuai, Caturra, Pache | German-era history; wet rainforest; challenging drying; remote |
| **Nuevo Oriente** | 1,300–1,700m | Dec–Mar | Bourbon, Catuai, Caturra, Pache | Driest region; smallholders; coffee only since 1950s |
| **Huehuetenango** | 1,500–2,000m | Jan–Apr | Bourbon, Catuai, Caturra | Highest non-volcanic mountains in Central America; most coffee-dependent; outstanding quality |
| **Fraijanes** | 1,400–1,800m | Dec–Feb | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai, Pache | Surrounds Guatemala City; volcanic soil; shrinking land base |
| **Antigua** | 1,500–1,700m | Jan–Mar | Bourbon, Caturra, Catuai | Most famous globally; Denomination of Origin 2000; fraud ongoing |

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- No contradictions with existing wiki.
- Brewing overview previously lumped Guatemala under "Central America | Honey / Natural" — incorrect; Guatemala is primarily **washed**. Updated brewing overview to add Guatemala-specific row.
- Pache variety: appears in Cobán, Nuevo Oriente, Fraijanes. Not documented anywhere in this wiki yet. Compact Guatemalan/Mesoamerican variety; insufficient source detail for a standalone concept page.
- Note: Hoffmann's variety lists per region show Bourbon and Caturra in 7 of 8 regions. Only Atitlán lists Typica; only Cobán lists Maragogype. Pache appears in 3 regions. This makes Bourbon + Caturra essentially the backbone of all Guatemalan coffee.
- Production figure (3,143,000 bags in 2013) is outdated; for trend data prefer USDA/ICO current sources.
