# The Ultimate Guide to Colombian Coffee (1Zpresso, 2025)

## Summary

Marketing article by grinder brand 1Zpresso covering Colombian coffee comprehensively: origin, FNC history, varietal landscape, growing conditions, processing methods, export grading (Excelso standard), certifications, climate challenges, and sustainability initiatives. Statistics are sourced from USDA Coffee Annual 2025 and FNC documents. Content is generally reliable but carries the marketing editorial tone typical of brand blogs, and contains one factual error on varietal genetics (flagged below).

**Source credibility**: Medium. Statistics are traceable to USDA/FNC. Varietal descriptions are broadly accurate with one exception. Use for Colombian-specific context; defer to WCR for genetics claims.

## Key Claims

- Colombia is the world's 3rd-largest coffee producer (~8% of global output, Oct 2024–Jun 2025; USDA)
- Exclusively Arabica — Robusta remains in research stages only; no large-scale production
- 22 major producing regions; ~880,000 hectares of cultivation
- **87% of Colombia's crops are now rust-resistant varieties** (up from 35% in 2010; USDA Coffee Annual 2025)
- ~40% of total production meets specialty-grade standards
- Elevation: 1,200–2,300m ASL; volcanic soil; dual harvest cycle (Oct–Dec primary, Apr–Jun secondary)
- Dominant processing: washed; natural, anaerobic, and honey also used
- Export standard: Excelso grade (≥95% beans pass size 14 screen; moisture ≤12.5%; ≤24 defects per 500g)
- PDO certification (EU, 2005); PGI certification (EU, 2007)
- Coffee, Forest & Climate Agreement launched 2021 — climate-smart agriculture initiative
- Water-efficient washed processing saves >10B liters/year (World Economic Forum, 2025)
- Climate change is forcing farms to migrate to higher elevations, risking deforestation

## Contradictions / Errors

⚠️ **Factual error**: The source states "Bourbon — A natural mutation of Typica." This is incorrect. DNA fingerprinting (WCR, 2023) confirms Bourbon and Typica are **parallel lineages** from Yemen stock — not parent-child. See [[concepts/bourbon]] and [[concepts/typica]]. Do not propagate this claim.

No other contradictions with existing wiki. The 87% rust-resistant adoption stat is new and significant for [[concepts/coffee-leaf-rust]] and [[concepts/introgressed-varieties]].
