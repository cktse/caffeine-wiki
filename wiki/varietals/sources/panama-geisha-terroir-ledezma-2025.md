# Sensory Perception and Physicochemical Characteristics of Geisha Coffee From Different Production Zones in Panama

**Source**: Ledezma, D. B., Sartori, C., and Tomasino, E. 2025. *Food Science & Nutrition* 13, no. 12: e71278. DOI: 10.1002/fsn3.71278
**PMC**: PMC12645158
**Published**: 2025-11-25
**Credibility**: **High-Medium** — peer-reviewed (Wiley), PMC-indexed; rigorous statistics (CA, MFA, AHC, DA); same OSU Tomasino lab as Batali 2022 (cold brew). Limitations: consumer panel (RATA, n=24), not Q-graders; macro physicochemical analysis only (no HPLC/GC-MS volatiles); single 2023-2024 harvest year.

## Summary

First empirical terroir study for Panamanian Geisha, comparing green coffee from four western Panama production zones (Boquete, Potrerillos Arriba, Renacimiento, Tierras Altas/Volcán) from the 2023-2024 harvest. All samples washed, all SHB (1,300–2,000m). Macro physicochemical parameters (roast degree, density, pH) did not differ significantly across zones. Discriminant analysis of consumer sensory data confirmed statistically significant terroir differences in aroma, flavor, and taste among Boquete, Tierras Altas, and Renacimiento — the first peer-reviewed evidence that Panama coffee terroir exists.

## Key Claims

- **Sensory terroir confirmed**: Boquete, Tierras Altas, and Renacimiento produce statistically distinct sensory profiles (p<0.05, 95% CI discriminant analysis)
- **Physicochemical parameters converge**: Roast degree (Agtron 67.6–68.9), density, pH, and bean color did not differ significantly across zones — terroir is encoded in volatile compounds not captured by these macro measurements
- **Bergamot is a Geisha fingerprint**: Bergamot aroma/flavor appeared in <15% of responses per sample (too low for discriminant analysis) but across ALL samples — indicating it is a variety-level constant, not a regional differentiator
- **Boquete is fruity/floral/bitter**: Most associated with fruit aroma + floral aroma; dried fruit + cocoa + fermented flavor; bitter taste; highest °Brix (1.45) and titratable acidity (6.20 mL NaOH/50mL)
- **Tierras Altas (Volcán) is citrus/sweet/tart**: Floral, fruity, citrus, herbal aroma; butter, dried fruit, fermented aroma also noted; citrus + herbal flavor; sour + tart + sweet taste
- **Renacimiento is citrus/herbal/nutty**: Citrus, cocoa, herbal, nutty in both aroma and flavor
- Roasting does not create terroir — it amplifies or suppresses flavor precursors already present in green beans; light-medium roast (Agtron ~68) was chosen to preserve terroir expression over roast-derived compounds
- Different brewing methods alter terroir expression; this study used French press (1:16 ratio, 4 min immersion, 99°C)

## Contradictions / Open Questions

- This is a single-harvest study; terroir conclusions require multi-year confirmation
- Consumer panel (RATA) findings are not directly comparable to SCA cupping protocol scores — no absolute quality ranking possible
- Boquete "fruity/bitter" and Tierras Altas "sweet/tart" differences may partly reflect farm-level variation within zones rather than zone-level terroir (Boquete n=11, Tierras Altas n=4)
- No volatile compound analysis — the specific aromatic compounds driving terroir expression remain unknown
- The "fermented" descriptor appearing in Boquete and Tierras Altas clusters is unexpected for washed coffees; may reflect residue from washed processing variation at farm level, or consumer vocabulary conflating "fruity complexity" with "fermented"
- Does terroir expression change under different roasting profiles? This study used one light-medium profile for all.
- Does terroir express differently through pour-over vs. French press? Not tested.
